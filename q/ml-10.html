Compare L1 Lasso regularization and L2 Ridge regularization: Why does L1 produce sparse weights?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Fundamentals · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

L1 (Lasso) adds the sum of absolute weights to the loss, L2 (Ridge) the sum of squares. L1 applies a constant-size pull toward zero and sets many weights exactly to zero, giving sparse models and feature selection; L2 shrinks all weights smoothly and rarely makes any exactly zero.

Explained in depth

L1:  loss + lambda * sum(|w_j|)        L2:  loss + lambda * sum(w_j^2)

Why L1 gives sparsity

  • Gradient view: the L1 penalty has a gradient of constant magnitude, lambda * sign(w), that keeps pushing a small weight toward zero at the same speed, so it can land on exactly zero. The L2 gradient, 2 * lambda * w, shrinks as w shrinks, so weights approach zero but seldom reach it.
  • Geometry view: the L1 constraint region is a diamond with corners on the axes. The loss contours tend to touch it at a corner, where some coordinates are zero. The L2 region is a smooth sphere with no corners.
  • Optimisation view: solvers apply soft-thresholding for L1, which zeroes weights whose gradient is smaller than lambda.

Practical differences

L1 / LassoL2 / Ridge
Sparse solutionYesNo
Correlated featuresTends to pick one arbitrarilySpreads weight among them
Closed-form solutionNoYes (linear regression)
Good forFeature selection, interpretabilityStability, many small effects

Elastic Net mixes both and is helpful when features are correlated and you still want sparsity.

Tips

Standardise features first, because the penalty depends on scale. Do not penalise the intercept. Choose lambda by cross-validation. From a Bayesian view, L2 is a Gaussian prior on weights and L1 a Laplace prior. In neural networks, L2 is often applied as weight decay.
