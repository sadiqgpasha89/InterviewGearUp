How do you coach and mentor first-time engineering managers stepping into leadership?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Management · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Coach first-time managers by setting expectations about the shift from individual contributor to leader, meeting regularly, and building core skills: 1-on-1s, delegation, feedback, prioritisation and handling conflict. Use real situations for coaching, ask questions to develop their judgement, share frameworks and resources, give safe space to make small mistakes, and connect them with peers.

Explained in depth

Set the foundation

  • Agree on expectations for the role and what success looks like in the first 3, 6 and 12 months. Discuss the identity shift: your output is now the team's output.
  • Hold regular 1-on-1s (weekly or biweekly) with their agenda, and keep them protected.
  • Provide context: team goals, organisational priorities, politics and decision rights.

Core skills to coach

  1. Running 1-on-1s: listening, career conversations, asking open questions.
  2. Delegation: giving ownership and context instead of doing the work; resisting the urge to rescue.
  3. Feedback: specific, timely, kind and direct, including difficult conversations and performance concerns.
  4. Prioritisation and time management: saying no, protecting maker time for the team, reducing meetings.
  5. Hiring, onboarding and team building.
  6. Managing up and across: communicating risks, negotiating priorities.
  7. Handling conflict and emotions in the team.

How to coach

  • Use real, current situations: "What are you seeing, what options do you have, what would you try?" Ask questions to develop judgement before offering answers.
  • Role-play hard conversations beforehand and debrief afterwards.
  • Share frameworks and recommended reading (for example management basics, radical candor, the manager's path) and take a management course or cohort if available.
  • Give room to make small mistakes and talk about your own early mistakes openly.
  • Collect feedback from their team, peers and stakeholders (skip-level meetings) and share patterns.
  • Encourage a peer network of other new managers.

Watch for warning signs

Still doing most of the technical work, avoiding conflict, long hours, a team that seems directionless, or drops in team morale. Adjust support early, and remember that returning to a technical track is an acceptable outcome if the role is not a fit.
