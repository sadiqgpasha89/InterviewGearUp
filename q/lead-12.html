How do you recognize early signs of burnout in an engineering team and intervene effectively?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 3 (Senior) · People · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Look for changes from a person's normal: more cynicism and withdrawal, dropping quality, missed deadlines, irritability, long hours or fatigue. Talk privately and ask open questions, then change the causes (workload, unclear priorities, on-call load, lack of control or recognition) rather than offering only wellness perks, and check back.

Explained in depth

Early signs (compare with the person's own baseline)

  • Withdrawal: quiet in meetings, camera off, skipping social time.
  • Cynicism or flat enthusiasm about work they used to enjoy.
  • More mistakes, slower delivery, missed commitments.
  • Irritability, or more conflict than usual.
  • Late-night and weekend activity, little time off, or frequent sick days.

Team-level signals: rising on-call pages, repeated overtime, sustained crunch, high attrition, and a falling score in pulse surveys.

Common causes

Burnout research groups the causes into workload, lack of control, insufficient reward or recognition, loss of community, unfairness and a mismatch of values. In engineering, the usual culprits are constant urgency, noisy on-call, unclear priorities and no visible progress.

How to intervene

  1. Talk privately and with care: 'I have noticed you seem stretched lately. How are things?' Listen without fixing or diagnosing.
  2. Remove causes: cut or defer scope, reprioritise, rebalance work, rotate on-call, protect focus time, clarify priorities.
  3. Encourage time off and make sure it is really possible, with someone covering.
  4. Point to support such as an employee assistance programme where available, and respect privacy.
  5. Follow up in later 1-on-1s and check that things actually changed.

Prevent it at team level

Sustainable pace, honest planning, track overtime and pages, limit work in progress, celebrate completed work and model healthy behaviour yourself. Wellness perks do not help if the workload stays the same.

Likely follow-ups

  • How would you respond if an engineer says they are burned out but a critical launch is next week?
  • How do you tell burnout from a temporary rough patch?
  • What team-level metrics would warn you early?
