Walk through how you design and manage a fair Performance Improvement Plan (PIP).

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 3 (Senior) · People · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

A fair PIP gives an engineer a real chance to meet clear expectations: use it only after clear, documented feedback, work with HR, set specific measurable goals with a timeline and genuine support, hold regular check-ins in writing, and be honest that failure can lead to an exit. It should never be a surprise or a formality.

Explained in depth

Before a PIP

  • The person has already had specific, documented feedback and time to improve. A PIP that is the first they hear of a problem is unfair.
  • Involve HR (and legal where required) early, and check company policy and local employment rules.
  • Check yourself: were expectations clear and appropriate for the level? Is there a role fit, workload or personal factor you could help with?

Designing the plan

  1. Specific, observable expectations tied to the role and level, such as 'deliver the pagination change with tests and review feedback addressed within two cycles', not 'be more proactive'.
  2. Measures and evidence: how each goal will be judged, by whom, and from what artifacts.
  3. Timeline: commonly 30 to 90 days, with checkpoints.
  4. Support: coaching, a mentor, clarified priorities, training and reduced distractions, so the plan is a real chance.
  5. Consequences: stated plainly, including that unsuccessful completion may end employment.

Running it

  • Deliver it in person, explain the reasons, and let the person respond and add their view.
  • Meet weekly, with short written notes shared afterwards, and give honest progress feedback each time.
  • Do not move the goalposts; if circumstances change, update the plan in writing.
  • Keep it confidential and treat the person with respect throughout.

Outcomes

If the person succeeds, close the plan, keep supporting them and recognise the improvement. If not, handle the exit with dignity and clear communication, per policy.

Pitfalls

Vague goals, no support, using a PIP as paperwork for a decision already made, inconsistent treatment across employees (a fairness and legal risk), and ignoring signs of health or personal difficulties, which should go through HR.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you decide between coaching informally and starting a PIP?
  • What would you do if the engineer improves in the first two weeks and then slips?
  • How do you keep the rest of the team motivated while a colleague is on a PIP?
