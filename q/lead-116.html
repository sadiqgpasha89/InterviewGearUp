Tell me about a time you explained a technical idea to someone who was not technical.

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 1 (Junior) · Communication · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Pick a real moment, such as a product manager, customer or support colleague, and show how you found out what they needed to decide, used a simple analogy or example instead of jargon, checked understanding, and followed up in writing. Emphasise audience awareness, patience, and an outcome that depended on them understanding.

Explained in depth

What they are testing

Whether you can translate technical work into the other person's terms: their goals, risks and decisions. Strong engineers are valued for being understood, not for sounding clever.

A simple structure

  1. Context: who the person was (a product manager, a client, a support lead) and what decision or action depended on the explanation.
  2. Audience first: you asked what they already knew and what they needed (a go or no-go, a date, a risk level).
  3. Method: lead with the conclusion, use an analogy from their world, one diagram or example, and avoid acronyms; offer detail only if asked.
  4. Check: ask them to say it back in their own words, or ask "what would you tell your team?"
  5. Follow up: a short written summary and a way to ask further questions.
  6. Result: a decision made faster, fewer misunderstandings, a trust gain.

Example

"A product manager wanted a feature in a week, and I thought it needed a database change that was risky. I explained that changing the database is like renovating a kitchen while people still cook in it: doable, but we must stage it. I gave two options, a smaller version in a week or the full version in three, with the risk of each. She picked the smaller version, and we agreed a date for the rest."

Tips

  • Avoid condescension; assume intelligence, not knowledge.
  • Show that you listen as much as you explain.
  • Mention what you learned, for example to prepare an analogy in advance.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you check that the other person really understood?
  • What do you do when someone keeps asking for more technical detail than they need?
  • How would you explain an API to a child?
