What are the main types of malware (virus, worm, trojan, ransomware, spyware), and how do they differ?

Security engineer · Gear 1 (Junior) · Fundamentals · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

A virus attaches to files and spreads when they are run, a worm spreads by itself across networks, a trojan pretends to be legitimate software, ransomware encrypts data for payment, and spyware secretly collects information. Rootkits hide malicious activity, botnets turn infected machines into remote-controlled armies. Defences combine patching, endpoint protection, least privilege, backups and user caution.

Explained in depth

Types

TypeBehaviourExample
VirusAttaches to a host file or program; spreads when the host is executed or sharedMacro viruses in documents
WormSelf-replicating; spreads across networks exploiting vulnerabilities without user actionWannaCry (also ransomware), Conficker
TrojanLooks like useful software but carries a malicious payload; does not self-replicateFake installers, cracked software
RansomwareEncrypts files (and often steals them), demands payment; "double extortion" threatens to publish dataLockBit, Ryuk
Spyware and infostealersCollects keystrokes, credentials, browser cookies and tokensKeyloggers, Redline
AdwareUnwanted ads, may track behaviourBundled toolbars
RootkitHides its presence, often with kernel or boot-level accessBootkits
Botnet agentGives remote control of the device for DDoS, spam or miningMirai on IoT devices
Backdoor and RATProvide persistent remote accessRemote access trojans
Fileless malwareRuns in memory using legitimate tools such as PowerShell, leaving little on diskLiving-off-the-land attacks
CryptominerUses resources to mine cryptocurrencyCryptojacking scripts

Typical infection paths

Phishing attachments and links, malicious downloads, drive-by downloads from compromised sites, unpatched vulnerabilities, exposed remote desktop, infected USB drives, and supply chain compromises.

Defences

  • Patching operating systems and software promptly.
  • Endpoint detection and response (EDR), application allowlisting, and disabling macros from the internet.
  • Least privilege (no daily admin accounts), network segmentation, and egress filtering.
  • Email and web filtering and user training.
  • Immutable, offline backups and tested restores, which are the best answer to ransomware.
  • Monitoring and an incident plan: isolate infected systems from the network, preserve evidence, identify scope, eradicate, recover from clean sources, and rotate credentials.
