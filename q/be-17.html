What is database replication lag, and how do you ensure read-your-own-writes consistency?

Backend engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Databases · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Replication lag is the delay before a write on the primary becomes visible on a replica. Reads from a lagging replica can miss a user's own recent write. Ensure read-your-own-writes by routing recent writers to the primary, tracking LSN or GTID positions, using sticky sessions, or reading from replicas only once caught up.

Explained in depth

Why lag happens

Asynchronous replication ships the write-ahead log (WAL) or binlog after commit. Heavy write bursts, long-running queries on replicas that block replay, slow disks or network, and single-threaded apply can all increase lag from milliseconds to minutes.

The anomaly

A user posts a comment (written to primary), the page reloads and reads from a replica that has not applied it, so the comment appears missing. Other anomalies: monotonic reads (seeing newer then older data across different replicas) and consistent prefix violations.

Strategies for read-your-own-writes

  1. Read from the primary for a short window after a write (for example 5 to 10 seconds, stored in a cookie or session), or for data the user just modified.
  2. Route by criticality: profile, checkout and account pages read from the primary; feeds and analytics from replicas.
  3. Position tracking: after a write, capture the commit position (PostgreSQL LSN with pg_current_wal_lsn(), MySQL GTID) and store it in the session. For the next read, pick a replica whose replayed position is at or beyond it (pg_last_wal_replay_lsn()), wait briefly (WAIT_FOR_EXECUTED_GTID_SET in MySQL), or fall back to the primary.
  4. Sticky routing of a user to one replica plus a per-session version check gives monotonic reads.
  5. Synchronous or semi-synchronous replication (synchronous_commit = remote_apply) makes a commit wait for replicas, at the cost of write latency and availability.
  6. Client-side merge: keep the new data in the UI (optimistic update) until the replica catches up.
-- PostgreSQL: lag in bytes and time on a replica
SELECT now() - pg_last_xact_replay_timestamp() AS lag;
SELECT pg_wal_lsn_diff(pg_current_wal_lsn(), replay_lsn) FROM pg_stat_replication;

Operating it

  • Alert on lag thresholds, and remove replicas from the read pool automatically when lag exceeds a limit (proxy health checks in ProxySQL, PgBouncer, RDS Proxy).
  • Cap long queries on replicas (max_standby_streaming_delay), tune apply parallelism (MySQL replica_parallel_workers), and reduce write amplification (big transactions, bulk updates in batches).
  • Cloud systems with shared storage (Aurora) reduce lag but do not remove it entirely.

Pitfalls

Assuming replicas are always fresh, reading and then immediately writing based on replica data (lost updates), using lag in seconds only (a quiet primary shows a false large lag), and failing over to a lagging replica which loses committed transactions.
