How do you plan quality assurance for a major re-platforming or migration project without regressions?

QA / SDET · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Strategy · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Define what must stay the same, capture current behaviour with characterisation and golden-master tests, build a parity test suite that runs against old and new systems, migrate in phases with traffic shadowing and canaries, reconcile data with automated comparisons, test performance and rollback, and set clear go and no-go criteria. Involve users early and keep the old system as a fallback.

Explained in depth

1. Scope and risk

Map the functionality, integrations, data, and non-functional properties (performance, security, compliance) that the old system provides, including undocumented behaviour and batch jobs and reports that people rely on. Rank by business risk. Agree explicit acceptance criteria.

2. Capture current behaviour

  • Characterisation (golden master) tests: record outputs of the existing system for a wide range of inputs and use them as the reference.
  • Use production traffic samples (anonymised) and logs to discover real usage patterns and edge cases.
  • Document and clarify business rules with domain experts; add tests for known defects you intend to preserve or fix.

3. Build parity tests

  • A suite that runs the same scenarios against old and new systems and compares results: API responses, calculated values, files and reports, UI flows.
  • Shadow traffic or dual running: mirror real requests to the new system, log differences (diff testing), and investigate mismatches before users see the new system.
  • Automate comparison with tolerances (rounding, timestamps, ordering).

4. Data migration validation

Row counts, checksums and aggregates per table and period, sampling of records, referential integrity, rule-based reconciliations (balances match), and trial migrations (dress rehearsals) with timing and rollback measured. Keep data synchronised during parallel running.

5. Non-functional

Performance and load tests at least at production peak, resilience and failover tests, security testing, permission and role mapping checks, monitoring and alert verification, and accessibility.

6. Phased rollout

Migrate by customer segment, region or feature using feature flags and canaries; start internal, then small percentages; monitor error rates, latency, business KPIs and support tickets. Define rollback triggers and rehearse the rollback.

7. Governance

Go and no-go checklists (defect thresholds, parity coverage, performance, data reconciliation signed off), UAT with business users, communication plan, and hypercare period with extra support. Keep the old system read-only until confidence is established, then decommission through a controlled process.

Likely follow-ups

  • What are characterisation tests and why are they useful in migrations?
  • How do you validate data migration correctness at scale?
  • How do you decide when it is safe to turn off the old system?
