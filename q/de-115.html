How would you build a data governance programme that people actually follow?

Data engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Governance · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Start with business outcomes and a few critical data domains, assign clear owners and stewards, classify data and set practical policies for quality, access, retention and privacy, then enforce them through tooling in the platform such as catalog, lineage, automated checks and access controls. Keep process light, measure adoption and incidents, and align incentives so governance helps teams ship faster.

Explained in depth

Why programmes fail

Governance imposed as paperwork and committees, with no tools, no owners and no benefit to the people asked to comply.

Approach

  1. Anchor to outcomes: regulatory needs (GDPR, SOX, HIPAA), trusted metrics for the board, faster analytics, safe AI use. Pick 3 to 5 critical domains (customer, product, finance) and expand.
  2. Roles and accountability:
  • Data owner (a business leader accountable for a domain's data).
  • Data steward (day-to-day definitions and quality).
  • Data custodian or platform (technical operation).
  • A governance council to resolve conflicts, kept small and decision-focused.
  1. Policies (short and practical): data classification (public, internal, confidential, restricted) and handling rules; access request and approval; quality standards and SLAs; retention and deletion; privacy and consent; naming and definitions (a business glossary).
  2. Enforce through tooling (policy as code):
  • Catalog (DataHub, Collibra, Unity Catalog) with owners, descriptions, tags and certified datasets.
  • Lineage to see impact and trace sensitive data.
  • Automated classification of PII, with masking and row or column policies applied by tags.
  • Access control via groups and attribute-based rules, with time-limited access and audit logs.
  • Quality checks and data contracts in pipelines, with incident workflows.
  1. Make the right thing easy: self-service request flows, templates, and certified "golden" datasets that save analysts time.

Measuring

  • Coverage: percent of critical assets with owner, description, classification and quality checks.
  • Quality and incidents: number and severity of data incidents, time to resolve, SLA attainment.
  • Access: time to grant access, number of audit findings, policy violations.
  • Adoption and value: use of certified datasets, reduction in duplicated metrics and reports, analyst satisfaction.

Culture

Train people, celebrate good stewardship, include data responsibilities in roles and goals, and keep a feedback loop so policies evolve with real use.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you decide which data assets are critical enough to govern first?
  • What roles do data owners and stewards have?
  • How do you show the value of governance to executives?
