Why are metrics like lines of code (LOC) and velocity flawed proxies for engineering productivity?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Delivery · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Lines of code and velocity measure activity, not value. More code is often worse, and velocity is a private planning tool that is relative to each team; once used as a target, both are gamed (Goodhart's law) and distort behaviour. Better signals look at outcomes and flow: delivery metrics, quality, reliability, customer impact and developer experience.

Explained in depth

Why lines of code fail

  • A good solution can be shorter. Deleting code, simplifying and reusing libraries are valuable and would count as negative output.
  • It varies by language, style and generated code, and rewards verbosity.
  • It ignores the hard work: design, reviews, debugging, mentoring and decisions about what not to build.

Why velocity fails as a productivity measure

  • Story points are relative to one team's estimates; they cannot be compared between teams.
  • When velocity becomes a target, teams inflate estimates, split stories artificially, or skip quality, so the number rises while value does not.
  • It measures output size, not whether customers benefit.

Goodhart's law

"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." People optimise the metric and not the goal behind it.

Better approaches

  • DORA (software delivery): deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate and time to restore service. They balance speed and stability.
  • SPACE framework: satisfaction, performance, activity, communication and collaboration, efficiency and flow. It encourages several dimensions, including developer survey data.
  • Outcome measures: customer satisfaction, adoption, revenue or cost impact, SLO attainment.
  • Developer experience: build times, review wait time, onboarding time, and survey data.

Using metrics well

Use them to find systems problems and trends at team level, never to rank individuals; discuss them openly with the team; look at several metrics together; and combine them with qualitative judgement.

Likely follow-ups

  • What are the DORA metrics and what do they show?
  • What is the SPACE framework?
  • How do you use metrics without damaging trust?
