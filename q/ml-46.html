How does Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) eliminate the separate reward model required by PPO?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · LLMs · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

DPO shows that the policy trained under a KL-constrained reward objective implicitly defines its own reward, as beta times the log ratio of the policy to the reference model. Substituting that into the Bradley-Terry preference model gives a simple loss on chosen versus rejected responses, so no separate reward model, value model or on-policy sampling loop is needed.

Explained in depth

The RLHF pipeline it replaces

Supervised fine-tuning, then train a reward model on preference pairs (chosen y_w, rejected y_l), then optimise the policy with PPO to maximise reward while a KL penalty keeps it near a reference model. PPO needs the policy, a frozen reference, a reward model and a value model, plus sampling from the policy, and is sensitive to tuning.

The key step

The KL-regularised objective max E[r(x, y)] - beta * KL(pi || pi_ref) has a closed-form optimal policy:

pi*(y|x) = pi_ref(y|x) * exp(r(x, y) / beta) / Z(x)

Rearranged, the reward can be written in terms of the policy: r(x, y) = beta * log( pi*(y|x) / pi_ref(y|x) ) + beta * log Z(x). In the Bradley-Terry model, preferences depend only on the difference of rewards for two responses to the same prompt, so the intractable log Z(x) term cancels.

The DPO loss

L = -log sigmoid( beta * [ log(pi(y_w|x)/pi_ref(y_w|x)) - log(pi(y_l|x)/pi_ref(y_l|x)) ] )

It raises the likelihood of the chosen response relative to the rejected one, measured against the reference model. Training is ordinary supervised learning on a fixed preference dataset, needing only the policy and a frozen reference (whose log-probabilities can be precomputed).

Role of beta and limits

  • Beta controls how far the policy may move from the reference; small values allow larger changes.
  • It is offline: it learns from fixed data, so quality and coverage of preferences matter, and it may not match online RL on harder tasks.
  • Known issues: the likelihood of both chosen and rejected answers can fall, length bias, and overfitting a small dataset. Variants such as IPO, KTO and SimPO address some of these.
