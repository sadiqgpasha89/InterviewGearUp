What are the core workflows and responsibilities of Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3 SOC analysts?

Security engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Operations · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Tier 1 SOC analysts monitor the alert queue, triage and resolve routine events or escalate. Tier 2 analysts investigate escalated incidents in depth, correlate data, contain threats and drive response. Tier 3 are senior analysts and threat hunters who handle advanced incidents, forensics and malware analysis, and improve detections, tooling and playbooks.

Explained in depth

What a SOC is

A Security Operations Center is the team, processes and technology that continuously monitor, detect, analyse and respond to cybersecurity threats (24 by 7 in many organisations, in-house, outsourced MSSP/MDR, or hybrid). The tiered model distributes work by skill and urgency so scarce expertise focuses on the hardest problems.

Tier 1: triage and monitoring

Role: first line, "alert analysts".

  • Monitor SIEM, EDR, email security and cloud consoles for alerts; triage: is this real, what is affected, how severe?
  • Follow runbooks and playbooks for common alerts (phishing reports, malware detections, failed login spikes, policy violations); verify with quick checks (reputation lookups, user confirmation, asset context).
  • Close false positives with documented reasons, handle simple true positives (reset password, block sender, remove phishing email, quarantine file), open and update tickets, collect initial evidence.
  • Escalate with a clear summary (what, when, where, evidence, actions taken) to Tier 2 when scope, impact or uncertainty exceeds the playbook.
  • Skills: fundamentals of networking, operating systems, common attacks, SIEM queries, ticketing; attention to detail and communication.

Tier 2: incident responders

Role: deeper investigation and response.

  • Take escalated incidents; correlate across data sources (identity, endpoint, network, cloud logs) to build a timeline and determine scope, root cause, affected accounts and systems, data exposure, and attacker techniques (map to MITRE ATT&CK).
  • Perform containment and remediation: isolate hosts via EDR, disable compromised accounts, revoke tokens and sessions, block indicators at firewalls and proxies, coordinate patching or reimaging, remove persistence.
  • Analyse suspicious artefacts (email headers, URLs, attachments in sandboxes), run threat intelligence enrichment, query logs at scale, run IR playbooks.
  • Coordinate with IT, application owners, legal, HR and communications; keep stakeholders informed; document actions and evidence for audit and potential legal action.
  • Tune detection rules and suppress noisy alerts, propose improvements.
  • Skills: incident response, log analysis, endpoint forensics basics, scripting (Python, PowerShell), cloud security, ATT&CK.

Tier 3: advanced analysts, threat hunters, subject matter experts

Role: handle the most complex and high-impact incidents and proactively look for what automated tools miss.

  • Advanced forensics and malware reverse engineering (memory, disk, network captures), investigation of APTs, ransomware, insider threats and supply chain compromises.
  • Threat hunting: hypothesis-driven searches for stealthy activity (living-off-the-land binaries, beaconing, unusual service accounts, lateral movement) using EDR and SIEM data, often derived from threat intelligence about adversary TTPs.
  • Detection engineering: write and test detections (Sigma, YARA, Suricata), build analytics, reduce false positives, validate coverage with purple team exercises and breach and attack simulation.
  • Tooling and automation: develop SOAR playbooks, integrations and enrichments; improve log onboarding and data quality.
  • Incident command for major incidents, post-incident reviews, lessons learned, training and mentoring Tier 1 and 2, red and purple team collaboration.
  • Skills: deep OS internals, reverse engineering, advanced scripting, adversary tradecraft, data analysis.

Supporting roles

SOC manager, threat intelligence analysts, detection engineers, incident response lead, forensic specialists, SOAR and platform engineers, vulnerability management liaison, and on-call management.

Typical workflow

Alert → Tier 1 triage (enrich, classify, playbook) → close or escalate with context → Tier 2 investigation, containment → Tier 3 deep analysis if complex → eradication, recovery (with IT) → post-incident review → detections and playbooks updated → metrics reported.

Metrics and health

MTTD (mean time to detect), MTTA/MTTR (acknowledge, respond or resolve), alert volume and false positive rate, escalation quality, dwell time, coverage of ATT&CK techniques, SLA adherence by severity, percentage of alerts automated, analyst workload and burnout indicators, quality assurance sampling of closed alerts.

Modern evolution

Many SOCs move from rigid tiers to flatter models with automation: SOAR and AI-assisted triage handle Tier 1 tasks, analysts specialise by domain (cloud, identity, endpoint), and MDR providers supply 24 by 7 coverage. Whatever the structure, clear escalation criteria, runbooks, shift handovers and continuous training are essential.

Pitfalls

Tier 1 as a ticket-closing factory with no growth path (attrition), escalations without context, no feedback loop from IR into detections, ignoring analyst wellbeing on 24 by 7 shifts, and measuring volume of tickets rather than quality and outcomes.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you reduce Tier 1 alert fatigue and burnout using automation and better detections?
  • What metrics such as MTTD and MTTR show whether a SOC is effective?
  • How does a SOC combine tiered and flat team models, and where do threat intelligence and detection engineering fit?
