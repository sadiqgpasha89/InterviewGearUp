How would you design a workforce identity architecture with single sign-on, strong authentication and conditional access?

Security engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Identity · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Centralise identities in one identity provider with lifecycle automation from HR, federate all applications through SAML or OIDC with SSO, enforce phishing-resistant MFA and conditional access based on user, device and risk signals, manage privileged access with just-in-time elevation, apply least-privilege roles and access reviews, and log and monitor everything. Plan break-glass accounts and resilience.

Explained in depth

Principles

Identity is the new perimeter: authenticate strongly, authorise narrowly, verify continuously, and automate the lifecycle.

Architecture

  1. Source of truth and lifecycle: HR system (Workday, SAP) drives joiner, mover, leaver events. Provisioning through SCIM and connectors creates, updates and removes accounts and group memberships automatically, so access disappears at termination.
  2. Identity provider (IdP) such as Entra ID, Okta or Google Workspace: one directory, groups and roles, and a single place for policy and audit.
  3. SSO and federation: applications authenticate via OIDC or SAML; legacy apps via a proxy or gateway; cloud consoles and servers via federation and short-lived credentials. Retire local passwords.
  4. Strong authentication: phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2 security keys, passkeys, certificate-based) for all workers, with stricter requirements for administrators; avoid SMS. Passwordless where possible.
  5. Conditional access (policy engine): decide per sign-in using user and group, device posture (managed, encrypted, patched, EDR running), location, network, application sensitivity, and risk signals (impossible travel, leaked credentials, anomaly detection). Outcomes: allow, require MFA, step-up, limit session, or block.
  6. Authorisation: role-based access with groups per job function, attribute-based rules for finer cases, least privilege, and entitlement management with request and approval workflows and expiry.
  7. Privileged access: separate admin accounts, just-in-time elevation (PIM), session recording, PAM vaults for shared secrets, and no standing domain-admin rights.
  8. Non-human identities: service accounts and workload identities with short-lived tokens and ownership.
  9. External identities: guest accounts with limited scope, B2B federation, and time-bound access.

Governance and monitoring

  • Periodic access reviews (certifications) by managers and owners; segregation-of-duties checks.
  • Central logging of sign-ins and admin actions to the SIEM, alerts for risky patterns, and identity threat detection and response.
  • Session lifetime policies, token binding, and continuous access evaluation to revoke quickly.

Resilience

Two or more break-glass emergency accounts (excluded from conditional access, very strong credentials stored offline, monitored and tested), IdP redundancy planning, documented recovery, and regular drills.

Likely follow-ups

  • What is conditional access and what signals does it use?
  • How do you handle contractors and external partners?
  • How do you design break-glass accounts safely?
