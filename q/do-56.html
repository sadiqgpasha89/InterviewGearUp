Explain Prometheus metrics architecture: Counter, Gauge, Histogram, and Summary metric types.

DevOps / SRE / Cloud · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Observability · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Prometheus has four metric types. A Counter only increases (requests, errors) and is queried with rate(). A Gauge goes up and down (memory, queue size, temperature). A Histogram counts observations into configurable buckets plus a sum and count, enabling server-side quantiles and aggregation. A Summary computes client-side quantiles that cannot be aggregated across instances.

Explained in depth

Architecture refresher

Prometheus pulls (scrapes) text-format metrics from /metrics endpoints on a schedule, stores them in a time-series database as name{labels} value timestamp, and offers PromQL for querying and alerting. Instrumentation libraries expose the types below.

Counter

Monotonically increasing number that resets to zero on process restart. Examples: http_requests_total, errors_total, bytes_sent_total.

requests = prometheus.NewCounterVec(prometheus.CounterOpts{Name: "http_requests_total"}, []string{"method", "status"})
requests.WithLabelValues("GET", "200").Inc()

Query with rate(http_requests_total[5m]) (per-second average, handles resets) or increase(). Never alert on the raw value.

Gauge

Value that can rise and fall: current memory, in-flight requests, queue depth, temperature, number of replicas. Use directly, with avg_over_time, max_over_time, or deriv()/predict_linear() for trends.

sum(kube_deployment_status_replicas_available) by (deployment)
predict_linear(node_filesystem_avail_bytes[6h], 4 * 3600) < 0     # disk full in 4 hours

Histogram

Samples observations (request duration, response size) into cumulative buckets le ("less than or equal"), exposing _bucket{le="0.1"}, _sum and _count.

# p95 latency over 5 minutes, aggregated across all instances
histogram_quantile(0.95, sum by (le) (rate(http_request_duration_seconds_bucket[5m])))
# average latency
rate(http_request_duration_seconds_sum[5m]) / rate(http_request_duration_seconds_count[5m])

Buckets must be chosen up front (for example 0.005, 0.01, 0.025, ..., 10 seconds). Quantiles are estimated by linear interpolation inside a bucket, so accuracy depends on bucket layout near your SLO threshold (put a bucket exactly at the SLO, for example 0.3 s). Histograms are aggregatable across instances, which is their main strength. Cost: one time series per bucket per label combination.

Summary

The client computes configurable quantiles (quantile="0.99") over a sliding time window and exposes them with _sum and _count. Accurate, no bucket design, but quantiles cannot be averaged or aggregated across instances (the average of p99s is not a p99), and the quantiles are fixed at instrumentation time.

Choosing

NeedType
Events counted over timeCounter
Current level or stateGauge
Latency or size distribution aggregated across a fleet, SLOsHistogram
Precise per-instance quantiles, no aggregationSummary

Newer native (sparse) histograms (Prometheus 2.40 and later, stable in 3.x) use exponentially spaced buckets with high resolution and far lower cost, removing manual bucket choices. OpenTelemetry exponential histograms map to them.

Naming and labels

Use base units (seconds, bytes), suffix counters with _total, histograms with _seconds and similar. Keep label cardinality low (method, status class, route template), never user ids or raw URLs.

Pitfalls

Using irate or raw counters in alerts, histograms with too few or poorly placed buckets, high-cardinality labels exploding series, and averaging precomputed quantiles.
