How does Dropout regularize deep neural networks during training, and how does it behave at inference?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Deep Learning · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Dropout randomly sets a fraction of activations to zero in each training step, so units cannot rely on specific partners and the network behaves like an ensemble of many thinned sub-networks, reducing overfitting. At inference, dropout is turned off and all units are used; with inverted dropout the remaining activations were scaled up during training so no adjustment is needed at test time.

Explained in depth

During training

For each forward pass, each unit is dropped with probability p (for example 0.5 for fully connected layers, 0.1 to 0.3 elsewhere). Dropped units contribute nothing and receive no gradient.

Inverted dropout scales the surviving activations by 1 / (1 - p) during training, so the expected activation stays the same:

mask = (np.random.rand(*x.shape) > p) / (1 - p)
out = x * mask          # training
out = x                 # inference: nothing to do

Why it regularises

  • It prevents co-adaptation: a neuron cannot depend on the presence of particular others, so features must be useful on their own.
  • Each step trains a different random sub-network, and the full network at inference approximates averaging their predictions (an implicit ensemble).

At inference

Use model.eval() (in PyTorch) so dropout is disabled, otherwise predictions become noisy. Forgetting to switch modes is a common bug.

Variants and notes

  • Monte Carlo dropout keeps dropout on at inference and averages several passes to estimate uncertainty.
  • DropConnect, spatial dropout for CNNs (drop whole channels), DropPath or stochastic depth in transformers and residual networks.
  • Interaction with normalisation: with batch norm, dropout can shift variance statistics, so it is often reduced or removed.
  • Very large language models trained on huge corpora for about one epoch rarely use dropout, since overfitting is less of a concern; fine-tuning on small data may use it.
  • Tune p on validation data; too high underfits.
