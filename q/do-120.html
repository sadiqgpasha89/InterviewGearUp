How do you design an observability architecture that scales to thousands of services while controlling cost?

DevOps / SRE / Cloud · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Observability · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Standardise instrumentation on OpenTelemetry with a collector tier, then set telemetry budgets: cap metric cardinality, sample traces with tail-based rules to keep errors and slow requests, log less and structure more with tiered retention, aggregate at the edge, and route data to cost-appropriate backends. Make owners see their cost, and keep alerting tied to SLOs.

Explained in depth

Why costs explode

Telemetry volume grows with services, traffic and developer enthusiasm: debug logs at scale, high-cardinality labels (user ID, request ID in metrics), and 100 percent trace capture. Vendor pricing per GB, per series or per span makes this visible fast.

Architecture

  1. Standard instrumentation: OpenTelemetry SDKs and auto-instrumentation, common semantic conventions, and shared libraries, so signals are consistent and backends can change.
  2. Collector tier: OpenTelemetry Collectors as agents (per node) and gateways (central) that batch, enrich (service, team, environment), filter, redact PII, sample and route. This is the main control point for cost.
  3. Backends by signal: metrics (Prometheus-compatible, Mimir, Thanos, VictoriaMetrics), logs (Loki, ClickHouse, OpenSearch), traces (Tempo, Jaeger), with object storage for cheap retention. Or a vendor with strict budgets.
  4. Correlation: trace ID in logs, exemplars linking metrics to traces.

Cost controls

  • Metrics: limit labels, drop high-cardinality dimensions, use recording rules and downsampling, and set per-team series quotas.
  • Traces: head-based sampling for steady baseline and tail-based sampling (keep all errors and slow traces, a small share of the rest), which needs a collector tier that sees whole traces.
  • Logs: structured, with levels; sample or drop noisy debug logs; keep hot retention short (days), warm and cold in object storage; avoid logging payloads.
  • Retention tiers by value, and aggregation of raw events into metrics where possible.

Governance

  • Ownership and showback: attribute telemetry cost to teams using the labels added at collection, with dashboards and alerts on spikes.
  • Standards for naming, dashboards and alerts, and a review for new high-volume sources.
  • Alert on SLOs and symptoms, which reduces the need to retain every raw signal. Keep a quick way to turn on debug sampling temporarily during incidents.
  • Evaluate build versus buy on total cost including engineers' time to run backends.

Reliability

Make the observability pipeline itself resilient (queues and backpressure, multiple collectors, monitoring of the monitoring), and ensure incident tooling works when the main platform is down.
