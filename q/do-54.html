How do you calculate and manage error budgets to balance feature release velocity with system stability?

DevOps / SRE / Cloud · Gear 3 (Senior) · Observability · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

An error budget is the amount of unreliability you can spend: 100 percent minus the SLO. A 99.9 percent monthly availability SLO allows about 43 minutes of downtime. While budget remains, teams ship features; when it is spent, they slow or freeze releases and prioritise reliability work, making the trade-off objective and shared between product and operations.

Explained in depth

Definitions

  • SLI: a measured indicator, for example the share of requests that succeed in under 300 ms.
  • SLO: the target for the SLI over a window, for example 99.9% over 30 days.
  • Error budget = 1 - SLO. It is the allowed failure.

Calculating it

For 99.9% availability over 30 days: 0.1% of 43,200 minutes is about 43.2 minutes. For request-based SLIs, with 100 million requests a month the budget is 100,000 failed requests.

SLOAllowed downtime per 30 days
99%about 7.2 hours
99.9%about 43 minutes
99.99%about 4.3 minutes

Using it to balance velocity and stability

  • Budget remaining: release at normal speed, take risks, run experiments.
  • Budget low or exhausted: apply the agreed error budget policy, such as pausing non-critical releases, doing more testing and fixing the causes of reliability loss, until it recovers.

This turns the argument between "ship faster" and "be safer" into a data-based agreement made in advance.

Alerting on burn rate

Alert on how fast the budget is being consumed, not on single errors. A burn rate of 1 uses the budget exactly over the window; a rate of 14 exhausts a 30-day budget in about 2 days. Multi-window alerts (for example a fast window of 5 minutes and 1 hour paired with a slower one) catch both sudden outages and slow burns with fewer false alarms.

Cautions

Choose SLOs from user expectations, not from what is achievable; 100% is the wrong target. Review them regularly, and count only what users experience.
