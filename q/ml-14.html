Compare Mean Squared Error (MSE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE).

ML / AI engineer · Gear 1 (Junior) · Metrics · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

MSE averages squared errors, so it penalises large errors heavily and is smooth for optimisation. RMSE is its square root, in the same units as the target, which makes it easier to interpret. MAE averages absolute errors, treats all errors proportionally and is more robust to outliers.

Explained in depth

Definitions

For predictions y_hat and truths y over n samples:

  • MSE = (1/n) * sum((y - y_hat)^2)
  • RMSE = sqrt(MSE)
  • MAE = (1/n) * sum(|y - y_hat|)

Differences

MSERMSEMAE
UnitsSquared unitsSame as targetSame as target
Outlier sensitivityVery high (squares errors)HighLower, linear in the error
Differentiable everywhereYesYesNot at zero
Optimal point predictorMeanMeanMedian

Example

Errors of 1, 1, 1 and 10: MAE = 3.25, MSE = 25.75 and RMSE = about 5.07. The single big error dominates MSE and RMSE but affects MAE much less.

Choosing

  • Use MSE or RMSE when large errors are especially bad, or when the noise is roughly Gaussian. MSE is a convenient training loss because it is smooth.
  • Use MAE when outliers are noise you do not want to dominate, and when you want an error in plain units ("off by 3 minutes on average").
  • Huber loss acts like MSE for small errors and like MAE for large ones.
  • For percentage-style errors consider MAPE (unstable near zero) or relative metrics, and R-squared for variance explained.

Always report the metric on a held-out set and compare it with a naive baseline such as predicting the mean.
