Your default-rate forecast broke during a macroeconomic shock. How do you make forecasting more robust to shocks?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Time Series · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Accept that history will not contain the next shock and design for fast adaptation: add leading external indicators, forecast hierarchically (global, region, segment) with reconciliation, weight recent data more, use models that detect structural breaks, publish prediction intervals and scenarios rather than one number, and monitor forecast error so you recalibrate within weeks.

Explained in depth

Inputs

  • Add leading indicators that move before defaults: interest rates, sector indices, unemployment or business insolvency rates, and your own early signals (seat reductions, late-payment share in the last 2 weeks).
  • Be careful: external data can be revised or published with a lag; use the values that were known at forecast time.

Model structure

  • Hierarchical forecasting: forecast at total, region and segment levels and reconcile so numbers add up (MinT or similar); segments react differently to shocks.
  • Adaptive components: exponentially weighted training data, rolling re-estimation, Bayesian structural time series or state space models that let levels shift, and change-point detection to trigger re-fitting.
  • Ensembles of a simple model and a richer model often degrade more gracefully than one complex model.

Communicate uncertainty

  • Publish prediction intervals and scenarios (base, mild downturn, severe downturn) built by shifting the macro inputs; finance plans reserves on scenarios, not a point estimate.
  • Back-test on past stress periods to see how wide the intervals needed to be.

Operate

  • Track forecast error weekly; when it breaches a threshold, recalibrate and add expert judgement overrides with written reasons.
  • After the shock, do a review: which signals moved first, and should they become features?

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you avoid look-ahead bias with revised macro data?
  • What is forecast reconciliation and why does it matter?
  • How would you build a severe-downturn scenario?
