How would you set up an SRE practice and reliability targets for an organisation with many services?

DevOps / SRE / Cloud · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · SRE · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Start by classifying services by criticality, define SLIs and SLOs with product owners for the critical ones, adopt error budgets with a clear policy for what happens when they are spent, build shared tooling and on-call and incident processes, and choose an engagement model such as embedded SREs or a consulting team. Scale by teaching and automation, not by absorbing all operations.

Explained in depth

Principles

Reliability is a feature with a cost. The goal is the right reliability for each service, not the highest. SREs apply software engineering to operations: automation, measurement and design for failure.

Steps

  1. Tier services (critical, important, best effort) by business impact and dependencies. Not everything needs 99.99 percent.
  2. Define SLIs and SLOs with product owners: user-facing indicators (availability of successful requests, latency at percentiles, freshness), measured at the edge or load balancer, with a window such as 28 days. Example: 99.9 percent of requests succeed in under 300 ms.
  3. Error budgets: the allowed unreliability (0.1 percent over 28 days is about 40 minutes). Write a policy that is agreed with leadership: while budget remains, ship features; when exhausted, prioritise reliability work and slow risky changes. Use burn-rate alerts (multi-window) to page on fast consumption.
  4. Operational foundations: observability standards, alerting on symptoms, on-call rotations with escalation, runbooks, incident management (roles, severity levels, status communication) and blameless postmortems with tracked actions.
  5. Engineering reliability: capacity planning, load testing, graceful degradation, safe rollouts (canaries, automated rollback), disaster recovery drills and chaos experiments.
  6. Toil reduction: measure the share of time spent on manual repetitive work (cap around 50 percent), and invest in automation.

Engagement models

  • Embedded SREs in critical product teams.
  • A central SRE or platform team providing tooling, standards and consulting, and taking on the most critical services with a production readiness review that the service must pass (and can lose, if its reliability falls and the team does not fix it).
  • "You build it, you run it" with SRE coaching for the long tail.

Measure and improve

Track SLO attainment, incident count and time to detect and restore, error budget consumption, toil, and on-call load. Review quarterly with leadership, and adjust targets with evidence.

Likely follow-ups

  • What should the error budget policy say when a budget is exhausted?
  • How do you decide which services deserve a dedicated SRE?
  • How do you reduce toil and measure it?
