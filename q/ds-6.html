What is the difference between supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning, with practical examples of each?

Data scientist · Gear 1 (Junior) · ML fundamentals · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Supervised learning learns from labelled examples to predict a target, such as churn or price. Unsupervised learning finds structure in unlabelled data, such as customer segments or anomalies. Reinforcement learning learns a policy by trial and reward in an environment, such as bidding or robotics. Semi-supervised and self-supervised methods sit between them.

Explained in depth

Supervised learning

You have inputs X and known outputs y. The model learns a mapping f(X) to y and is judged on unseen data.

  • Classification: categorical target. Examples: churn yes or no, spam, fraud, image category. Algorithms: logistic regression, trees, gradient boosting, neural networks.
  • Regression: numeric target. Examples: house price, delivery time, demand. Algorithms: linear regression, random forest, boosting.
  • Requires labels, which can be expensive or biased; labels may arrive late (fraud chargebacks after 60 days).
  • Evaluation: accuracy, precision, recall, AUC, RMSE, MAE, with proper train, validation and test splits.

Unsupervised learning

No target. The goal is structure: groups, directions of variation, unusual points.

  • Clustering: customer segmentation (k-means, hierarchical, DBSCAN, Gaussian mixtures).
  • Dimensionality reduction: PCA, t-SNE, UMAP for visualisation and compression.
  • Anomaly detection: isolation forest, one-class SVM, autoencoders for fraud or machine faults.
  • Association rules: market-basket analysis (apriori).
  • Evaluation is harder: silhouette score, stability across samples, business interpretability, downstream usefulness.

Reinforcement learning (RL)

An agent takes actions in an environment, receives rewards, and learns a policy maximising cumulative reward. Examples: game-playing agents, robotics control, dynamic pricing, ad bidding, recommendation with long-term engagement, RLHF to tune language models. Concepts: state, action, reward, exploration versus exploitation, Q-learning, policy gradients. Needs a simulator or safe way to explore, and is data-hungry and tricky to evaluate. Simpler cousins: multi-armed bandits, common in experimentation.

In between

  • Semi-supervised: a few labels plus many unlabelled examples (label propagation, pseudo-labelling).
  • Self-supervised: labels are created from the data itself (predict the next word or masked tokens, contrastive image learning); pretraining then fine-tuning is the basis of modern LLMs and vision models.
  • Weak supervision and active learning: reduce labelling cost by programmatic labels or by asking humans to label the most informative examples.

Choosing in practice

ProblemApproach
Predict whether a customer cancels, have history of outcomessupervised classification
Group customers for marketing without predefined groupsunsupervised clustering, then profile clusters
Find unusual transactions with few confirmed fraudsanomaly detection plus supervised model as labels grow
Optimise which offer to show over timebandits or RL, with careful offline evaluation

Pitfalls

Treating clusters as ground truth, using supervised metrics without checking label quality, and reaching for RL when a supervised model or an A/B test is enough.
