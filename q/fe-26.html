Explain CSS Stacking Contexts and how `z-index` works across parent and child stacking contexts.

Frontend engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · CSS · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

A stacking context is a self-contained 3D layering scope. Inside it, z-index values only compare with siblings in the same context; the context itself is painted as one unit at its own z-level in its parent. So a child with z-index 9999 cannot escape a parent stacking context with a lower rank.

Explained in depth

The rule people forget

z-index is not global. It orders elements only within the same stacking context. Each stacking context is atomic: the browser paints its whole subtree together, and the context as a whole sits at one position in its parent's ordering.

<div class="a" style="position:relative; z-index:1">   <!-- context A -->
  <div class="modal" style="position:absolute; z-index:9999">I am inside A</div>
</div>
<div class="b" style="position:relative; z-index:2">B covers the modal</div>

.modal has a huge z-index but lives in context A (rank 1); .b is rank 2 in the root context, so B paints above the whole of A, including the modal.

What creates a stacking context

  • The root element
  • position: relative or absolute with a z-index other than auto; position: fixed or sticky (always)
  • A flex or grid item with a z-index other than auto
  • opacity below 1
  • transform, filter, perspective, clip-path, mask, backdrop-filter, mix-blend-mode other than normal
  • isolation: isolate, will-change of one of those properties, contain: layout or paint

The accidental ones (transform for animation, opacity: .99, will-change: transform) are the usual reason a tooltip or dropdown is trapped behind something else.

Paint order inside one context (low to high)

  1. The context root's background and borders
  2. Descendants with negative z-index
  3. In-flow, non-positioned block descendants
  4. Floats
  5. In-flow inline content
  6. Positioned descendants with z-index: auto or 0, in DOM order
  7. Descendants with positive z-index, ascending

Debugging

Walk up from the hidden element and look at each ancestor's position, z-index, transform, opacity and filter. Browser DevTools (Chrome 3D view or the "Layers" panel, Firefox's "z-index" display) help. A quick test is to temporarily remove ancestors' properties.

Practical strategies

  • Render overlays (modals, popovers, toasts) with a portal at the end of <body> so they are in the root context.
  • Use isolation: isolate on components to create a deliberate local context, so internal z-indexes cannot leak.
  • Keep a small scale of z-index tokens (--z-dropdown, --z-modal, --z-toast) instead of arbitrary numbers.
  • The modern popover attribute and <dialog> use the top layer, which sits above all stacking contexts.

Pitfalls

Increasing z-index without understanding contexts leads to "z-index wars". Fix the structure instead.
