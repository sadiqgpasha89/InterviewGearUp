What is probability calibration, how do you measure it, and how do you fix a poorly calibrated model?

Data scientist · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Model evaluation · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

A model is calibrated if among cases predicted at 30 percent risk roughly 30 percent are positive. Measure it with reliability diagrams, expected calibration error and the Brier score, and fix miscalibration with Platt scaling, isotonic regression or temperature scaling fitted on held-out data. Calibration matters whenever you use probabilities for decisions, not only rankings.

Explained in depth

What calibration means

A probabilistic classifier is well calibrated if P(y = 1 | predicted p) = p. Out of all examples scored 0.8, about 80 percent should be positive. Discrimination (AUC) is about ranking; calibration is about the honesty of the numbers. A model can rank perfectly and still be badly calibrated (monotone transforms leave AUC unchanged).

Why it matters

Probabilities feed decisions and economics: expected loss = p x amount, thresholds from cost ratios, pricing and underwriting, clinical risk scores, bidding in ad auctions, combining model outputs, and communicating risk to people. Miscalibration leads to wrong expected-value calculations and poor thresholds.

Measuring

  • Reliability diagram (calibration curve): bin predictions (equal-width or quantile), plot mean predicted probability versus observed frequency; the diagonal is perfect.
  • Brier score: mean squared error between predicted probability and outcome (lower is better); decomposes into calibration, refinement and uncertainty.
  • Log loss: strictly proper scoring rule, punishes confident errors.
  • Expected calibration error (ECE) and max calibration error: weighted average gap across bins (sensitive to binning).
  • Calibration slope and intercept (logistic recalibration) for clinical models; calibration in the large (mean prediction versus prevalence).
from sklearn.calibration import calibration_curve, CalibratedClassifierCV
frac_pos, mean_pred = calibration_curve(y_te, p_te, n_bins=10, strategy="quantile")
from sklearn.metrics import brier_score_loss
print(brier_score_loss(y_te, p_te))

Typical patterns

  • Logistic regression: usually well calibrated if the model is correctly specified.
  • Random forests: probabilities pulled towards the middle (underconfident), because averaging trees rarely gives extreme values.
  • Boosted trees and SVMs: sigmoid-shaped distortions; boosting with log loss is often decent, but still check.
  • Modern neural networks: often overconfident.
  • Naive Bayes: overconfident (extreme probabilities).
  • Class weights, undersampling, SMOTE: shift the base rate and distort probabilities; the model's outputs no longer reflect true prevalence.
  • Dataset shift: calibration degrades when prevalence or distributions change.

Fixing it

Fit a calibrator on held-out data (not the training data the model fit; use cross-validation inside CalibratedClassifierCV) that maps raw scores to probabilities:

MethodDescriptionUse when
Platt scalinglogistic regression on the scoresmall data, sigmoid-shaped distortion
Isotonic regressionnon-parametric monotone mappingenough data (thousands), arbitrary shapes; can overfit small samples
Temperature scalingdivide logits by a learned Tneural networks, multi-class
Beta calibration, Venn-Abersalternatives with validity guarantees
Prior-shift correctionadjust odds for known change in prevalenceresampled training data or new population

After calibrating, re-check the reliability diagram on a fresh test set. Calibrate per important subgroup when needed, as overall calibration can hide subgroup miscalibration (a fairness issue).

Workflow

Train for ranking quality, calibrate afterwards on a representative holdout, choose thresholds from calibrated probabilities and costs, and monitor calibration drift in production.

Pitfalls

Calibrating on the training set, using isotonic with tiny data, assuming AUC implies calibration, and forgetting to recalibrate after the base rate changes.
