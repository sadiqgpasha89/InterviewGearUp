Design a large-scale recommendation system for an e-commerce platform, from candidate generation to ranking and serving.

ML / AI engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · System design · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Use a multi-stage funnel: fast candidate generation from collaborative filtering, two-tower retrieval and popularity sources narrows millions of items to hundreds, a feature-rich ranking model scores them, and re-ranking applies business rules, diversity and freshness. Serve with precomputed embeddings, a feature store and caching, train on logged interactions, and evaluate offline then with A/B tests.

Explained in depth

Requirements and metrics

Personalised homepage, product pages and emails with latency under about 100 to 200 ms, millions of items and users. Business goals: conversion, revenue, engagement, and also diversity and long-term satisfaction. Guardrails: latency, inventory, fairness, and no recommendations the user should not see.

Funnel architecture

  1. Candidate generation (recall): choose about 500 to 1000 items from millions using several sources merged together:
  • Two-tower model: user tower and item tower produce embeddings; approximate nearest neighbour search (FAISS, ScaNN, HNSW) retrieves items near the user vector.
  • Item-to-item collaborative filtering ("people who bought this also bought"), co-visitation, recently viewed, trending and popular items, content-based similarity, and category affinities.
  1. Ranking: a heavier model (gradient boosting, or a deep network such as Wide and Deep, DLRM, or transformer-based sequence models) scores each candidate for a target such as click or purchase probability, using rich features: user history and context, item attributes, price, freshness, and cross features. Often multi-objective (click, add to cart, purchase, return).
  2. Re-ranking: business rules and constraints: stock availability, diversity (avoid ten similar items), exploration (inject new items), sponsored placements, deduplication, and fairness.

Data and features

  • Event pipeline (impressions, clicks, purchases) through Kafka into a lake; training labels built from logged outcomes with point-in-time correct features.
  • Feature store: offline for training, online (Redis, DynamoDB) for serving, ensuring consistent computation.
  • Real-time features from the session (recent clicks) alongside batch features.

Serving

Request path: fetch user features, retrieve candidates, rank, re-rank, return in time budget. Precompute item embeddings and index offline, cache popular lists, run models on CPU or GPU with batching, and fall back to popularity if a component fails.

Training and evaluation

  • Negative sampling for retrieval; calibrated probabilities for ranking.
  • Offline: recall at K, NDCG, MAP, AUC, calibration, on time-based splits. Offline gains do not guarantee online wins because of logging bias.
  • Online A/B tests with business and guardrail metrics; interleaving for faster ranking comparisons.

Hard problems

  • Cold start: use content features, popularity and exploration for new items and users; contextual bandits.
  • Feedback loops and popularity bias: log exploration traffic, use inverse propensity weighting.
  • Freshness: incremental training and near-real-time embeddings updates.
  • Monitoring: drift, coverage, diversity, latency, and model rollback.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you address cold start for new users and new items?
  • How do you avoid feedback loops and popularity bias?
  • What offline metrics predict online success, and where do they fail?
