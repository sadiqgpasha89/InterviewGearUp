How do you successfully merge two engineering teams with differing tech stacks and cultures?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Management · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Merging teams with different stacks and cultures works best with a clear shared mission, fast and transparent communication, explicit leadership roles, and respect for both sides' strengths. Map the differences, agree working principles together, create joint goals and early shared wins, converge technology gradually by business value and cost, and watch retention and morale closely.

Explained in depth

Start with clarity

  • Define why the teams are merging, the combined mission, and what success looks like. Settle leadership and reporting lines early to avoid power vacuums, and be open about the decision process.
  • Communicate quickly and honestly; uncertainty makes the best people leave.

Understand both sides

  • Hold listening sessions and 1-on-1s with everyone: strengths, concerns, pride points, what they would keep.
  • Map practices and culture: how they plan, review code, release, handle incidents, document and make decisions. Map the tech stacks, ownership, and critical knowledge.

Build one team

  • Co-create working agreements and principles (for example how decisions are made, review norms), taking the best from each side instead of imposing one culture.
  • Mix people in joint projects and pairing, rotate roles and presentations, and share knowledge through demos and brown bags.
  • Set shared goals, and achieve early wins together. Celebrate contributions from both former teams and avoid "us and them" language.
  • Invest in team building and psychological safety, with retros focused on the merge itself.

Tech convergence

  • Do not rush a rewrite. Decide using criteria: business value, operational cost, hiring market, reliability, security and migration effort. Document the decision as an ADR.
  • Options: converge on one stack over time, keep both with clear boundaries and interfaces, or retire one product. Offer training and time to learn the target stack and recognise the expertise being transitioned.
  • Standardise the essentials first (CI/CD, observability, on-call, security), then languages and frameworks.

Monitor

Track attrition, engagement, delivery metrics and informal feedback; address friction fast and adjust the plan as the team learns.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you decide which tech stack wins, or whether to keep both?
  • How do you handle two strong leaders from the merged teams?
  • How do you handle people who feel they are on the losing side?
