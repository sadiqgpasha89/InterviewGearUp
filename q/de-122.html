How do you define SLAs and SLOs for data products and manage data incidents?

Data engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Reliability · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Treat datasets as products with measurable objectives for freshness, completeness, accuracy and availability, agreed with consumers and monitored automatically. Alert on SLO burn, route incidents to owners with severity levels and runbooks, communicate impact on downstream consumers using lineage, and run blameless postmortems that produce preventive checks and contracts.

Explained in depth

Define the objectives

For each critical data product agree with consumers:

  • Freshness: "the orders table is updated within 30 minutes of source commits, 99 percent of the time".
  • Completeness and volume: expected row counts and null rates within tolerance.
  • Accuracy and consistency: reconciles with the source of truth within a threshold.
  • Availability: query access and pipeline success rate.
  • Schema stability: no breaking changes without notice.

SLIs are the measured metrics; SLOs are targets over a window; SLAs are agreements with consequences. Tier datasets by criticality so effort matches impact.

Monitor

  • Automated checks inside pipelines (tests, expectations) plus data observability (freshness, volume, schema and distribution anomaly detection; tools such as Monte Carlo, Soda, or custom monitors).
  • A dashboard of SLO status per product, with error budget consumption and burn-rate alerts.
  • Use lineage (OpenLineage, catalog) to know downstream dashboards, models and teams.

Prevent propagation

Circuit-breaker patterns: failing critical checks stop publishing (write-audit-publish with staging tables and branch-based table formats), quarantine bad records, and mark datasets as stale with a visible banner in BI. Data contracts catch breaking upstream changes early.

Incident management

  1. Detect and triage: severity levels based on consumers and decisions affected (executive report, customer-facing features, ML models).
  2. Assign an incident lead and communicate in a channel: what is known, impact, who is affected, next update time. Use lineage to notify downstream owners.
  3. Mitigate: roll back to the last good snapshot (time travel), pause downstream jobs, rerun or backfill after the fix.
  4. Recover and verify with reconciliation, then confirm to consumers.
  5. Postmortem (blameless): timeline, root and contributing causes, detection gaps, and action items such as new tests, contracts, alerts or design changes, tracked to completion.

Culture

Make data reliability visible in team goals, include data on-call, and review incident trends monthly.
