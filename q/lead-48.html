How do you define team mission charters and write actionable OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Delivery · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

A team charter states the team's mission, who it serves, what it owns and how it works. OKRs then set a few ambitious Objectives, qualitative and inspiring, each with 2 to 5 measurable Key Results that show outcomes, not activities. Align them with company goals, review them regularly, and treat them as a learning tool rather than a performance score.

Explained in depth

Team charter

A one or two page living document:

  • Mission: why the team exists and whom it serves (customers, internal teams).
  • Scope and ownership: systems, services and responsibilities, and what is explicitly out of scope.
  • Customers and interfaces: how others engage the team, and service expectations.
  • Principles and ways of working: decision making, on-call, communication.
  • Success measures.

OKRs

  • Objective: a short, qualitative, inspiring statement. "Make checkout feel instant and reliable."
  • Key Results: 2 to 5 measurable outcomes with a baseline and target, which would prove the objective is achieved.
  • Good: "Reduce p95 checkout latency from 1.8 s to 0.8 s."
  • Good: "Raise checkout success rate from 96.5% to 98.5%."
  • Poor (a task): "Migrate to the new payment library."
  • Initiatives are the work you choose to move the key results and are tracked separately.

Process

  1. Start from company or department goals and customer problems.
  2. Draft with the team, not for it. Keep 2 to 3 objectives per quarter.
  3. Agree owners and check alignment with dependent teams.
  4. Review progress weekly or biweekly, adjusting initiatives (not the goals) as you learn.
  5. At the end, score honestly (0.0 to 1.0), reflect on what you learned, and do not tie scores directly to compensation.

Pitfalls

Too many objectives, vanity metrics, outputs disguised as results, sandbagged targets, and writing OKRs once and forgetting them. Stretch goals are meant to be about 70 percent achievable.
