Walk through the TCP three-way handshake and four-way connection termination states.

DevOps / SRE / Cloud · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Networking · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

TCP opens with a three-way handshake (SYN, SYN-ACK, ACK) so both sides agree on sequence numbers and options. It closes with a four-way exchange (FIN, ACK, FIN, ACK) because each direction is shut down separately. The side that closes first ends in TIME_WAIT to make sure the last ACK arrives and old packets die out.

Explained in depth

Opening (three-way handshake)

  1. Client sends SYN with its initial sequence number (client state SYN_SENT).
  2. Server replies SYN-ACK, acknowledging it and sending its own sequence number (server state SYN_RECEIVED).
  3. Client sends ACK. Both sides are now ESTABLISHED.

Options are negotiated in the SYN packets: maximum segment size, window scaling, selective acknowledgements and timestamps. Until the final ACK arrives, the server holds a half-open entry, which is what a SYN flood abuses and why SYN cookies exist.

Closing (four-way termination)

  1. The active closer sends FIN (FIN_WAIT_1).
  2. The other side sends ACK (the closer enters FIN_WAIT_2; the peer is in CLOSE_WAIT and may still send data, a half-close).
  3. When the peer's application closes, it sends its own FIN (LAST_ACK).
  4. The closer sends the final ACK and waits in TIME_WAIT for twice the maximum segment lifetime (60 seconds on Linux), then moves to CLOSED.

States worth recognising when debugging

  • Many TIME_WAIT sockets on a client or proxy are normal, but can exhaust ephemeral ports under high connection churn. Use connection reuse (keep-alive, pooling).
  • Many CLOSE_WAIT sockets mean the application received a FIN and never closed its side. That is a bug (a leaked connection).
  • Many SYN_RECV can mean a SYN flood or a full accept backlog.

A RST packet aborts a connection immediately, for example when a port is closed. Inspect states with ss -tan or netstat.

Likely follow-ups

  • Why does TIME_WAIT exist and what are the downsides of many sockets in that state?
  • What does a growing number of CLOSE_WAIT sockets tell you?
  • How do SYN cookies defend against a SYN flood?
