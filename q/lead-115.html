Why do you want to work here, and why this role?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 1 (Junior) · Behavioral · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Show you researched the company: connect its product, mission, technology or culture to your skills and goals, explain what you would contribute in the role, and why it fits your next step. Be specific and honest rather than generic praise or only mentioning salary. End with how you would add value and what you hope to learn.

Explained in depth

What they are assessing

Genuine interest, preparation and fit. Generic answers ("great company, good culture") suggest low effort.

How to prepare

  • Read the company's website, product pages, engineering blog, recent news, job description and values. Try the product if possible.
  • Identify two or three specifics that interest you: a product problem, a technology choice, the scale, the mission, the way teams work, or growth opportunities.
  • Map them to your skills and experiences: "I built X, which is similar to your Y".

Structure (about one minute)

  1. The company: "I'm interested in how you handle [specific problem or mission]."
  2. The role: "This role uses [skills], where I have done [relevant work]."
  3. Your contribution: what you can deliver in the first months.
  4. Your growth: what you want to learn there, and why it aligns with your career direction.

Example

"I have used your API in a side project and liked how clear the docs are. I see you are growing the platform team, and my experience building internal tools in Python and improving build times fits that. I also want to work on larger-scale systems, which this role offers."

Tips

  • Be honest; do not flatter excessively or criticise your current employer.
  • Do not lead with pay or perks, and avoid answers that fit any company.
  • Tie your answer to the role's real responsibilities.

Likely follow-ups

  • What do you know about our product or users?
  • Where do you see yourself in three years?
  • What would make you leave a job?
