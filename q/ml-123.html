How would you optimise the length of the grace period before restricting service for an overdue customer?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Experimentation · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Frame it as a policy choice with a delayed, two-sided reward: cash recovered and losses avoided versus churn and goodwill lost. Start with a randomised test of a few grace lengths across risk segments, estimate segment-level effects, then consider a contextual bandit with safety limits. Because rewards arrive weeks later, use batched updates and off-policy evaluation, and keep policy rules a human can approve.

Explained in depth

Define the objective

  • Reward per account = expected cash recovered - expected write-off - (churn probability x customer lifetime value) - operational cost.
  • Rewards arrive late (payment within 60 days, renewal months later), so define a short-term proxy and validate it against the long-term outcome.

Step 1: a randomised experiment

  • Test a small set of options (for example 7, 14 and 30 days) with randomisation stratified by risk band, size and tenure.
  • Estimate the effect per segment (heterogeneous treatment effects, for example with uplift models or causal forests). This often gives most of the value: long grace for loyal low-risk accounts, short for high-risk new ones.

Step 2: a contextual bandit, if it is worth it

  • Context: risk score, payment history, size, tenure, usage trend. Actions: the grace lengths. Algorithms: Thompson sampling or LinUCB.
  • Adapt it to the delay: update in batches as outcomes mature, and use the proxy reward.
  • Safety: minimum and maximum grace by contract and law, a cap on exploration share, never explore the harshest action on the largest accounts, and a fixed holdout to measure overall lift.
  • Log action probabilities so you can do off-policy evaluation (inverse propensity or doubly robust estimators) of new policies before deploying them.

Governance

  • Turn the learned policy into readable rules finance and legal can approve, and review them regularly. A fully opaque adaptive policy that restricts service is hard to defend to customers.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you handle a reward that arrives 90 days after the action?
  • Why log the probability of each action?
  • When is a simple A/B test better than a bandit?
