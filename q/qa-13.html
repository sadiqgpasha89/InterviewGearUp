What is Exploratory Testing, and how do you structure session-based test charters for team exploratory runs?

QA / SDET · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Fundamentals · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Exploratory testing combines learning, test design and execution at the same time, guided by a goal rather than a script. Session-based test management keeps it structured: time-boxed sessions (60 to 120 minutes), each with a charter stating the mission, notes taken during the session, and a short debrief afterwards.

Explained in depth

Charters

A charter is a one-sentence mission, not a list of steps: Explore <area> with <resources, data, tools> to discover <information or risk> Example: "Explore checkout with expired, international and declined cards to discover gaps in error handling." Keep it narrow enough to finish in one session.

Running a session

  • Time-box it (often 60 to 120 minutes), without interruptions.
  • Take notes as you go: what you tried, what you saw, bugs, questions, ideas for later, and what you did not cover.
  • Track the split between testing, bug investigation and setup, to see where time is lost.
  • Use heuristics to generate ideas: SFDIPOT (Structure, Function, Data, Interfaces, Platform, Operations, Time), tours (such as the money tour or the back-alley tour), boundaries, CRUD, interruptions and slow networks.

Debrief

Spend 10 to 15 minutes with a lead or a peer to review the notes and bugs, agree follow-up charters and decide what to automate. A common prompt is PROOF: Past, Results, Obstacles, Outlook, Feelings.

Doing it as a team

  • Split the product into charters and assign them, then rotate areas so fresh eyes find new problems.
  • Pair or mob for tricky areas, and invite developers, designers and support staff to run a session.
  • Run sessions on new features, high-risk areas and before releases.
  • Convert useful findings into automated regression tests. Exploratory testing finds what scripts did not anticipate, and automation protects against recurrence.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you report exploratory testing progress to a manager who wants numbers?
  • How does exploratory testing differ from ad hoc testing?
  • Which heuristics would you use to explore a file upload feature?
