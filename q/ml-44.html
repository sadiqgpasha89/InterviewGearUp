What is Instruction Tuning and how does dataset formatting (Alpaca/ShareGPT format) steer LLM behavior?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · LLMs · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Instruction tuning fine-tunes a pre-trained model on examples of instructions paired with good responses, so it learns to follow requests instead of merely continuing text. The data format, such as Alpaca (instruction, input, output) or ShareGPT (multi-turn conversations with roles), and the chat template used, directly shape the model's behaviour, tone and ability to handle multi-turn chat.

Explained in depth

What it does

A base model predicts likely continuations of text. After supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on instruction and response pairs, it learns the pattern "when asked something, answer helpfully". Quality and diversity of the data matter more than sheer quantity (the LIMA paper showed about 1,000 curated examples can go a long way).

Common formats

Alpaca style (single turn):

{"instruction": "Summarise the text.", "input": "Long text here...", "output": "Short summary."}

ShareGPT or chat style (multi-turn with roles):

{"conversations": [
  {"from": "human", "value": "What is a mutex?"},
  {"from": "gpt", "value": "A mutex is a lock that..."}
]}

Modern pipelines use a chat template with special tokens for system, user and assistant turns.

Details that steer behaviour

  • Template consistency: use exactly the same template in training and at inference. A mismatch leads to degraded answers, missing stop tokens and runaway generation.
  • Loss masking: compute loss only on the assistant tokens, not on the prompt, so the model learns to answer and not to imitate user text.
  • End-of-turn token: include it so the model knows when to stop.
  • Data mix: variety of tasks, lengths and styles; include system prompts, refusals and multi-turn context if you want these behaviours; remove duplicates and low-quality or unsafe answers.
  • Synthetic data from stronger models can help, but check licences and the risk of copying errors.

After SFT

Preference tuning (RLHF, DPO) further aligns style and safety. Evaluate with held-out tasks, human or model-based judging, and regression checks for general abilities (catastrophic forgetting).

Likely follow-ups

  • Why is it important to mask the loss on the prompt and train only on the response?
  • What problems come from a mismatched chat template at inference?
  • How does instruction tuning differ from preference alignment such as RLHF or DPO?
