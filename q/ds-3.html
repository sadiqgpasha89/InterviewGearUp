Explain precision, recall and F1 score, and give examples of when you would favour each.

Data scientist · Gear 1 (Junior) · Model evaluation · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Precision is the share of predicted positives that are correct, recall is the share of actual positives found, and F1 is their harmonic mean. Favour precision when false alarms are costly (spam filtering of important mail), recall when misses are costly (cancer screening, fraud), and F1 or a cost-weighted metric when you need a balance.

Explained in depth

Definitions from the confusion matrix

For a binary classifier: TP (predicted positive, actually positive), FP (false alarm), FN (miss), TN.

  • Precision = TP / (TP + FP): of everything flagged, how much was right?
  • Recall (sensitivity, true positive rate) = TP / (TP + FN): of all real positives, how many did we catch?
  • F1 = 2 x precision x recall / (precision + recall): the harmonic mean, which is low if either is low. F-beta weights recall beta times as much as precision (F2 favours recall).
  • Specificity = TN / (TN + FP), accuracy = (TP + TN) / total.

Worked example

A fraud model flags 100 transactions. 80 are real fraud (TP = 80, FP = 20). There were 200 fraudulent transactions in total (FN = 120).

  • Precision = 80/100 = 0.80. Recall = 80/200 = 0.40. F1 = 2 x 0.8 x 0.4 / 1.2 = 0.53.

Accuracy could still be about 99.9 percent because most transactions are legitimate, which is why accuracy is misleading on imbalanced data.

When to favour which

SituationCost structureFavour
Cancer or disease screening, safety defects, security intrusionmissing a positive is far worsehigh recall (accept follow-up tests for false alarms)
Fraud detection with manual review capacitymisses cost money, but analysts are limitedrecall at a fixed review budget, so precision at top-k
Spam filter, content moderation auto-removal, legal e-discovery productiona false positive hurts users (lost mail)high precision
Search and recommendationsusers see only top resultsprecision at k, recall at k, NDCG
General balance, no clear costsboth errors matterF1 or PR AUC

Better: put real costs on FP and FN and choose the threshold that minimises expected cost.

Threshold effect

Most models output scores. Lowering the threshold flags more cases: recall rises, precision usually falls. The precision-recall curve shows the trade-off; pick the operating point from business constraints ("at least 90 percent recall" or "no more than 5 percent false alarms").

from sklearn.metrics import precision_recall_curve, classification_report
p, r, t = precision_recall_curve(y_true, scores)
print(classification_report(y_true, scores > 0.35))

Multi-class averaging

  • Macro: average per-class metric equally (treats rare classes as important).
  • Micro: pool all decisions (dominated by frequent classes; equals accuracy for single-label).
  • Weighted: average weighted by class support.

Pitfalls

Reporting accuracy for rare events, comparing F1 at different thresholds, ignoring calibration, and tuning the threshold on the test set.

Likely follow-ups

  • Why is accuracy a poor metric for imbalanced classes?
  • How does changing the classification threshold move precision and recall?
  • What are macro, micro and weighted averages for multi-class F1?
