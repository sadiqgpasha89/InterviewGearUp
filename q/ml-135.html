How do you enforce privacy, access control and fair-treatment rules in agentic insurance workflows?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Ethics · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Redact or tokenise personal and payment data before it reaches the model, use model endpoints with zero data retention and no training on your data, enforce role- and attribute-based access in the tool layer with the end user's identity, keep protected attributes and their proxies out of decisions, require human review for adverse outcomes, and log every step immutably for regulators.

Explained in depth

Data protection

  • An inline redaction step (Cloud DLP, Microsoft Presidio, NER plus rules) replaces national IDs, licence numbers, card and bank details with tokens (<SSN_1>) and keeps the mapping in a secure vault; the real value is reinserted only by trusted code that needs it.
  • Minimise: send the model only the fields the task needs. Health information in claims needs extra care (for example HIPAA in the US, GDPR special categories in the EU).
  • Use enterprise model endpoints with contractual zero data retention and no training on customer data, in the required region.

Access control in the tools, not the prompt

  • Tools run with the calling user's identity and scopes (RBAC plus attributes such as region, line of business, claim assignment).
  • A customer-facing agent can never call tools tagged for internal use (payouts, adjuster notes, underwriting models).
  • Deny by default, and check authorisation on every call on the server side.

Fair treatment

  • Decisions use approved rating and underwriting factors only; protected attributes are excluded and proxies tested.
  • Keep the decision itself in deterministic rules or validated models; the LLM extracts and explains, it does not decide eligibility or price.
  • Monitor outcomes (referral rates, decline rates, time to settle) across segments, and give customers a reason and a route to appeal.

Human oversight and audit

  • Adverse decisions (declines, reduced payouts) are reviewed by a qualified person before they go to the customer; many regulators expect this, and it is good practice everywhere.
  • An append-only audit log per case: inputs, retrieved documents, model and prompt versions, tool calls with arguments and results, the decision and the human sign-off, with timestamps.
  • Rules differ by country and line of business (GLBA, HIPAA, GDPR, India's IRDAI and DPDP Act, and others); compliance owns the mapping of rules to controls.

Likely follow-ups

  • Why should authorisation be enforced in the tool layer rather than the prompt?
  • How do you reinsert tokenised values safely?
  • How would you monitor for unfair outcomes?
