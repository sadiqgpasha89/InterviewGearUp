Explain the STRIDE threat modeling framework: Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Info Disclosure, DoS, Elevation.

Security engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Threat Modeling · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

STRIDE is a threat modelling checklist that classifies threats into Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information disclosure, Denial of service and Elevation of privilege. You draw a data flow diagram with trust boundaries, apply the six categories to each element and flow, and record mitigations such as authentication, integrity checks, logging, encryption, rate limiting and authorisation.

Explained in depth

The six categories and the property they violate

ThreatViolatesExampleTypical mitigation
SpoofingAuthenticityAttacker logs in as another user, forged service identityStrong authentication, MFA, mTLS
TamperingIntegrityChanging a price in a request or modifying a stored recordSignatures, MACs, input validation, access control
RepudiationNon-repudiationUser denies making a transfer, no audit trailSecure audit logs, signed events, time stamps
Information disclosureConfidentialityLeaked database, verbose errors, unencrypted trafficEncryption, least privilege, data minimisation
Denial of serviceAvailabilityFlooding an API, resource exhaustionRate limits, quotas, autoscaling, timeouts
Elevation of privilegeAuthorisationNormal user gains admin rightsAuthorisation checks, least privilege, sandboxing

Process

  1. Model the system: draw a data flow diagram with external entities, processes, data stores, data flows and trust boundaries (where privilege or ownership changes).
  2. Identify threats: for each element and each flow crossing a boundary, ask which STRIDE categories apply (processes are open to all six, data stores to T, R, I and D, and so on).
  3. Rate and prioritise by impact and likelihood.
  4. Mitigate: choose controls, accept or transfer the risk, and record decisions.
  5. Validate: test the mitigations and revisit when the design changes.

Practices

Do it early in design, with developers and security together, keep the model as a living document, and track resulting tasks in the backlog. Tools: Microsoft Threat Modeling Tool, OWASP Threat Dragon and diagrams as code.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you decide which threats to fix first?
  • How does STRIDE map to security properties?
  • What is the difference between STRIDE and attack trees?
