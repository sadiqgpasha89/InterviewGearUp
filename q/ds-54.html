How do you design a metrics framework for a product, including a north star metric, input metrics and guardrails, and avoid Goodhart's law?

Data scientist · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Product analytics · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Derive a north star from the value users receive and the business model, decompose it into input metrics teams can influence, add guardrail and counter metrics to catch harm, and validate that each metric is measurable, sensitive and linked to long-term outcomes. Review regularly because targets distort behaviour, as Goodhart's law warns.

Explained in depth

Start with strategy and users

Ask: what value does the product give users, how does the company make money, and what behaviour signals real value delivery? A good north star metric captures sustained customer value and predicts business success: Airbnb nights booked, Spotify time spent listening, a marketplace's successful transactions per week, a SaaS tool's weekly active teams completing a key action. Clicks and page views alone rarely qualify.

Properties of a good metric

  • Meaningful: reflects user value and business outcomes.
  • Measurable and well-defined: precise definition (numerator, denominator, unit, time window, inclusions), reliable instrumentation.
  • Sensitive: moves within the experiment horizon when the product changes (otherwise use a proxy).
  • Robust and hard to game, not trivially manipulated by notifications or dark patterns.
  • Actionable: teams understand how to influence it.
  • Stable semantics over time and explainable to stakeholders.

Metric tree

Decompose the north star into input (driver) metrics, which teams own. Example for an e-commerce marketplace: *Gross merchandise value = visitors x conversion rate x average order value*, with conversion split into search success, product page quality, checkout completion, and delivery reliability. Each team picks input metrics tied to the north star; experiments evaluate primary metrics, secondary diagnostic metrics, and guardrails.

Balance quantity with quality (watch time with satisfaction surveys), short-term with long-term, user side with provider side.

Proxy and surrogate metrics

Long-term outcomes (annual retention, lifetime value) are slow. Identify leading indicators that correlate with and ideally causally drive them (e.g., "added 7 friends in 10 days"), and validate with cohort analysis, holdout experiments, and surrogate-index methods across many past experiments (do proxy gains translate into long-term gains?). Correlation alone may reflect user type, not causality.

Goodhart's law and metric hygiene

"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." Teams may optimise a number in ways that hurt users (notification spam raising DAU, easier-to-click ads, cherry-picked definitions). Mitigations: paired counter-metrics, long-term holdouts, qualitative research and surveys, auditing definitions, avoiding targets on easily gamed metrics, rotating or complementing metrics, and reviewing incentives. Also monitor metric definition changes, instrumentation breaks, and Simpson's paradox across segments.

Process

Write a metric spec (definition, owner, SQL logic, known caveats), build trusted dashboards from a single semantic layer, define experiment decision rules ("ship if primary improves with confidence interval above 0 and no guardrail degrades beyond a threshold"), and revisit metrics each strategy cycle.

Example: recommendation feature

Primary: incremental conversions or revenue per user (A/B). Secondary: CTR, diversity of consumption, discovery of new items. Guardrails: latency, returns, complaints, creator or seller exposure fairness, long-term retention holdback.

Pitfalls

Choosing many co-equal metrics, optimising engagement without value, ignoring segments, and using relative changes without sample sizes.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you validate that a short-term proxy metric actually predicts long-term retention or revenue?
  • What makes a metric sensitive enough for experiments yet resistant to gaming?
  • How would you define success metrics for a new recommendation feature?
