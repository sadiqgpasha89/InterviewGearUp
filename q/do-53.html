Define Service Level Indicators (SLI), Service Level Objectives (SLO), and Service Level Agreements (SLA).

DevOps / SRE / Cloud · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Observability · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

An SLI is a measured indicator of service quality, such as the share of requests that succeed within 300 ms. An SLO is the internal target for that SLI over a window, such as 99.9% over 30 days. An SLA is a contractual promise to customers with penalties, normally set looser than the SLO. The gap between 100% and the SLO is the error budget.

Explained in depth

The three terms

  • SLI (indicator): a number you measure, best expressed as good events / valid events. Examples: successful requests over all requests, requests faster than 300 ms over all requests, jobs that finish on time.
  • SLO (objective): the target for the SLI over a time window, such as 99.9% of requests succeed over a rolling 30 days. It is an engineering decision that balances reliability against cost and speed of change.
  • SLA (agreement): a business contract that says what happens (credits, refunds) if the service falls below a stated level. Set it below the SLO so you hear about trouble before you owe money.

Error budget

Budget = 100% minus the SLO. A 99.9% SLO over 30 days allows about 43 minutes of failure (30 days x 24 hours x 60 minutes x 0.1%). While there is budget left, the team can ship quickly; if it is exhausted, they slow releases and focus on reliability.

Choosing good SLIs

  • Measure what users experience on key journeys (checkout, login), not internal CPU numbers.
  • Use percentiles (p95, p99) for latency, not averages, which hide slow tails.
  • Measure at the edge, such as the load balancer or the client, so failures upstream are visible.
  • Keep to a few SLIs per service: availability, latency, and where relevant correctness or freshness.

Alerting

Alert on burn rate, how quickly the budget is being consumed, with fast and slow windows, instead of on every error spike. That means you page for real user impact and not for noise.

Do not aim for 100%: it is unattainable, extremely costly and stops all change.

Likely follow-ups

  • How would you pick the SLO for a new service with no history?
  • How does burn-rate alerting differ from threshold alerting?
  • What should happen when a team has used up its error budget?
