How do you build an accessibility program for a large product organisation rather than fixing issues ad hoc?

Frontend engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Accessibility · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Set a target standard such as WCAG 2.2 AA, bake accessibility into the design system and components so teams inherit it, add automated checks in CI and manual audits with assistive technology, train designers and engineers, assign clear ownership and metrics, include disabled users in research, and track and prioritise defects like any other quality issue.

Explained in depth

1. Set the standard and ownership

  • Choose the target: WCAG 2.2 level AA, plus legal requirements (ADA, EN 301 549, European Accessibility Act). Publish an accessibility policy and an accessibility statement.
  • Name an owner (an accessibility lead or small centre of excellence) and champions in each team. Leadership sponsorship matters, so include accessibility in the definition of done and in quarterly goals.

2. Prevent issues at the source

  • Design system: accessible, tested components (focus management, keyboard support, ARIA patterns, colour contrast tokens) so product teams get compliance by default. Document usage and known limits.
  • Design phase: annotated mockups for headings, focus order, labels and states; colour contrast and target-size checks.
  • Training: role-based, practical (keyboard and screen reader demos), onboarding for new hires.

3. Detect issues continuously

  • Automated: eslint-plugin-jsx-a11y, axe-core in unit, component and end-to-end tests, and Lighthouse in CI. Automation finds roughly a third of issues (missing alt text, contrast, labels), but not whether the experience makes sense.
  • Manual: keyboard-only walkthroughs, screen readers (NVDA, VoiceOver, TalkBack), zoom and reflow, reduced motion, and speech control, run before launches on key journeys.
  • Expert audits and user testing with people with disabilities for critical flows.

4. Manage and measure

  • Track defects in the normal backlog with severity (blockers for completing a task come first) and SLAs.
  • Metrics: open issues by severity, automated-test pass rate, share of components and flows audited, time to fix, and user feedback.
  • Maintain a VPAT or conformance report for customers and a clear feedback channel.

5. Procurement and third parties

Require accessibility from vendors, evaluate widgets before adoption, and plan alternatives or wrappers for those that fall short.
