Compare Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), LSTMs, and GRUs in managing long-term temporal dependencies.

ML / AI engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Deep Learning · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

A vanilla RNN passes a hidden state from step to step, but repeated multiplication makes gradients vanish or explode over long sequences, so it forgets distant context. LSTMs add a cell state with input, forget and output gates that let information and gradients flow across many steps; GRUs merge these into two gates, giving similar results with fewer parameters.

Explained in depth

Vanilla RNN

h_t = tanh(W_h h_{t-1} + W_x x_t + b). Training uses backpropagation through time, which multiplies by W_h and tanh derivatives at every step. Gradients vanish (so the model cannot learn dependencies more than about 10 to 20 steps back) or explode (fixed by gradient clipping).

LSTM

Adds a cell state c_t, a memory line with additive updates, controlled by gates (sigmoid outputs between 0 and 1):

  • Forget gate f_t: how much of the old cell to keep.
  • Input gate i_t with candidate g_t: what new information to write.
  • Output gate o_t: how much of the cell to expose as hidden state.

c_t = f_t * c_{t-1} + i_t * g_t, h_t = o_t * tanh(c_t) Because the cell update is mostly additive and the forget gate can stay near 1, gradients can flow across many time steps (a constant error carousel).

GRU

Two gates: an update gate (how much of the old state to keep) and a reset gate (how much past to use when proposing the new state). No separate cell state. About 25 percent fewer parameters than an LSTM and often equally accurate.

Limits

Sequential computation prevents parallelism over time steps, and very long contexts are still hard. Transformers use attention to connect any two positions directly and train in parallel, so they replaced LSTMs in most NLP tasks; RNN-style models still appear in small, streaming and low-latency settings.
