Compare Agile Scrum and Kanban: How do you choose the right delivery framework for your team?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Delivery · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Scrum uses fixed-length sprints, defined roles and ceremonies to commit to a batch of work each cycle, which suits teams building product increments with plannable work. Kanban is a continuous flow system that visualises work and limits work in progress, which suits interrupt-driven or operational work. Choose by how predictable the work is and how much the team needs cadence versus flow.

Explained in depth

Scrum

  • Time-boxed sprints (1 to 4 weeks) with a sprint goal, a prioritised backlog and a commitment made in sprint planning.
  • Roles: product owner, scrum master, developers. Events: planning, daily scrum, review and retrospective.
  • Metrics: velocity (for team planning only), sprint goal success, burn-up.
  • Strengths: regular cadence, stakeholder feedback, shared focus, built-in reflection.
  • Weaknesses: rigid mid-sprint changes, ceremony overhead, and bad fit for unplanned work.

Kanban

  • A board with columns for each stage, WIP limits on each, and pull-based flow. No fixed iterations, roles or required meetings.
  • Metrics: cycle time, throughput, WIP and flow efficiency, using cumulative flow diagrams.
  • Strengths: handles interruptions, easy to adopt gradually, exposes bottlenecks.
  • Weaknesses: less built-in cadence and commitment, so reviews and planning must be added deliberately.

Choosing

SituationBetter fit
Product development with a roadmap, many dependencies on stakeholder feedbackScrum
Support, SRE, platform or maintenance with unpredictable arrivalKanban
New team needing structure and a rhythmScrum
Mature team with stable flow and small itemsKanban

Many teams use Scrumban: sprints or regular cadences with Kanban WIP limits and flow metrics.

Advice

Pick one, try it for several cycles, run retrospectives, and adapt. The framework should serve the team's delivery, not the other way around; avoid copying ceremonies that do not produce value.
