Compare Transformer architectures: Encoder-Only (BERT), Decoder-Only (GPT), and Encoder-Decoder (T5).

ML / AI engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Transformers · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Encoder-only models (BERT) read the whole input bidirectionally and are best for understanding tasks such as classification, retrieval embeddings and tagging. Decoder-only models (GPT) generate text left to right with causal attention and dominate general-purpose LLMs. Encoder-decoder models (T5) encode an input then generate an output, suiting translation and summarisation.

Why decoder-only dominates LLMs

  • One simple objective (predict the next token) scales to huge unlabelled data.
  • The same model handles any task through prompting, with no task-specific heads.
  • Efficient inference with a KV cache, and a uniform architecture that is easy to scale and parallelise.
  • Research found decoder-only models match or beat encoder-decoder at scale for generative and few-shot tasks.

When others still make sense

  • Encoder-only: small, fast models for classification, semantic search embeddings and cross-encoder reranking, where bidirectional context helps and generation is not needed.
  • Encoder-decoder: tasks with a clear input and output separation, with strong results in translation, speech recognition (Whisper) and fine-tuned summarisation, especially at modest model sizes.

Takeaway

Pick the architecture from the task: understanding versus generation, latency and size limits, and whether you can use a pre-trained checkpoint.
