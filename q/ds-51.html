How do you protect privacy when analysing user data or training models, covering anonymisation, k-anonymity and differential privacy?

Data scientist · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Privacy · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Minimise and pseudonymise data first, because simple anonymisation is easily reversed by linkage attacks. Use aggregation thresholds and k-anonymity for releases, differential privacy to bound what any single person's data can reveal through added calibrated noise, and federated or secure computation where raw data cannot move. Manage a privacy budget and document the trade-off with utility.

Explained in depth

Principles

Collect only what you need (data minimisation), keep it for a limited time, control access, and handle consent and legal bases (GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA). Privacy engineering combines policy, access controls and technical protections.

Why naive anonymisation fails

Removing direct identifiers (name, email) leaves quasi-identifiers (ZIP code, birth date, gender, device model, rare purchases). Linkage attacks (Sweeney identified a governor's health record via ZIP, birth date and sex; the Netflix prize dataset was re-identified using IMDb ratings) show that combinations can be unique. High-dimensional behavioural data are especially re-identifiable.

Differential privacy

A randomised mechanism M is epsilon-differentially private if for any two datasets differing in one person, the probability of any output changes by at most a factor of e^epsilon (plus delta in the approximate version). Smaller epsilon means stronger privacy and more noise. A simple mechanism: the Laplace mechanism adds noise with scale = sensitivity / epsilon to a query result (for a count, sensitivity is 1).

import numpy as np
def dp_count(true_count, epsilon):
    return true_count + np.random.laplace(0, 1.0 / epsilon)      # sensitivity of a count is 1

Key ideas: sensitivity (how much one person can change the output, so clip contributions), composition (every query spends budget; total epsilon accumulates), post-processing immunity, and local vs central DP (noise at the user's device versus at a trusted curator). For ML use DP-SGD: clip per-example gradients and add Gaussian noise (Opacus, TensorFlow Privacy), tracking the budget with an accountant. Practical epsilon values range from below 1 (strong) to 8 or more (weak but common in production); the choice is a policy decision with a utility trade-off.

Model privacy risks

Models can leak: membership inference (was this record in training?), training data extraction from large language models, and model inversion. Defences: DP training, regularisation and less overfitting, deduplicating training data, output filtering, limiting query access, and auditing with attack simulations.

Practice

Perform a data protection impact assessment; separate identifiers from analytics data; use role-based access, audit logs and encryption; apply aggregate-only dashboards with minimum cohort sizes; test re-identification risk before releasing datasets; honour deletion requests (machine unlearning, retraining schedule).

Pitfalls

Believing hashing emails is anonymisation, releasing many overlapping aggregates without budget accounting, choosing epsilon without understanding utility, and treating synthetic data as automatically safe.
