How do you prepare an engineering department for its first SOC 2 Type II compliance audit?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Organization · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Prepare for a first SOC 2 Type II audit by scoping the systems and Trust Services Criteria, running a readiness assessment, writing and implementing policies and controls, collecting evidence continuously across the observation window (typically 3 to 12 months), automating where possible with a compliance platform, and engaging an auditor early. Type II tests that controls operated effectively over time.

Explained in depth

What SOC 2 is

A report by an independent CPA firm on controls relating to the AICPA Trust Services Criteria: Security (mandatory), plus optional Availability, Confidentiality, Processing Integrity and Privacy. Type I looks at control design at a point in time; Type II tests that controls operated effectively over a period (often 3 to 12 months). Customers frequently ask for Type II.

Preparation roadmap

  1. Scope: choose the in-scope products, systems, data and criteria based on customer needs. Smaller scope is easier for a first audit.
  2. Gap assessment: compare current practice with the criteria (a readiness review by a consultant or a compliance tool).
  3. Define policies: information security, access control, change management, incident response, vendor management, business continuity, data retention, secure development, onboarding and offboarding. Keep them practical and approved by leadership.
  4. Implement controls, typically including:
  • Single sign-on, MFA and least-privilege access, with periodic access reviews.
  • Change management: pull requests with review, CI checks, approvals and traceability to tickets.
  • Logging, monitoring, alerting and incident response with documented drills and postmortems.
  • Vulnerability management and patching SLAs, penetration tests, dependency scanning.
  • Encryption at rest and in transit, secrets management, backups and restore tests, and a disaster recovery plan.
  • Employee background checks, security training, device management and vendor risk reviews.
  1. Collect evidence continuously: screenshots, exports, tickets and logs showing that controls ran throughout the window. Compliance automation platforms (Vanta, Drata, Secureframe) integrate with cloud and tools to help.
  2. Engage an auditor early, agree the observation window and sample sizes, and run a mock audit.

Making it sustainable

Assign control owners, automate checks, review monthly, and treat exceptions as findings to fix. Build controls into engineering workflows so compliance is a by-product of good practice, not a once-a-year scramble.
