How do you manage tech stack consolidation following a corporate merger or acquisition?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Strategy · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

After a merger or acquisition, consolidate the tech stack in stages: inventory systems and costs, decide the target state with explicit criteria (customer value, risk, cost, capability, team skills), sequence work by benefit and dependency, keep customers and critical operations stable, integrate teams and culture, and communicate openly. Not everything must be merged; some systems should coexist or be retired.

Explained in depth

1. Discover and inventory

Catalogue applications, infrastructure, data stores, cloud accounts, vendors, licences, security posture, technical debt, ownership and costs on both sides. Identify critical customer-facing systems, compliance obligations and key people (knowledge holders).

2. Define the integration strategy

Align with the business reasons for the deal (cost savings, cross-selling, new capability). For each domain choose among:

  • Consolidate into one system.
  • Coexist with integration (APIs, a shared identity layer, a data platform) for now.
  • Retire a duplicate.
  • Adopt the better of the two (or a third).

Make decisions with explicit criteria: customer impact, functional fit, scalability, security, total cost, migration effort and team capability, and write them down (ADRs). Beware of defaulting to the acquirer's stack out of pride; also resist keeping everything.

3. Sequence the work

  • Quick, low-risk wins first: shared identity and access, email, collaboration tools, security baselines, finance and HR systems, cloud cost consolidation.
  • Then data integration and customer-facing platforms, with migration plans, parallel runs, rollback paths and careful cutovers.
  • Protect revenue-critical systems; freeze risky changes around cutovers.
  • Track progress and savings against the business case.

4. Security and compliance

Run security assessments of the acquired environment early, enforce minimum controls, and align policies, audits (SOC 2, ISO 27001) and data residency obligations.

5. People and culture

Clarify the organisation design, roles and leadership early. Build joint teams around the target architecture, recognise skills on both sides, run knowledge-transfer programmes, and communicate often and honestly; unclear plans cause attrition among the people you most need.

Pitfalls

Underestimating the effort, big-bang migrations, neglecting integration of data, losing key engineers, and a long period of two parallel stacks that never ends.
