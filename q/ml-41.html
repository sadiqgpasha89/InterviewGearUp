What are the pre-training objectives for Large Language Models: Next-Token Prediction vs Masked Language Modeling?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · LLMs · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Next-token prediction (causal language modelling) trains the model to predict each token from the ones before it, which matches text generation and gives a training signal at every position. Masked language modelling hides about 15 percent of tokens and predicts them from both sides, giving bidirectional representations but a weaker signal and a mismatch with generation.

Explained in depth

Next-token prediction (autoregressive)

The objective is to maximise sum over t of log P(x_t | x_1 ... x_{t-1}), trained with cross-entropy over the vocabulary.

  • A causal attention mask prevents looking ahead, so all positions of a sequence are trained in parallel (teacher forcing).
  • Every token provides a gradient, so it is data efficient.
  • The model learns exactly the task it performs at inference, which makes sampling, chat and prompting natural.
  • Used by GPT, LLaMA, Claude-style models and most modern LLMs.

Masked language modelling (MLM)

Randomly replace about 15 percent of tokens with a [MASK] token (or a random or unchanged token), then predict the originals using bidirectional context (BERT).

  • Representations use left and right context, which is excellent for understanding tasks, classification and embeddings.
  • Only the masked tokens (15 percent) produce loss per sequence, so it is less sample efficient.
  • [MASK] never appears at fine-tuning or inference time (a mismatch), and the model is not designed for free text generation.

Other objectives

  • Span corruption (T5): mask contiguous spans and generate them.
  • Replaced token detection (ELECTRA): decide which tokens were replaced by a small generator; efficient since every position gives signal.
  • Next sentence prediction (BERT, later found unnecessary).

Metrics

The cross-entropy loss on held-out text converts to perplexity = exp(loss), a measure of how surprised the model is by the text.

Why it matters

The objective decides what the model is good at: MLM for understanding encoders, next-token prediction for general generative assistants, which are then aligned with instruction tuning and preference training.
