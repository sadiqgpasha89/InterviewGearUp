A stakeholder wants an agent to auto-approve 80% of health and motor claims within 5 minutes with no human involvement. How do you respond?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Agents · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Treat 80% as a hypothesis, not a requirement. Explain the risks (regulatory expectations, fraud and leakage, errors at scale), then propose risk tiers: straight-through settlement only for low-value, simple, well-evidenced claims under strict rules; assisted one-click approval for the middle; full review above limits or on fraud signals. Agree measurable acceptance criteria and expand as evidence grows.

Explained in depth

Challenge the premise constructively

  • Find the goal behind the number: faster settlement, lower handling cost, better customer experience. Those can be reached without full automation of every claim type.
  • Check the data: what share of claims today are simple, low-value and clean? That sets a realistic automation ceiling.

Risks to name

  • Regulation and conduct: regulators expect fair handling, explanations and accountable decisions; health claims involve sensitive data and medical judgement.
  • Fraud and leakage: an automated path is attractive to fraud rings and vulnerable to manipulated documents.
  • Errors at scale: a mistake repeats across thousands of claims before anyone notices.

Tiered design

TierExample criteriaHandling
Straight-throughGlass or tyre, below a small limit, policy active for a minimum period, clear evidence, no third party, low fraud scoreRules-based approval and payment, sampled audits
AssistedMid-value, standard documentsAgent prepares extraction, checks and a recommendation; adjuster approves in one click
Full reviewAbove limits, injuries, fraud signals, exclusions, conflicting factsAdjuster investigation

Thresholds are set with claims, fraud and compliance teams, and tightened or relaxed based on audit results.

Acceptance criteria

  • Payout calculation matches the rules engine on 100% of test cases.
  • Zero auto-approvals on a test suite of known exclusions and fraud patterns.
  • 100% of low-confidence, ambiguous or adversarial inputs route to people without errors.
  • Groundedness of explanations above an agreed rate on a golden set.
  • Post-launch: audit sample error rate, leakage, complaint rate and time to settle per tier.

Roll out

  • Shadow mode first (the agent decides, people still process), compare decisions, then enable straight-through for the safest tier and expand with evidence.

Likely follow-ups

  • How would you set the limit for straight-through processing?
  • What would you audit after launch?
  • How do you explain to the stakeholder why 80% is not the starting target?
