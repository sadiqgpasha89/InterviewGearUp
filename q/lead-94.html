How do you build an engineering employer brand and technical blog that attracts top engineering talent?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Hiring · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Build an engineering employer brand by showing real work and culture: publish honest technical blog posts by engineers on problems solved and lessons learned, speak at conferences, contribute to open source, keep a clear careers page, run a great candidate experience, and let employees tell their own stories. Measure applications, offer acceptance and referral rates, and be consistent with how people are treated.

Explained in depth

What an employer brand is

Your engineering employer brand is what engineers think about working for you: technical challenges, culture, growth, compensation, and how you treat people. It is shaped by employees' stories, candidate experiences, reviews (Glassdoor, Blind), open-source presence, and public content. A strong brand reduces time and cost to hire, improves offer acceptance and attracts candidates who fit the culture. Authenticity matters more than polish, because experienced engineers will ask current employees and check reviews.

Foundations: be worth joining

Brand content cannot cover for a poor reality. Fix fundamentals first: fair pay and leveling, growth and mentoring, reasonable on-call and workload, modern tooling and practices, psychological safety, and inclusive hiring. Define your employee value proposition (EVP): what makes the company distinctive (hard problems at scale, impact, autonomy, remote flexibility, learning budget, mission) with evidence.

Technical blog and content programme

  1. Set goals and audience: attract engineers of the target profiles (backend, SRE, ML) and build credibility. Choose a platform (company engineering blog on your domain, with RSS and good SEO, plus Medium or dev.to syndication), and a lightweight editorial process: a few editors and reviewers, a style guide, legal and security review (no confidential details), publishing cadence (for example one or two posts per month).
  2. Content types that resonate:
  • Deep dives on real problems: migration stories, scaling lessons, performance investigations, architecture decisions with trade-offs, incident postmortems (sanitised), database or search design.
  • Engineering practices and culture: how we do code review, on-call, RFCs, hiring, mentoring, remote work.
  • Open-source releases and tools, with documentation.
  • Technical talks and videos, conference recaps, podcast appearances, meetups hosted at your office or online.
  • Career stories and "day in the life", interns' experiences, team spotlights, diversity and inclusion efforts (with substance), and honest failure stories.
  • Include code, diagrams and metrics. Avoid marketing speak.
  1. Make it sustainable: encourage and recognise authors (count writing in performance and promotion criteria "influence beyond your team"), give time and editing support (a technical editor or comms partner), maintain a backlog of ideas from design docs and postmortems, run writing workshops, and reuse internal RFCs as content. Celebrate publications internally, track results and share them.
  2. Distribution: share via company social channels, employees' networks, newsletters, Hacker News and Reddit communities when appropriate, conference talks, and recruiter outreach emails linking to relevant posts. Optimise the careers page and job descriptions with clear expectations, salary ranges where appropriate, tech stack, interview process, and links to content.

Other brand levers

  • Open source and community: contribute to projects you use, sponsor events, host meetups and hackathons, offer scholarships and mentorship programmes, and maintain a GitHub organisation with quality READMEs.
  • Employee advocacy: encourage (not force) employees to share their work, speak at conferences and talk about the company, with guidelines and support.
  • Candidate experience: a respectful, well-organised, transparent interview process with timely feedback, structured interviews, and clear communication; send rejected candidates useful feedback. Candidates become advocates (or critics).
  • Employer reputation management: monitor Glassdoor, Blind and social media, respond professionally, and address recurring issues.
  • Diversity and inclusion: demonstrate real commitment through representation in content, inclusive language, programmes and outcomes.
  • Alumni and referrals: keep alumni networks warm, since former employees often refer strong candidates.

Measure

Careers page traffic and blog readership, applicant volume and quality, source-of-hire breakdown, referral rate, offer acceptance rate, time to hire, cost per hire, candidate NPS, Glassdoor ratings, social engagement, number of speaking slots and open-source contributions, and new-hire survey ("what attracted you?").

Pitfalls

Marketing fluff that engineers dismiss, content without engineering involvement, inconsistent posting, ignoring negative reviews, promising what the culture does not deliver, and leaking sensitive details.
