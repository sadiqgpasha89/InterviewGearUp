How do you structure a quality organisation: embedded SDETs, a central QA team, or developers owning testing?

QA / SDET · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Leadership · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Most scaling organisations succeed with developers owning quality of their code, SDETs embedded in or aligned to product teams for strategy and automation, and a small central quality platform or enablement group for shared tooling, standards and coaching. Choose by product complexity, risk and team maturity, and avoid a separate gate-keeping QA phase.

Explained in depth

Models

ModelStrengthsRisks
Central QA team that tests features after developmentSpecialist skills, consistent process, pooled resourcesHand-offs, a bottleneck before release, "quality is QA's job" thinking, late feedback
Embedded QA or SDETs in each product teamEarly involvement, shared goals, fast feedback, domain knowledgeInconsistent practices, isolation of testers, uneven skills
Developers own testing (no dedicated testers)Strong ownership, fastest feedback, lowest hand-off costBlind spots in test design and exploratory skill, risk of neglecting non-functional and cross-system quality
Hybrid: embedded plus enablement platformLocal ownership plus shared tools and standardsNeeds clear roles, good communication

A common effective setup

  • Developers write and maintain unit, integration and component tests, and are responsible for fixing defects and the quality of their changes.
  • SDETs or quality engineers are embedded (often one per one to three teams): shape test strategy, build and review automation, run risk workshops, do exploratory and cross-feature testing, and coach developers.
  • A quality platform or enablement team provides the test framework, CI integration, test data and environment tooling, flaky-test management, dashboards, training, and guidelines (a guild or community of practice connects everyone).
  • Specialists (performance, security, accessibility) available as a shared service.

Choosing

Consider product risk (regulated, safety, financial), release cadence, architecture complexity, maturity of engineering practices, and hiring market. Early-stage startups often rely on developers plus a few quality engineers; large enterprises with complex systems add platform and specialist capabilities.

Making it work

  • Define roles and expectations (who writes which tests, who decides release readiness).
  • Move QA into planning and design, not just execution.
  • Create career paths for quality engineers with technical depth and leadership tracks.
  • Review the structure yearly using outcomes: escaped defects, cycle time, team satisfaction.

Anti-patterns

A QA gate that must approve every release, testers with no code access or influence, developers throwing work over the wall, and measuring testers by bug counts.
