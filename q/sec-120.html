How do you build resilience against ransomware, from prevention to recovery?

Security engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Resilience · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Reduce compromise through patching, phishing-resistant MFA and least privilege, limit spread with segmentation and tiered admin accounts, detect early with EDR and monitoring, keep immutable isolated backups with tested restores, rehearse the incident and communication plan, and decide your payment stance in advance.

Explained in depth

Understand the threat

Modern groups steal data first (double extortion), disable backups and security tools, move laterally with stolen credentials, and encrypt at scale, often at night or on holidays. Common entry points: phishing, exposed remote access (RDP, VPN) without MFA, unpatched edge devices, stolen credentials, and compromised vendors.

Prevention

  • Patch and harden internet-facing systems quickly; remove unused exposure; require phishing-resistant MFA on VPN, email, admin and remote access.
  • Email and web filtering, macro and script restrictions, application control, and EDR on all endpoints and servers.
  • Least privilege and tiered administration: no domain-admin logins on workstations, separate admin accounts, LAPS for local admin passwords, and privileged access management.

Limit blast radius and detect early

  • Network segmentation, restricted lateral movement (SMB, RDP, WinRM), and egress filtering.
  • Monitoring for ransomware precursors: credential dumping, unusual admin tool use, mass file renames, disabled backups or antivirus, large outbound transfers. A 24 by 7 detection and response capability (in-house or managed).

Backups and recovery capability

  • Follow 3-2-1-1-0: three copies, two media, one offsite, one immutable or offline, zero errors after verification.
  • Immutable storage (object lock, WORM), backups in a separate account or security domain with separate credentials, and protected backup consoles.
  • Test restores regularly and measure actual recovery time; know the order of recovery (identity, networking, critical applications) and keep golden images and infrastructure as code to rebuild clean systems.
  • Document dependencies and maintain offline copies of runbooks, contacts and the incident plan.

Incident response plan

  • Roles (incident commander, technical lead, legal, communications, executives), outside help on retainer (IR firm, counsel, insurer), and regulator and customer notification obligations.
  • First hour: isolate affected systems (network containment, not powering off if forensics matter), disable compromised accounts, preserve evidence, and activate the plan; avoid reconnecting until the intrusion is understood and credentials reset (including KRBTGT where relevant).
  • Tabletop exercises with executives, including the payment decision (legal, sanctions and operational considerations, no guarantee of recovery), made in advance.

Measure

Time to detect, percentage of systems with EDR, backup restore test success and duration, MFA coverage, and time to patch critical edge vulnerabilities.
