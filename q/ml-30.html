Compare Batch Normalization and Layer Normalization: Why does LayerNorm dominate in NLP?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Deep Learning · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Batch Normalisation normalises each feature across the batch dimension, so it depends on batch statistics and needs running averages at inference. Layer Normalisation normalises across the features of each single example, independent of batch size and sequence length. LayerNorm dominates in NLP because sequences vary in length, batches are small or distributed, and it behaves identically in training and inference.

Explained in depth

What each computes

Given activations shaped (batch, features):

  • BatchNorm computes the mean and variance of each feature over the batch (and spatial positions for images), normalises, then applies learnable scale and shift.
  • LayerNorm computes the mean and variance over the features of each example (for transformers, the hidden dimension at each token), then scale and shift.

y = gamma * (x - mean) / sqrt(var + eps) + beta

Why NLP prefers LayerNorm

  • Sequence lengths and padding vary, so batch statistics are noisy and mixed across token positions.
  • Large language model training uses tiny per-device batches, or none, making batch statistics unreliable.
  • No running statistics, so there is no train and inference mismatch and no extra synchronisation across devices.

Variants

  • Pre-norm (normalise before each sublayer) trains more stably for deep transformers than the original post-norm.
  • RMSNorm drops mean subtraction and the shift, normalising only by the root mean square; it is cheaper and used in many recent LLMs.
  • GroupNorm and InstanceNorm serve small-batch vision tasks.
