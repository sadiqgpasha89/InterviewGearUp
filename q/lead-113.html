Tell me about a time you took initiative without being asked.

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 1 (Junior) · Behavioral · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Describe noticing a problem or opportunity nobody owned, how you checked it mattered, proposed or started a small solution with your manager's awareness, delivered it, and measured the benefit. Show ownership, good judgement about scope, and that you brought others along rather than going rogue.

Explained in depth

What it shows

Ownership and proactivity: you improve things beyond your assigned tasks, with judgement about impact and scope.

Story outline

  1. Notice: a recurring pain, such as flaky tests, manual deploy steps, missing documentation or repeated customer questions.
  2. Validate: gather a quick fact or number ("this takes 30 minutes each release", "5 bug reports from this cause").
  3. Align: tell your manager or team, and agree on a small, time-boxed attempt so it does not hurt your main work.
  4. Act: build the script, document the process, fix the root cause, or run a short experiment.
  5. Share and sustain: demo it, document how to use it, and invite feedback.
  6. Result: time saved, fewer incidents, adoption by others.

Example

"I noticed our release checklist took about an hour of manual steps and caused mistakes. I proposed automating the repeated ones, my lead agreed to a day of effort, and I wrote a script plus a short README. Releases dropped to ten minutes, and a teammate extended the script with two more steps."

Tips

  • Show you kept your regular commitments.
  • Quantify the result.
  • Avoid stories where you ignored others or built something no one wanted.
  • If it partly failed, say what you learned.

Likely follow-ups

  • How did you decide it was worth your time?
  • Did you tell your manager before you started?
  • What happened when it did not go as planned?
