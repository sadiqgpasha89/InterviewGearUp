Describe a time when the requirements or plan changed late in a project. How did you adapt?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 1 (Junior) · Behavioral · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Describe staying calm, understanding why the change happened, assessing its effect on scope, time and risk, re-planning with the team and stakeholders, protecting what mattered most, and communicating the new plan clearly. Show flexibility without silently absorbing unlimited extra work, and say what you learned about handling change.

Explained in depth

What they look for

Flexibility with judgement: you respond constructively to change while being honest about trade-offs. Neither rigid resistance nor cheerful overcommitment impresses.

Structure

  1. Situation: the project, the stage it had reached, and what changed (a customer need, a new legal rule, a dropped dependency).
  2. Understand: ask why, who decided, and what success now looks like.
  3. Assess impact: list what is affected (scope, schedule, tests, other teams) and estimate honestly.
  4. Re-plan with options: for example keep the date and cut scope, keep scope and move the date, or add people only if it truly helps.
  5. Decide and communicate: the stakeholder chooses with the trade-offs in front of them; write the decision down and tell the team.
  6. Result and learning: delivery outcome, plus a habit such as earlier demos or a change log.

Example

"Two weeks before launch, a customer asked for single sign-on. I asked what exactly they needed, and it was only for one pilot team. I estimated a week for a basic version, and we offered either that with a one-week delay or SSO after launch. The product owner chose the first. We split the work, tested SSO early, and launched a week later with no incident. We also started demoing weekly, so such needs surfaced sooner."

Tips

  • Show a calm tone and facts, not complaints.
  • Make clear you raised trade-offs rather than quietly working late.
  • If the change was a bad idea, show how you disagreed respectfully and then committed.

Likely follow-ups

  • How did you decide what to cut or move?
  • How do you stop changing requirements from becoming a habit?
  • What if you disagreed with the change?
