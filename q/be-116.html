How would you lead a migration from a single large PostgreSQL database to a sharded architecture?

Backend engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Databases · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

First exhaust simpler options such as indexing, read replicas, partitioning and archiving. If sharding is still required, choose a shard key that matches access patterns, migrate in phases with dual writes and verification, route through a thin data-access layer or proxy, move one tenant or range at a time with rollback, and plan for cross-shard queries, IDs and operations.

Explained in depth

Step 0: be sure you need it

Sharding adds permanent complexity. Before it, try: query and index tuning, caching, read replicas, connection pooling, table partitioning, archiving old data, bigger hardware, and splitting by service (vertical partitioning). Measure which limit you will hit (write throughput, storage size, hot tables) and when.

Choose the sharding approach

  • Shard key: should appear in most queries, spread load evenly, and keep related data together. For SaaS, tenant ID is ideal (all of a tenant's data on one shard, so joins and transactions stay local). Bad keys: low-cardinality, monotonically increasing (hot shard), or something rarely used in queries.
  • Mapping: hash-based (even, hard to rebalance), range-based (simple, risk of hotspots) or directory-based lookup (flexible: a table maps tenant to shard, allowing moves).
  • Consider managed or extension options (Citus, Vitess for MySQL, CockroachDB, Aurora Limitless) instead of building your own.

Prepare the application

  • Add the shard key to every table and query; remove cross-tenant joins and foreign keys across shards.
  • Replace auto-increment IDs with globally unique IDs (UUIDv7, Snowflake-style).
  • Introduce a routing layer (library or proxy) that maps key to shard, with connection pools per shard.
  • Plan for cross-shard needs: scatter-gather for rare queries, a separate analytics store (warehouse) for reporting, and sagas instead of distributed transactions.

Migration plan

  1. Build the new shards and routing, test in staging with production-like data.
  2. Copy data using logical replication or CDC (change data capture) while the old database keeps serving.
  3. Dual write or replicate, then verify with checksums and row counts, and shadow-read comparisons.
  4. Cut over per tenant or range with a feature flag, watching errors and latency; keep the ability to route back.
  5. Decommission old data after a safe period.

Operations

Schema changes across all shards with automation, backups and point-in-time recovery per shard, monitoring per shard, a rebalancing process (move tenants between shards), and capacity planning. Communicate the plan and risks to the business, and run migration rehearsals and a rollback drill before the final cutover.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you choose a shard key and what makes a bad one?
  • How do you handle queries and transactions that span shards?
  • How do you rebalance data when one shard becomes too large?
