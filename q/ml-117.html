Defaults are under 2% of accounts. How do you keep a risk model useful instead of predicting 'no default' for everyone?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Metrics · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Optimise and report metrics that respect rarity (PR-AUC, recall at a fixed review capacity, expected loss captured), not accuracy. Use class weights or a focal-style loss before resampling, keep the test set at the true base rate, calibrate probabilities afterwards (weighting and SMOTE distort them), and choose the decision threshold from the cost of each error and team capacity.

Explained in depth

Metrics

  • Accuracy is useless here: predicting no default for everyone scores 98 percent.
  • Use PR-AUC (precision-recall), precision and recall at k where k is the number of accounts the team can review, and dollar-weighted recall (share of at-risk money captured). ROC-AUC can look good while precision in the top band is poor.

Training techniques, in order of preference

  1. Class weights (scale_pos_weight in XGBoost or LightGBM, class_weight in scikit-learn): simple and keeps all real data.
  2. Focal or cost-sensitive loss for neural models, which concentrates on hard examples.
  3. Resampling: undersampling the majority class speeds training; SMOTE creates synthetic minority points but can blur boundaries on mixed categorical data and must be applied only inside the training folds.
  • Never resample the validation or test set: they must reflect the real base rate.

Calibrate after rebalancing

  • Weighting and resampling inflate predicted probabilities. Fix them with isotonic regression or Platt scaling on a held-out set at the true rate, and check a reliability curve.
  • Calibrated probabilities let finance compute expected loss (probability x exposure) and rank by money at risk.

Choose the threshold deliberately

  • Set it from the cost matrix (cost of a missed default versus cost of an unnecessary contact) or simply from capacity: the top 200 accounts per week.
  • Revisit it when the base rate shifts, for example in a downturn.

Other options

  • Anomaly detection (isolation forest, autoencoders) helps when labels are very scarce or new fraud-like patterns appear, but usually as an extra signal rather than the main model.
  • More positives beat any trick: lengthen the history, use earlier-stage events (30 days past due) as additional labels.
