Compare model rollout strategies: Shadow deployment, Canary deployment, and A/B testing in production.

ML / AI engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · MLOps · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Shadow deployment sends copies of live traffic to a new model whose predictions are logged but not returned to users, so it is risk-free but measures only agreement and system behaviour. A canary routes a small share of real traffic to the new model and expands gradually while watching metrics. An A/B test splits users into randomised groups to measure the causal effect on business outcomes.

Explained in depth

Why staged rollout

Offline metrics do not guarantee production behaviour: data differs, latency and resource usage change, feedback loops appear, and user impact is uncertain. Rollout strategies reduce risk and answer different questions.

Shadow deployment (dark launch)

  • How: the router duplicates each production request to the candidate model (asynchronously); the champion's response is returned to the user, while the candidate's output is logged for comparison.
  • Learns: system health (latency, memory, errors, throughput under real traffic), prediction distributions, agreement and disagreement with the champion, offline evaluation once labels arrive, feature pipeline correctness (train-serve skew).
  • Risk: none to users (only extra compute cost, and side effects must be disabled).
  • Limits: cannot measure user behaviour impact (clicks, conversion) because users never see its outputs; in interactive systems the model's decisions would change future data (feedback loop), which shadow cannot capture.
  • Best for: first validation of a new model or infrastructure, high-risk domains (credit, fraud), and migrations.

Canary deployment

  • How: route a small percentage (1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100 percent) of live traffic to the new version; increase if guardrail metrics stay healthy; otherwise roll back.
  • Learns: real-world technical and quality signals with limited blast radius: error rate, latency percentiles, model score distributions, downstream business guardrails, user complaints.
  • Selection: random by request, or by user hash for consistency, region or tenant. Automated analysis (Argo Rollouts, Flagger, Istio, SageMaker deployment guardrails, Seldon) compares canary against baseline with statistical checks and auto-rollback on SLO violations.
  • Not designed for causal inference of long-term metrics; groups are small and not always randomised.

A/B testing (online controlled experiment)

  • How: randomly assign users (or sessions or entities) to control (A, current model) and treatment (B, new model) for a planned duration and sample size; compare primary business metrics (conversion, revenue per user, retention, fraud loss) plus guardrails.
  • Learns: causal effect of the model on outcomes with statistical significance.
  • Needs: sound experiment design (hypothesis, minimum detectable effect, power analysis, sample size, duration covering weekly cycles), consistent assignment, sample ratio mismatch (SRM) checks, no peeking-driven false positives (use sequential testing or fixed horizon), correction for multiple metrics, novelty effects, interference between users (marketplaces, social networks: use cluster or switchback designs).
  • Variants: multi-armed bandits for adaptive allocation, interleaving experiments for ranking, holdout groups for long-term effects.

Typical sequence

Offline evaluation → shadow → canary with automated guardrails → A/B test to prove benefit → full rollout with a holdout and rollback plan. Keep the old model warm, version everything (model, features, config), use feature flags, monitor drift after launch, and document decisions.

Pitfalls

Skipping shadow for infrastructure changes, canary with too little traffic to detect issues, ending A/B tests early on a good-looking day, contamination between groups, and neglecting segment effects (overall flat but negative for a key cohort).

Likely follow-ups

  • When is shadow mode not enough to validate a model?
  • How do you guard against sample ratio mismatch and peeking in A/B tests?
  • How do you roll back automatically if a canary degrades?
