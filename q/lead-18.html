How do you balance paying down technical debt with executive demands for rapid product feature delivery?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 3 (Senior) · Strategy · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Make technical debt visible in business terms (slower delivery, incidents, onboarding time, risk), prioritise only the debt that blocks the roadmap or causes real pain, and reserve steady capacity for it, combined with fixing what you touch. Present trade-offs and cost of delay to executives instead of asking for a rewrite sprint.

Explained in depth

1. Make it concrete

'The code is messy' does not persuade. Quantify the cost: time lost per sprint to workarounds, incident counts and hours, change failure rate, cycle time for changes in that area, onboarding time, and the number of upcoming roadmap items that touch it. Keep a visible debt register with the impact and estimated effort for each item.

2. Prioritise by impact

Fix debt that (a) sits on the path of planned features, (b) causes recurring incidents or security risk, or (c) slows many engineers. Leave harmless, rarely touched ugliness alone. Distinguish prudent, deliberate debt taken to hit a date from careless debt.

3. Pay it down in sustainable ways

  • Reserve a steady share of capacity (often 15 to 20%) for maintenance and debt, agreed with product, instead of begging for it each quarter.
  • Fix as you go: leave code a little better when changing it, and size features to include the necessary refactor.
  • Use targeted, time-boxed hardening work for larger items, with measurable outcomes.
  • Avoid full rewrites unless the case is overwhelming; incremental replacement (such as the strangler approach) is less risky.

4. Talk to executives in their terms

Show options: 'Ship feature X in 6 weeks with the current design, or in 4 weeks after 2 weeks of cleanup, and every later feature in this area is 30% faster.' Explain the cost of delay and the risk of not acting, such as outage probability or compliance exposure. Keep promises small, report results with numbers, and celebrate wins to build trust.

Pitfalls

Hiding debt creation, treating all debt as equal, or promising benefits you cannot measure. Revisit the register regularly as the roadmap changes.

Likely follow-ups

  • How would you quantify the cost of a specific piece of debt?
  • What do you do if the business refuses any capacity for debt work?
  • When is a rewrite justified instead of incremental refactoring?
