Explain the difference between Defect Severity and Defect Priority with illustrative examples.

QA / SDET · Gear 1 (Junior) · Defects · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Severity measures how badly a defect affects the system (technical impact), while priority says how soon it should be fixed (business urgency). They are independent: a crash in a rarely used admin screen is high severity but low priority, and a misspelled company name on the home page is low severity but high priority.

Explained in depth

Definitions

  • Severity: the degree of damage the defect causes. It is usually set by QA based on technical impact, with levels such as critical, major, minor and trivial.
  • Priority: the order in which defects should be fixed. It is set by the product owner or triage group based on business impact, deadlines and customer exposure.

Examples

DefectSeverityPriority
Payment page crashes for every customerHighHigh
Application crashes when an obscure legacy setting is toggled that no customer usesHighLow
Company logo is wrong on the landing page before a launchLowHigh
Tooltip typo in an internal reportLowLow

Why both matter

Severity alone would send teams to fix technically serious but irrelevant bugs. Priority alone would ignore the risk of a hidden severe flaw. Triage uses both, plus frequency, workaround availability and the number of affected users.

Good practice

Define the levels in writing so everyone uses the same scale, review priorities when release plans change, and re-evaluate when new information appears (for example a rare bug turns out to hit a major customer).

Likely follow-ups

  • Who normally sets severity and who sets priority?
  • Give an example of high severity and high priority.
  • How do you handle a disagreement between QA and the product owner about priority?
