What are the assumptions of linear regression and how do you check and fix violations?

Data scientist · Gear 1 (Junior) · Modeling · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Linear regression assumes a linear relationship, independent errors, constant error variance (homoscedasticity), approximately normal residuals for inference, and little multicollinearity. Check residual plots, Q-Q plots, Durbin-Watson and variance inflation factors, then fix with transformations, additional terms, robust errors, regularisation or a different model.

Explained in depth

The model

Linear regression models y = b0 + b1 x1 + ... + bp xp + e and estimates coefficients by ordinary least squares (OLS), minimising squared residuals. For prediction the main requirement is good out-of-sample performance; for inference (confidence intervals, p-values, causal-ish interpretation) the assumptions matter much more.

Interpretation notes

  • A coefficient is the expected change in y per one-unit change in that predictor holding the others constant.
  • Log-transformed y: coefficient approximates a percentage change (b = 0.05 means about 5 percent). Log-log: elasticity.
  • Standardise predictors to compare effect sizes; centre variables for interpretable intercepts and interactions.
  • R-squared always rises with more predictors; use adjusted R-squared, AIC or cross-validated error.

Alternatives when assumptions fail

Regularised models (ridge, lasso, elastic net), generalised linear models (logistic, Poisson, negative binomial for counts), mixed-effects models, quantile regression, tree-based models and GAMs.

Pitfalls

Dropping variables because of VIF without considering the causal question, testing normality of the target instead of residuals, and treating a good R-squared as proof that assumptions hold.

Likely follow-ups
