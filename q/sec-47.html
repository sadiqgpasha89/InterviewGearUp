How do you audit and harden Kubernetes RBAC to prevent excessive ClusterRoleBinding permissions?

Security engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Kubernetes · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Audit RBAC by listing who holds powerful roles, especially cluster-admin and ClusterRoleBindings, then remove wildcard verbs and resources, replace cluster-wide bindings with namespaced RoleBindings, avoid permissions that allow privilege escalation (create pods, bind, escalate, impersonate, read secrets), use dedicated service accounts and review regularly with automated tools.

Explained in depth

Why it matters

In Kubernetes, authorisation is additive (no deny rules). Over-broad bindings, especially cluster-wide ones, are a common route to full cluster takeover after one pod or token is compromised.

Audit steps

kubectl get clusterrolebindings -o wide
kubectl auth can-i --list --as=system:serviceaccount:app:web -n app
kubectl get clusterrolebindings -o json | jq '.items[] | select(.roleRef.name=="cluster-admin") | .subjects'

Look for:

  • cluster-admin bound to users, groups or service accounts that should not have it, including system:authenticated or system:unauthenticated.
  • Wildcards (verbs: ["*"], resources: ["*"], apiGroups: ["*"]).
  • Risky verbs and resources: secrets (get, list, watch), pods/exec, create pods (can mount any service account or hostPath), escalate, bind, impersonate, modifying roles, rolebindings or admission webhooks, nodes/proxy.
  • Default service accounts in use, and tokens automatically mounted into pods that do not call the API.

Hardening

  1. Least privilege: grant specific verbs on specific resources, using Roles and RoleBindings scoped to a namespace rather than ClusterRoles bound cluster-wide.
  2. One service account per workload, automountServiceAccountToken: false unless needed, and short-lived bound tokens.
  3. Humans authenticate through OIDC single sign-on, mapped to groups; avoid shared admin credentials and long-lived client certificates. Use break-glass procedures with logging.
  4. Remove stale bindings, and review as part of change management (RBAC as code in Git).
  5. Enable audit logging and alert on use of powerful permissions.

Tools

kubectl-who-can, rbac-lookup, KubiScan, rakkess, Kubescape, kube-bench, and policy engines (Kyverno, Gatekeeper) to block dangerous roles.
