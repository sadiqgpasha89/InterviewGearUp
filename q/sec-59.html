Compare Red Team, Blue Team, and Purple Team security exercises in evaluating organizational resilience.

Security engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Operations · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Red teams emulate real adversaries to test an organisation's detection and response end to end, usually with stealth and defined objectives. Blue teams defend, monitor and respond. Purple teaming is a collaborative exercise where red and blue work together in real time, sharing techniques and detections to rapidly improve coverage, so the three differ in secrecy, goals and learning speed.

Explained in depth

Definitions

  • Red team: offensive specialists who simulate a realistic threat actor to achieve business-relevant objectives ("reach the crown jewel database", "obtain domain admin", "exfiltrate test data") using multiple vectors: external attacks, phishing and social engineering, physical access, supply chain, cloud and insider-like paths. Success is measured by whether objectives are achieved and whether the blue team detects and responds, not by the number of vulnerabilities.
  • Blue team: defenders responsible for prevention, detection, response and recovery: SOC, incident response, threat hunting, IT security engineering, system hardening, monitoring and logging.
  • Purple team: a collaboration function or exercise where red and blue teams work together openly (often in the same room) to execute specific attack techniques step by step, check whether each is blocked, logged or alerted, and immediately build or tune detections and controls.
  • White team (control cell): referees who set rules of engagement, manage safety, coordinate communications, and observe.

How exercises are run

Red team engagement

  1. Scoping and rules of engagement: objectives, targets, off-limits systems, allowed techniques, time windows, escalation contacts, "get out of jail" letter, emergency stop, legal approval, data handling.
  2. Threat modelling: choose an adversary profile from threat intelligence (ransomware affiliate, nation-state, insider) and map TTPs to MITRE ATT&CK.
  3. Execution across the kill chain: reconnaissance, initial access (phishing, exposed services, stolen credentials), execution and persistence, privilege escalation, credential access, lateral movement, collection and exfiltration, impact simulation. Tools: Cobalt Strike, Sliver, Mythic, Havoc, BloodHound, Impacket, custom implants, living-off-the-land.
  4. Reporting and debrief: timeline with detection points, which actions were seen or missed, root causes, prioritised fixes, replay sessions with defenders.

Purple team

  1. Pick a set of ATT&CK techniques relevant to threats (for example credential dumping, WMI persistence, Kerberoasting, cloud IAM abuse).
  2. Red executes each technique with a tool (Atomic Red Team, Caldera, Red Canary, VECTR for tracking); blue observes in SIEM and EDR: Did we prevent it? Did telemetry exist? Did an alert fire? Was it triaged correctly?
  3. Gaps become engineering tasks: enable logging, write Sigma rules, tune EDR policy, add detections, update playbooks; re-run the technique to verify.
  4. Record results in a coverage matrix (prevented, detected, logged only, missed), measure improvement.

Benefits and metrics

Realistic assessment of people, process and technology; validated detection coverage; faster tuning; improved collaboration and morale; board-level evidence. Metrics: time to detect and time to respond during the exercise, percentage of techniques detected or prevented, ATT&CK coverage trend, number of detections created and validated, dwell time before discovery, objective success rate, escalation quality.

Choosing the right exercise

  • Vulnerability assessment and penetration tests: breadth of technical weaknesses, shorter, not stealth-focused.
  • Red team: mature organisations with working detection capabilities wanting to test the whole programme.
  • Purple team: organisations building detection capability or wanting rapid improvement; often the best first step.
  • Tabletop exercises: leadership and process validation without technical attacks.
  • Breach and attack simulation (BAS) tools provide automated continuous validation.

A common cadence is continuous purple-style testing with an annual or semiannual red team operation.

Safety and governance

Rules of engagement, change freezes for critical periods, backup and rollback plans, use of test accounts and data where possible, no destructive actions without approval, secure handling of exfiltrated data, real incident handling if an actual attacker is discovered mid-test (deconfliction), and legal and HR guidance for social engineering and insider scenarios.

Pitfalls

Red team results delivered as a PDF with no engagement from defenders, running a red team before basic hygiene exists, tests that stop at the first success rather than testing detection, blue team hostility to findings, and no tracking of remediation.

Likely follow-ups

  • How does a red team engagement differ from a penetration test in scope, duration and objectives?
  • How do you measure whether a purple team exercise improved detection coverage?
  • What rules of engagement and safety controls protect production during adversary emulation?
