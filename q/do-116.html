How do you build a FinOps programme that cuts cloud cost without slowing engineering teams?

DevOps / SRE / Cloud · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · FinOps · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Make cost visible and owned: tag and allocate spend to teams, show it in dashboards and unit costs, set budgets and anomaly alerts, then optimise in order of impact: remove waste, rightsize, buy commitments such as savings plans and use spot where fit. Embed cost in engineering reviews and make each team accountable, with finance and engineering sharing a cadence.

Explained in depth

Phase 1: inform

  • Allocation: enforce tags or labels (team, service, environment, cost centre) with policy, use accounts or projects per workload, and handle shared costs with agreed split rules. Untagged spend is reported.
  • Visibility: dashboards per team and service (Cost Explorer, CUR in a warehouse, Kubecost or OpenCost for Kubernetes), and unit economics: cost per customer, per transaction, per 1000 requests. Total cost can grow healthily if unit cost falls.
  • Budgets and anomaly detection alert owners before month-end surprises.

Phase 2: optimise

In rough order of payoff:

  1. Eliminate waste: idle instances, unattached volumes and old snapshots, unused load balancers, forgotten environments (schedule non-production to shut down at night), over-retained logs.
  2. Rightsize: match instance sizes and storage tiers to actual utilisation (use utilisation data over weeks); use newer generations and ARM (Graviton).
  3. Pricing models: savings plans or reserved instances for the steady baseline (60 to 70 percent coverage is often prudent), spot for fault-tolerant jobs, and enterprise discounts negotiated centrally.
  4. Architecture: autoscaling, caching, compression, serverless where it fits, storage lifecycle policies, cutting cross-AZ and egress traffic, and efficient data formats.

Phase 3: operate

  • Regular FinOps review with engineering, finance and product; track savings opportunities as tickets with owners and value.
  • Showback or chargeback to create accountability.
  • Add cost to design reviews and to architecture decisions, and expose estimated cost in pull requests (Infracost).
  • Guardrails: budgets per account, approvals for large instance types, and automation of cleanups.

Culture

Avoid blame; celebrate savings; do not sacrifice reliability or developer speed for small gains. Engineering time is expensive, so prioritise by return. Report progress with metrics such as cost per unit, percent tagged, commitment coverage and waste percentage.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you measure cloud cost per unit of business value?
  • How do you decide between reserved commitments and flexibility?
  • How do you drive adoption of cost recommendations by busy teams?
