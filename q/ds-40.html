What feature selection methods do you use, and when is feature selection worth doing at all?

Data scientist · Gear 3 (Senior) · Feature selection · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Use filter methods like variance, correlation and mutual information for quick pruning, wrapper methods such as recursive feature elimination for small feature sets, and embedded methods like L1 regularisation or tree importance during training. Always select inside cross-validation to avoid leakage, and remember that for strong regularised models selection often matters less than good features.

Explained in depth

Why select features

Reduce overfitting and variance (especially when p is large relative to n), speed up training and serving, lower data collection and monitoring cost, improve interpretability, and remove leakage-prone or unstable features. It is not always needed: gradient boosting and regularised models cope with many features, and aggressive selection can discard useful interactions.

Method families

from sklearn.pipeline import Pipeline
from sklearn.feature_selection import SelectFromModel, VarianceThreshold
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.model_selection import cross_val_score
pipe = Pipeline([("var", VarianceThreshold(1e-4)),
                 ("sel", SelectFromModel(LogisticRegression(penalty="l1", solver="liblinear", C=0.2))),
                 ("clf", LogisticRegression(max_iter=2000))])
print(cross_val_score(pipe, X, y, cv=5, scoring="roc_auc").mean())

Critical rules

  • Do selection inside each cross-validation fold (use a pipeline). Selecting features with the full dataset's labels and then cross-validating gives wildly optimistic scores, a classic leak that is especially severe with thousands of features and few rows.
  • Correlated features: importance is split or arbitrarily assigned; L1 picks one of a group unpredictably. Cluster correlated features and keep a representative, or use elastic net, or evaluate grouped importance.
  • Stability: check whether selected features are consistent across bootstrap samples (stability selection); unstable selections signal noise.
  • Serving constraints: drop features that are costly, delayed, unavailable at inference time, or risky (protected attributes and proxies, subject to fairness policy).
  • Evaluate the final subset with a clean holdout and compare with the full-feature model; choose the smallest set within one standard error of the best.
  • For tree models, remove obvious noise but do not overinvest; for linear and distance-based models, selection and scaling matter more.

Process

  1. Remove unusable features (constant, mostly missing, leakage, unavailable at serving).
  2. Cluster or prune highly correlated groups.
  3. Fit a regularised or boosted model; examine permutation or SHAP importance on validation data.
  4. Try a reduced set and confirm that performance holds, with latency or cost savings.
  5. Monitor the retained features for drift.

Pitfalls

Selecting on the full dataset before splitting, trusting impurity importance, removing features because univariate scores are low even though they matter in interactions, and chasing minimal subsets at the cost of robustness.

Likely follow-ups

  • Why must feature selection happen inside the cross-validation loop rather than before it?
  • How do correlated features distort importance-based selection, and how do you handle them?
  • When would you prefer dimensionality reduction such as PCA to feature selection?
