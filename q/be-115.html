How would you design an audit logging system that is tamper-evident, searchable and compliant?

Backend engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Compliance · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Capture structured audit events (who, what, when, where, outcome) at the service boundary, write them append-only to a durable pipeline, protect them with hash chaining or signing and write-once storage, separate them from application logs and from the people they audit, index them for search, and apply retention, access control and privacy rules.

Explained in depth

What an audit event contains

Who (user or service identity, tenant), what (action and resource, such as invoice.delete, old and new values or a reference), when (timestamp from a trusted clock), where (IP, device, region, request ID), outcome (success, denied, error), and why when relevant (ticket or reason). Use a stable schema and event names. Do not log secrets, passwords, full card numbers or excess personal data; log identifiers and references.

Capture reliably

  • Emit events from the place that makes the decision (API layer or domain service) rather than relying on clients.
  • For changes in a database, write the audit event in the same transaction or use the transactional outbox or change data capture, so you never record an action that failed or lose one that succeeded.
  • Include security-relevant reads (access to sensitive records), admin actions, authentication events and permission changes.

Pipeline and storage

  1. Producers publish to a durable stream (Kafka, Kinesis) with retries.
  2. A consumer writes to append-only, write-once storage: object storage with Object Lock (WORM) retention, or a ledger database (Amazon QLDB, immudb).
  3. A separate search index (OpenSearch, Elasticsearch) supports investigations and reports, treated as a derived copy.

Tamper evidence

  • Hash chain: each record stores the hash of the previous one, so altering or deleting an entry breaks the chain. Periodically sign or publish the chain head (or a Merkle tree root) to an external system or notarisation service.
  • Separate duties: application admins and database admins cannot modify the audit store; the audit account has restricted write-only access, with access to read governed by roles and itself audited.
  • Synchronised time (NTP) and monitoring for gaps or sequence anomalies.

Compliance concerns

  • Retention by regulation (for example SOC 2, PCI DSS commonly one year or more online, HIPAA longer), with legal holds.
  • Privacy: minimise personal data, pseudonymise identifiers where possible, and design for erasure requests (for example crypto-shredding by encrypting personal fields with per-subject keys that can be deleted).
  • Provide export and reporting for auditors and customers, and test the whole chain regularly with restore and verification drills.
