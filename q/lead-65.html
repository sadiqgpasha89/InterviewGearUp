How do you accurately size sprint capacity while accounting for PTO, on-call duty, and maintenance?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Delivery · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Start from the team's available person-days in the sprint, subtract PTO, holidays, on-call duty, planned meetings and expected interrupts or maintenance, then apply a focus factor from historical data. Plan work up to that capacity, keep a buffer for the unexpected, and compare against actual completed work so the assumptions improve.

Explained in depth

A simple model

  1. Gross capacity: people times working days. A team of 6 over a 10 day sprint is 60 person-days.
  2. Subtract absences: public holidays, PTO, training, interviews. Say 8 person-days off, leaving 52.
  3. Subtract recurring commitments: on-call or support rotation (for example 50 percent of one engineer is 5 days), maintenance and bug-fix allowance, ceremonies and unplanned interruptions. Say 12 more days, leaving 40.
  4. Apply a focus factor (often 0.6 to 0.8 of the remaining time, from history) for context switching, reviews and meetings.
  5. The result is net capacity for planned feature work, for example 25 to 30 person-days.

Converting to commitments

  • In story points, use the average velocity of recent sprints, adjusted proportionally for absences (if 2 of 6 are out half the time, expect roughly 85 percent of the usual).
  • In items, use throughput.
  • Leave 10 to 20 percent slack for the unexpected, and rank work so that low-priority items drop first.

Considerations

  • New joiners start with reduced capacity for the first weeks and consume mentor time.
  • Reserve an explicit maintenance budget (for example 15 to 20 percent for bugs and tech debt) rather than treating it as invisible.
  • Account for dependencies and release or freeze dates.
  • Consider the on-call load: heavy incident weeks can consume the person entirely.

Improve over time

Compare planned capacity with completed work in retrospectives, track actual interrupt rate, adjust the focus factor, and share the model so product partners understand what a sprint can hold.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you account for a team member who is new and ramping up?
  • How should on-call and support load be reflected in planning?
  • Why shouldn't you plan to 100 percent of capacity?
