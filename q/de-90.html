Compare BigQuery slot reservations with on-demand pricing for high-volume enterprise workloads.

Data engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · FinOps · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

BigQuery on-demand charges per terabyte of data scanned with shared slot capacity, which is flexible and cheap for spiky or light use. Capacity pricing (Editions with slot reservations) charges for slots per hour regardless of bytes scanned, giving predictable cost, workload isolation and autoscaling for heavy, steady enterprise workloads. Choose by usage pattern and compare with real job history.

Explained in depth

Two pricing models

On-demand (analysis pricing): pay per TB processed (billed on bytes read, with a monthly free tier). Compute comes from a shared pool: each project gets up to about 2,000 concurrent slots on a best-effort basis, with no guarantee or isolation. Capacity pricing (BigQuery Editions: Standard, Enterprise, Enterprise Plus): pay for slots (virtual CPUs) per slot-hour, regardless of bytes scanned. You create reservations of slots, assign projects, folders or organisations to them via assignments, and queries use that capacity. Options: autoscaling (pay for slots in use up to a maximum), baseline slots (always provisioned, can use commitments for discounts: one or three years), and idle slot sharing across reservations.

Decision method

  1. Pull usage from INFORMATION_SCHEMA.JOBS_BY_ORGANIZATION and compute monthly bytes billed and slot-milliseconds consumed per project and hour.
SELECT DATE_TRUNC(creation_time, MONTH) m, SUM(total_bytes_billed)/POW(1024,4) AS tb_billed,
       SUM(total_slot_ms)/1000/3600 AS slot_hours
FROM `region-us`.INFORMATION_SCHEMA.JOBS_BY_PROJECT WHERE job_type = 'QUERY' GROUP BY m ORDER BY m;
  1. Estimate on-demand cost: TB × price per TB. Estimate capacity cost: needed average and peak slots × price per slot-hour × hours (use autoscale for peaks, baseline for steady load).
  2. Break-even: if sustained on-demand spend exceeds roughly the cost of the baseline slots that would handle your steady load, move to capacity. Typically organisations with tens of thousands of dollars per month of scan charges benefit.
  3. Consider performance and isolation: when dashboards slow down because ETL jobs hog shared slots, use separate reservations; the Editions model also provides features (BI Engine, governance, CMEK, multi-region) by edition.
  4. Mix: keep exploratory projects on on-demand with maximum_bytes_billed and custom quotas, while production workloads run on reservations. Slot-based billing also removes the penalty for scanning more data, but inefficient queries still compete for slots and cause queueing.

Operational tips

  • Start with autoscaling and a small baseline, observe INFORMATION_SCHEMA.RESERVATION_TIMELINE and slot utilisation, then add commitments for the stable portion.
  • Use workload management: separate reservations for ETL, BI and data science with priorities, and allow idle slot sharing.
  • Control cost under both models: partitioning and clustering, avoid SELECT *, materialised views, BI Engine, query result cache, dry runs, maximum_bytes_billed, per-user custom quotas, budgets and alerts.
  • Chargeback with labels and reservation assignments.

Pitfalls

Buying commitments before measuring, one reservation for everything, forgetting that storage is separate, under-provisioning causing queued queries at peak, and assuming slots make inefficient SQL free.
