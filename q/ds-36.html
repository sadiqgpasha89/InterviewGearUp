How do you interpret a black-box model with SHAP, permutation importance and partial dependence, and what are the pitfalls?

Data scientist · Gear 3 (Senior) · Model interpretability · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

SHAP attributes each prediction to features using Shapley values, giving consistent local explanations that aggregate to global importance. Permutation importance measures the drop in score when a feature is shuffled and partial dependence or ICE plots show average effects. All describe the model, not causality, and correlated features, extrapolation and leakage can mislead.

Explained in depth

Why interpret

Debugging (find leakage and bugs), trust and adoption by stakeholders, regulatory requirements (credit decisions, adverse-action reasons), fairness audits, feature selection, and learning about the domain. Distinguish global (what drives the model overall) from local (why this prediction).

SHAP in more detail

Shapley values distribute the difference between a prediction and the baseline (average) prediction among features according to their average marginal contribution over all feature orderings. Properties: local accuracy (contributions sum to the prediction), consistency, missingness. TreeSHAP computes exact values for tree ensembles efficiently; KernelSHAP and DeepSHAP handle other models.

import shap
expl = shap.TreeExplainer(model)
sv = expl(X_valid)                       # Explanation object
shap.plots.beeswarm(sv)                  # global: importance and direction by feature
shap.plots.waterfall(sv[0])              # local: why this row got its score
shap.plots.scatter(sv[:, "tenure"])      # dependence and interactions

Mean absolute SHAP gives a global ranking; the beeswarm shows the direction and spread of effects. For classification, SHAP values are usually in log-odds space.

Pitfalls

  • Explanations describe the model, not the world: a feature with large SHAP value is important to the model's prediction, not necessarily a cause. Do not give recommendations ("raise income to lower risk") from them.
  • Correlated features share credit arbitrarily (SHAP) or hide importance (permutation); group correlated features or use clustering and grouped importance.
  • Extrapolation: permuting a feature creates unrealistic combinations; conditional variants help.
  • Leakage shows up as a suspiciously dominant feature; explanations are a great audit.
  • Instability: explanations vary with background data, sampling and model retraining; test their stability.
  • Explanations on training data show overfitting patterns; use held-out data.
  • Misinterpreting additive attributions for interactions; use SHAP interaction values.
  • Sharing explanations with users can enable gaming or leak information.

Choosing between interpretable models and black box plus explanations

For high-stakes domains (lending, healthcare), consider inherently interpretable models (sparse logistic regression, generalised additive models, EBMs, scorecards, small trees) when accuracy loss is small; they offer exact, faithful explanations. Otherwise use a complex model with documented, validated explanations and monitoring.

Pitfalls

Quoting default importance from a tree library as truth, treating SHAP as causal, and presenting explanations without checking their stability.
