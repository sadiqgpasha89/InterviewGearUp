Compare activation functions: Sigmoid, Tanh, ReLU, Leaky ReLU, GELU, and Swish.

ML / AI engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Deep Learning · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Sigmoid and tanh squash values but saturate, causing vanishing gradients. ReLU is cheap and does not saturate for positive inputs, but neurons can die when stuck at zero. Leaky ReLU keeps a small slope for negatives. GELU and Swish are smooth, slightly non-monotonic activations that work well in transformers and modern deep networks.

Gradients

  • Sigmoid's derivative is at most 0.25 and near zero for large inputs, so gradients shrink layer after layer (vanishing gradients). Tanh peaks at 1 but also saturates.
  • ReLU's gradient is 1 for positive inputs and 0 for negative ones: cheap and no saturation on the positive side. A neuron whose input is always negative has zero gradient and stops learning (dying ReLU), often caused by a large learning rate or bad initialisation.
  • Leaky ReLU, PReLU and ELU keep a nonzero gradient for negatives.

Choosing

  • Hidden layers: ReLU first; try Leaky ReLU if many units are dead.
  • Transformers and large language models: GELU or gated variants such as SwiGLU.
  • Outputs: sigmoid for binary probability, softmax for multi-class, linear for regression, tanh for bounded outputs.
  • Pair ReLU-family activations with He initialisation, and sigmoid or tanh with Xavier.
