Walk through a Build vs. Buy framework when deciding whether to construct an internal service or purchase SaaS.

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 3 (Senior) · Strategy · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Build when the capability is a core differentiator and you can maintain it; buy when it is commodity. Compare total cost of ownership over several years, including maintenance and opportunity cost, along with fit, time to market, vendor and lock-in risk, security and exit options. Validate with a proof of concept and a weighted scorecard, then revisit later.

Explained in depth

Start with strategic fit

Is this core to what makes the product special? If yes, owning it can be worth the cost. If it is a commodity (authentication, email delivery, payments, observability), a vendor usually does it better and cheaper, and your engineers should work on what only you can build.

Compare total cost over 3 to 5 years

  • Build: engineering time to build, then ongoing maintenance, on-call, security patches, upgrades and documentation. Building is often the smaller part of the lifetime cost. Include the opportunity cost of the work not done.
  • Buy: licence or usage fees (and how they grow), integration work, administration, training and vendor management.
  • Open source is not free: add hosting, upgrades and the expertise to run it.

Other criteria

  • Fit: how much of the requirements does the product cover out of the box, and how painful is the gap? Customisation needs.
  • Time to market and team capacity.
  • Risk: vendor lock-in and pricing changes, vendor viability, data ownership and exit strategy (can you export your data?), SLAs, security and compliance (certifications, data residency).
  • Scale and performance at your expected volume.

Make the decision

  1. Write the requirements and mark must-haves.
  2. Shortlist options, including build and a hybrid ('buy and extend').
  3. Run a proof of concept against the real use case, and check references.
  4. Score with a weighted matrix, and put the numbers in an ADR or RFC.
  5. Plan the exit or migration path, and set a date to revisit the decision.

Biases to watch

Not-invented-here pride, underestimating maintenance, and being swayed by a polished demo.
