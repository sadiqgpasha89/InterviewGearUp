What is the CIA triad in information security?

Security engineer · Gear 1 (Junior) · Fundamentals · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

The CIA triad is confidentiality (only authorised people can read data), integrity (data is accurate and cannot be altered without detection) and availability (systems and data are accessible when needed). Security controls and risk decisions are framed around protecting these three properties, and attacks usually target one or more of them.

Explained in depth

The three properties

PropertyMeaningControlsAttack example
ConfidentialityOnly authorised parties can access informationEncryption, access control, authentication, data classification, DLPData breach, eavesdropping, stolen credentials
IntegrityData and systems are accurate and unaltered, or changes are detectedHashing, digital signatures, checksums, version control, input validation, audit logs, least privilegeTampering with a transaction, malicious code injection, modified logs
AvailabilityAuthorised users can use systems and data when neededRedundancy, backups, load balancing, DDoS protection, patching, disaster recoveryDDoS, ransomware, hardware failure

Examples

  • A bank: confidentiality of account details, integrity of balances (a changed number is a serious problem), availability of online banking.
  • A hospital: availability and integrity of patient records can be life-critical; confidentiality is also legally required.
  • A public news site: availability and integrity matter more than confidentiality.

Using it

Classify assets by which properties matter, then choose controls and trade-offs: strong encryption helps confidentiality but key loss harms availability; heavy redundancy raises availability but expands the attack surface. Risk assessment weighs likelihood and impact on each property.

Extensions

Some models add authenticity (verifying who or what sent data), non-repudiation (a party cannot deny an action, via signatures and logs), and accountability (actions traced to identities). The related Parkerian hexad adds possession, authenticity and utility.

Likely follow-ups

  • Give an example of an attack against each property.
  • Which property matters most for a hospital system and why?
  • What do authenticity and non-repudiation add to the triad?
