What are the three pillars of observability (logs, metrics and traces), and how do they differ from basic monitoring?

DevOps / SRE / Cloud · Gear 1 (Junior) · Observability · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Metrics are numeric time series that show trends and trigger alerts, logs are timestamped event records that explain what happened in detail, and traces follow a single request across services to show where time is spent. Monitoring watches known failure conditions, while observability lets you ask new questions about unknown problems from the data your system emits.

Explained in depth

The three signals

SignalWhat it isGood forExample tools
MetricsAggregated numbers over time (request rate, error rate, latency, CPU)Dashboards, alerting, trends, capacityPrometheus, CloudWatch, Datadog
LogsDiscrete event records, ideally structured JSONDebugging detail: what happened, with what input and errorLoki, Elasticsearch, Splunk, CloudWatch Logs
TracesA request's path through services as timed spansFinding which service or call is slow or failingOpenTelemetry, Jaeger, Tempo, X-Ray

How they work together

  1. An alert from a metric says "checkout error rate is 5 percent".
  2. A trace shows the slow or failing span, such as the payment service call timing out.
  3. The logs for that trace ID show the exact exception and parameters.

Use a shared trace or correlation ID in all three so you can move between them.

Monitoring versus observability

  • Monitoring: checks and alerts for conditions you predicted ("disk above 90 percent").
  • Observability: the property that you can understand internal state from outputs, including novel failures. It needs rich, high-cardinality context (user, version, region) so you can slice data when something unexpected happens.

Practices

  • Use structured logs with levels and request IDs, and avoid logging secrets.
  • Choose metrics carefully (RED: rate, errors, duration for services; USE: utilisation, saturation, errors for resources).
  • Sample traces to control cost, and keep retention tiers.
  • Alert on user-facing symptoms and SLOs, not every cause.
