How do you design a data security architecture covering classification, encryption, tokenisation and data loss prevention?

Security engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Architecture · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Start by discovering and classifying data, then protect by sensitivity: minimise and tokenise the most sensitive fields, encrypt everywhere with managed keys, control access with least privilege and masking, monitor and prevent exfiltration with DLP and logging, and govern retention and deletion. Build controls into the data platform so teams get them by default.

Explained in depth

1. Know your data

  • Discover data stores (databases, object storage, SaaS, endpoints, logs, backups) using automated scanners and catalogues; find sensitive patterns (names, IDs, card numbers, health data).
  • Classify with a simple scheme (public, internal, confidential, restricted) and tags that travel with the data. Map to regulations (PCI DSS, GDPR, HIPAA) and assign data owners.
  • Maintain a data inventory and flow map: where data enters, is stored, shared and deleted.

2. Minimise and reduce exposure

  • Collect and retain only what is needed; remove sensitive fields from logs and analytics; delete on schedule.
  • Tokenisation: replace a value (card number, national ID) with a random token and keep the real value in a hardened vault; downstream systems handle only tokens, which shrinks compliance scope. Reversal needs access to the vault.
  • Format-preserving encryption, hashing (with keys, for matching), masking, pseudonymisation for analytics, and synthetic data for testing.

3. Encrypt

  • In transit (TLS 1.2 or 1.3, mutual TLS inside) and at rest (storage and database encryption using KMS keys), with application-level or field-level encryption for the most sensitive attributes so database administrators and cloud operators cannot read them.
  • Key management with separation of duties, rotation, and per-tenant or per-classification keys.

4. Control access

  • Least privilege and role- or attribute-based access, column and row-level security, dynamic masking, just-in-time access to restricted data, and strong authentication.
  • Separate environments: no production data in development; where realism is needed, use masked subsets.
  • Protect backups and exports equally.

5. Detect and prevent leakage

  • DLP on endpoints, email, web and cloud (CASB) with content inspection and classification-based policies.
  • Data access auditing, anomaly detection (bulk reads, unusual downloads), egress controls, and alerting.
  • Watermarking, canary records and honeytokens to detect misuse.

6. Govern

Policy, training, retention schedules, deletion workflows including backups (crypto-shredding), privacy impact assessments, and regular audits. Provide a paved road: platform defaults for encryption, tagging, masking and access request flow, with metrics on classified coverage and policy violations.
