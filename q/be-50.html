What is the PACELC theorem and how does it expand upon the CAP theorem during normal operation?

Backend engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Distributed systems · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

PACELC says: if there is a Partition, choose between Availability and Consistency (as in CAP); Else, during normal operation, choose between Latency and Consistency. It adds that replication forces a trade-off even when nothing is failing: waiting for replicas to agree costs latency, while answering from one replica risks stale reads.

Explained in depth

Why CAP is not enough

CAP only describes behaviour during a network partition, which is rare. Most of the time, the design question is different: how many replicas must a request wait for?

  • Synchronous replication or quorum reads and writes give stronger consistency but add latency, including cross-region round trips.
  • Answering from the nearest replica or acknowledging before replication is fast, but a reader may see old data.

PACELC states both choices: Partition: A or C; Else: L or C.

Many databases are tunable: Cassandra's consistency levels (ONE, QUORUM, ALL) or DynamoDB's strongly consistent reads move a request along the latency-consistency axis per query.

How to use it in an interview

Start from the business requirement: a shopping cart can accept stale reads (EL), a bank balance or inventory reservation usually cannot (EC). Then name what you pay: cross-region synchronous replication might add tens or hundreds of milliseconds, and a partition may make some operations unavailable. Note that the labels are simplifications: real systems offer different guarantees per operation.

Likely follow-ups

  • Where would you place a multi-region system with strong reads and why does that cost latency?
  • How do quorum settings (R + W > N) trade latency for consistency?
  • Which parts of an e-commerce system can tolerate EL and which cannot?
