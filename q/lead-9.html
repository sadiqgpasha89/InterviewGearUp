How do you deliver difficult, constructive feedback to an engineer without causing defensiveness?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 3 (Senior) · People · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Give feedback soon after the event, in private, about specific behaviour and its impact rather than about the person. A useful pattern is Situation, Behaviour, Impact, then ask for their view and agree on what happens next. Listen for context you may be missing, and follow up so it is clearly about helping them improve.

Explained in depth

Prepare

Be clear about the specific behaviour and the impact, and about what you want to change. Check that you have the facts and are not relying on hearsay. Choose a private setting and time soon after the event, so it is fresh and the person is not surprised in a review months later.

Use a simple structure (SBI)

  • Situation: 'In yesterday's design review...'
  • Behaviour (what you observed, not your interpretation): '...you interrupted two people before they finished their points.'
  • Impact: '...and the quieter reviewers stopped contributing, so we missed some concerns.'

Then ask: 'How did that look from your side?' The person may have context you lack, and a question invites reflection instead of a defence.

Agree on what happens next

Together, decide one or two concrete changes and when you will check in. Write a brief follow-up so there is no ambiguity, and recognise improvement when you see it.

Handling defensiveness

  • Expect an emotional reaction; stay calm, acknowledge the feeling ('I can see this is frustrating') and give them a moment.
  • Return to the observable behaviour and its impact, and do not argue about intent.
  • Do not pile on extra issues, and do not soften the message until it disappears. A feedback sandwich often hides the real point.
  • If the conversation heats up, pause and continue later.

Habits that make it easier

Give positive and developmental feedback regularly so that critical feedback is not a rare event, ask for feedback on yourself, and keep notes for performance discussions. If the behaviour continues after clear feedback, escalate through your performance process.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you give critical feedback to someone more senior or more experienced than you?
  • What would you do if the person cries or becomes angry in the meeting?
  • How do you give feedback remotely?
