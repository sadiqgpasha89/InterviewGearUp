What causes vanishing and exploding gradients, and how do Xavier/Glorot and He initialization help?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Deep Learning · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

In deep networks, gradients are products of many layer derivatives. If those factors are mostly below 1 (sigmoid, tanh, small weights) the gradient vanishes and early layers stop learning; if above 1 it explodes and training diverges. Xavier initialisation (for tanh and sigmoid) and He initialisation (for ReLU) set the weight variance so signal and gradient magnitudes stay stable across layers.

Explained in depth

Why it happens

Backpropagation multiplies derivatives layer by layer. Over L layers the gradient scales roughly like (w * f')^L. If the factor is below 1, it shrinks exponentially; above 1, it blows up.

  • Vanishing: saturating activations (sigmoid derivative is at most 0.25), small weights. Early layers get almost no update.
  • Exploding: large weights, long sequences in RNNs. Losses become NaN or the weights jump.

Initialisation to keep variance stable

Choose the weight variance so that the activations and gradients keep the same scale from layer to layer:

  • Xavier / Glorot: Var(w) = 2 / (fan_in + fan_out). Suited to tanh and sigmoid (and linear) activations.
  • He (Kaiming): Var(w) = 2 / fan_in. Suited to ReLU, which zeroes half the inputs and so needs twice the variance.
nn.init.xavier_uniform_(layer.weight)
nn.init.kaiming_normal_(layer.weight, nonlinearity='relu')

Initialising all weights to zero (or the same value) breaks symmetry badly, so every neuron learns the same thing.

Other remedies

  • ReLU-family activations instead of saturating ones.
  • Normalisation (batch norm or layer norm) keeps activations in a good range.
  • Residual connections give gradients a direct path.
  • Gradient clipping (by norm) for exploding gradients, especially in RNNs and transformers.
  • LSTM or GRU gating for long sequences, and a lower learning rate or warmup.

Diagnosing

Log per-layer gradient norms; norms near zero in early layers suggest vanishing, and spikes or NaNs suggest explosion.
