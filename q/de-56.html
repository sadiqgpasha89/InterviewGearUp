How do you detect statistical anomalies in data distributions using Z-score and Interquartile Range (IQR)?

Data engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Data Quality · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

The Z-score flags values far from the mean in standard deviations (for example above 3), which assumes roughly normal data and is distorted by the outliers themselves. The IQR method flags values outside Q1 minus 1.5 times IQR and Q3 plus 1.5 times IQR, which is robust to skew and outliers. Compare against recent history per segment and season for pipeline monitoring.

Explained in depth

Why statistical checks

Fixed thresholds (row count > 1,000,000) break as the business grows. Statistical checks learn what is normal for a metric (daily row count, null rate, mean order value, latency) from history and alert on deviations, catching silent issues such as partial loads, duplicated loads, or unit changes.

Z-score

For a value x with mean μ and standard deviation σ: z = (x - μ) / σ. Under a normal distribution about 99.7 percent of values have |z| < 3, so |z| > 3 is unusual.

import pandas as pd
s = df["daily_rows"]
z = (s - s.mean()) / s.std()
anomalies = df[z.abs() > 3]

Pros: simple, interpretable, works on rolling windows (s.rolling(30)). Cons: assumes approximate normality, mean and standard deviation are themselves inflated by outliers (masking: one huge value hides others), poor for heavily skewed or small samples. Modified Z-score uses median and median absolute deviation: 0.6745 * (x - median) / MAD; flag above 3.5. It is far more robust.

IQR method (Tukey fences)

IQR = Q3 - Q1; outliers lie below Q1 - 1.5*IQR or above Q3 + 1.5*IQR (use 3*IQR for extreme outliers).

q1, q3 = s.quantile([0.25, 0.75])
iqr = q3 - q1
low, high = q1 - 1.5 * iqr, q3 + 1.5 * iqr
anomalies = df[(s < low) | (s > high)]
WITH q AS (SELECT approx_percentile(amount, 0.25) q1, approx_percentile(amount, 0.75) q3 FROM orders WHERE order_date >= current_date - 30)
SELECT o.* FROM orders o, q WHERE amount > q3 + 1.5*(q3-q1) OR amount < q1 - 1.5*(q3-q1);

Pros: distribution-free, robust to outliers, fine for skewed data. Cons: ignores trend and seasonality, can over-flag long-tailed metrics (revenue), needs enough data.

Applying to pipelines

  • Monitor metrics per run: row count, distinct keys, null percentage, min/max/mean, freshness lag, and per-segment values (by country or source).
  • Use a rolling baseline (last 30 to 90 runs) and compare against the same weekday or hour to handle seasonality (weekend dips). Decompose with STL or use ratios to the same day last week; use Prophet-style or ML detectors for strong patterns.
  • Combine rules: statistical anomaly and minimum absolute change, to avoid alerting on tiny variations.
  • Warm up new metrics, handle holidays and planned launches (suppression windows), and let owners mark false positives.
  • Tools: Soda anomaly detection, Monte Carlo, Elementary (dbt), dbt-expectations, BigQuery ML ML.DETECT_ANOMALIES, Great Expectations with computed bounds.

Choosing

Z-score (robust version) for roughly symmetric metrics with stable variance; IQR for skewed metrics and quick first-line checks; seasonal models for traffic-like data.

Pitfalls

Estimating the baseline including the anomalous run, alert fatigue from many correlated metrics, ignoring multiple comparisons (hundreds of checks produce false positives), and treating an anomaly as an error when it is a real business change.
