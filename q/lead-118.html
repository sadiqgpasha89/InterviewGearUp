Tell me about a time you spotted a quality problem before it reached users.

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 1 (Junior) · Behavioral · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Describe how you noticed something wrong, such as a failing edge case, unclear requirement or suspicious data, checked that it was real, raised it promptly without blame, helped fix or delay the release, and added a test, check or habit to prevent a repeat. Show care for users, evidence over opinion, and willingness to be the person who speaks up.

Explained in depth

What they want to see

Ownership of quality beyond your own ticket, attention to detail, and the courage to raise a concern, in a constructive way.

Structure

  1. Situation: what was about to ship and why it mattered.
  2. Noticing: how you found the issue (reviewing a pull request, testing an unusual input, reading the logs, comparing numbers on a dashboard).
  3. Verify: reproduce it, measure how many users it could affect, and write down the evidence.
  4. Raise it: tell the owner or the team early, with facts and a suggested option (fix now, ship with a mitigation, delay), and no blame.
  5. Resolve: the fix or decision, plus how you checked it.
  6. Prevent: a regression test, a checklist item, a monitoring alert.

Example

"During review I noticed a date calculation that would fail for users in a time zone behind UTC. I wrote a failing test to prove it, posted it in the pull request with a short note, and offered a fix. The author fixed it the same day and we added time zone cases to our test helpers. A later change that would have hit the same bug was caught by those tests."

Tips

  • Use a real, modest example; it does not need to be a disaster.
  • Show that you checked facts before raising an alarm.
  • Mention thanking or crediting the colleague who fixed it.

Likely follow-ups

  • What if the team wanted to ship anyway?
  • How do you raise a concern without sounding negative?
  • What prevention did you put in place afterwards?
