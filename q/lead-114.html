How do you manage stress and pressure when deadlines are tight?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 1 (Junior) · Behavioral · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Describe practical habits: break work into small steps, focus on what you can control, communicate early about risks, ask for help or scope changes, protect sleep and breaks, and stay calm when problems occur. Give an example where you delivered under pressure, and show that you avoid unhealthy heroics and learn from each crunch.

Explained in depth

What they want to hear

Resilience, planning and honest communication. Not "I thrive on stress" without specifics, and not "I work all night every time".

Strategies

  1. Break the problem down: list the remaining work, order by importance, and tackle the next small step; progress reduces anxiety.
  2. Control the controllable: focus on actions rather than worries.
  3. Communicate early: if risk appears, tell stakeholders with options (reduce scope, extend date, add help). Surprises near a deadline cause the most damage.
  4. Prioritise ruthlessly: deliver the must-haves first, defer nice-to-haves.
  5. Ask for help from teammates or your manager, and offer to help in return.
  6. Look after yourself: sleep, short breaks, exercise; mistakes rise with exhaustion.
  7. Review afterwards: what caused the crunch (underestimation, scope growth) and how to prevent it.

Example

"Two days before a demo, an integration failed. I listed what had to work, asked a colleague to take the dashboard polish, told my manager the reduced scope and the plan, and focused on the integration. We demoed the core flow on time, and afterwards we added early integration testing to our plan so the risk showed up sooner."

Tips

Be honest about stress; naming your coping habits is a strength. Show a pattern of calm, communication and learning.

Likely follow-ups

  • Tell me about a time you worked under extreme pressure.
  • What do you do when you realise a deadline is not achievable?
  • How do you help teammates under stress?
