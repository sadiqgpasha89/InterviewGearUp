What is phishing, and how can individuals and organisations defend against it?

Security engineer · Gear 1 (Junior) · Social engineering · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Phishing tricks people into revealing credentials, installing malware or sending money by impersonating trusted senders through email, text, calls or fake sites. Defend with layered controls: email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), filtering and link protection, phishing-resistant MFA such as passkeys, endpoint protection, user training and easy reporting, and a tested incident response process.

Explained in depth

What it is

An attacker sends a message that appears to come from a bank, a colleague, a delivery company or IT support, using urgency or fear ("your account will be closed") to prompt an action: clicking a link to a fake login page, opening a malicious attachment, or approving a payment.

Variants

  • Spear phishing: targeted at a specific person using researched details.
  • Whaling: aimed at executives.
  • Business email compromise (BEC): impersonating a CEO or vendor to redirect payments, often with no malware at all.
  • Smishing (SMS), vishing (voice), quishing (QR codes), and attacker-in-the-middle kits that relay logins and steal session cookies.
  • Increasingly polished, AI-written messages without the old spelling mistakes.

Organisational defences

  1. Email authentication: SPF, DKIM and a DMARC policy of reject or quarantine to stop domain spoofing.
  2. Filtering: secure email gateways, attachment sandboxing, URL rewriting and time-of-click checks, warning banners for external senders.
  3. Phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2 security keys and passkeys bind logins to the real domain, so a fake site gets nothing); SMS and push codes can be relayed or fatigued.
  4. Least privilege and endpoint protection (EDR, application control, macros disabled) to limit damage.
  5. Process controls: out-of-band verification for payment or bank detail changes, dual approval, vendor callbacks.
  6. Training and simulations that teach, not shame, with a one-click "report phish" button, and quick feedback to reporters.
  7. Incident response: fast removal of malicious mail from all inboxes, password and session resets, and review of mailbox rules.

Individual habits

Check the sender and the real link destination (hover), do not open unexpected attachments, type known URLs or use bookmarks, use a password manager (it will not autofill on the wrong domain), and when in doubt contact the sender through another channel.
