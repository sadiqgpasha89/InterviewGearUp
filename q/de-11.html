How does Data Vault 2.0 structure data into Hubs, Links, and Satellites?

Data engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Data Modeling · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Data Vault 2.0 models the warehouse with three table types: Hubs hold unique business keys, Links record relationships between hubs, and Satellites store descriptive attributes with full history, each with load date and record source. This separates keys from changing context, supports parallel loads, tracks everything for audit and absorbs source changes without redesign.

Explained in depth

The three building blocks

  • Hub: one row per unique business key (customer number, product code), with a hash key, the business key, a load date and a record source. Hubs rarely change.
  • Link: the relationship between two or more hubs (a customer places an order), holding the hash keys of the hubs. It models many-to-many relationships naturally.
  • Satellite: descriptive attributes and context for a hub or link (name, address, status). Every change adds a new row with a load date, so the full history is kept. Typically one satellite per source system or rate of change.

     |                 |
SAT_CUSTOMER_CRM  SAT_ORDER_DETAILS

Why teams use it

  • Auditability and traceability: raw data is loaded unchanged with source and time, so you can reproduce history.
  • Parallel, insert-only loading: hubs, links and satellites load independently and fast, with hash keys avoiding lookups.
  • Resilience to change: a new source attribute means a new satellite, with no rebuild of existing tables.
  • Works for many sources and enterprise-scale integration.

Drawbacks

  • Many tables and joins; not designed for direct reporting.
  • More modelling effort and a steeper learning curve than a star schema.

Typical architecture

Source, then raw vault, then optional business vault (derived rules), then information marts (star schemas or wide tables) for BI. The vault is the integration layer, and the marts serve users.
