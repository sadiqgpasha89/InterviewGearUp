How do you guide a team through project estimation using story points, t-shirt sizing, or probabilistic forecasting?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Delivery · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Estimation helps planning, but it is uncertain by nature. Story points express relative size and complexity, t-shirt sizes give quick rough categories for early planning, and probabilistic forecasting uses historical throughput or cycle time (for example Monte Carlo simulation) to give ranges with confidence levels. Use the lightest method that supports decisions, and communicate ranges, not promises.

Explained in depth

Story points

  • Relative sizes (often Fibonacci: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) of effort, complexity and uncertainty, compared with reference stories.
  • Estimated by the team together (planning poker) to expose different assumptions; the conversation is the value.
  • Velocity (points per sprint) is used for the team's own planning. Do not compare it across teams or use it as a performance target; that invites inflation.
  • Break down anything above 8 or 13 before committing.

T-shirt sizing

S, M, L, XL for roadmap-level and early discovery estimates when detail is thin. Map sizes to ranges later, and split big items before they are scheduled.

Probabilistic forecasting

Uses real data instead of opinion:

  1. Collect throughput (items completed per week) or cycle times for the last 6 to 12 weeks.
  2. Run a Monte Carlo simulation: repeatedly sample from the history to simulate future weeks until the backlog is finished.
  3. Read results as probabilities: "85 percent confidence of finishing 40 items by 12 March; 50 percent by 28 February".

Works well with small, similarly sized items (right-sizing) and needs no estimation meetings.

Guiding the team

  • Estimate to support decisions, not to create commitments.
  • Share ranges and confidence, record assumptions and risks, and update forecasts as you learn.
  • Include testing, review and release work, plus a buffer for unknowns.
  • Review estimation accuracy in retrospectives, and use spikes to reduce uncertainty before estimating large unknowns.

Likely follow-ups

  • Why are story points not convertible to hours?
  • How does the Monte Carlo forecast work from throughput data?
  • How do you handle a stakeholder who demands a fixed date?
