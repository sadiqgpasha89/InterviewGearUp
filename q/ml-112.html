How would you design a model that predicts B2B SaaS invoice delinquency before it happens?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Applied ML · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Define the target at a decision point: for each open invoice, will it reach 30 (or 60) days past due within a set window, which matches when collectors can act. Build features only from data available at that moment: payment history, billing events, product usage trends, account and contract data. Split by time, measure precision at the capacity of the team, calibrate, and validate with a holdout.

Explained in depth

1. Target: tie it to a decision

  • A vague 'defaulted yes or no' label mixes very different outcomes. Instead, score each open invoice (or account) at a fixed point, for example on the issue date and again weekly, and predict 'reaches 30+ days past due within the next 30 days'. Add a second head or model for 60 or 90+ days if actions differ.
  • Alternatives worth naming: an ordinal target (current, 1 to 30, 31 to 60, 60+ days past due), a regression on days late, or a survival model of time to payment, which handles invoices that are still open (censored) naturally.
  • Exclude or label separately: disputed invoices, invoices paid late because of a procurement process (PO missing), and billing errors on the vendor side. Otherwise the model learns your own process failures as customer risk.

2. Features (all as of the scoring date)

  • Payment behaviour: days late on the last N invoices and its trend (paid on day 3, now on day 25), partial payments, failed card charges and retries, payment method changes, dispute count.
  • Billing context: invoice amount relative to the account's usual amount, a price increase or seat true-up, payment terms, billing frequency, currency.
  • Product usage: active users divided by paid seats, 30-day versus 90-day trend in active users, admin logins, API usage, recent seat reductions or downgrade attempts.
  • Relationship signals: support tickets about billing, a lost champion (key admin deactivated), renewal date proximity, customer success health scores.
  • Firmographics: segment, region, industry, tenure; parent and subsidiary links, because one parent may pay for many instances.

3. Avoid leakage

  • Use point-in-time feature values (a feature store with as-of joins). Collection notes, dunning stage or 'sent to agency' flags are consequences of delinquency and must not be features.
  • Split by time (train on older periods, test on later ones) and keep all invoices of one account in the same fold.

4. Model and evaluation

  • Gradient-boosted trees are a strong baseline for this tabular data; a survival model if timing matters.
  • Positives are rare, so report PR-AUC, and above all precision and recall at the number of accounts the team can actually work each week.
  • Calibrate the scores (isotonic or Platt) so 0.3 means about 30 percent, which lets finance compute expected loss = probability x amount and prioritise by money at risk, not just probability.
  • Check performance by segment and region, and against seasonality (fiscal year ends, budget freezes).

5. From score to impact

  • Map score bands to actions (earlier reminder, CSM call, offer a payment plan, no action) and run a randomized holdout to measure the effect on days sales outstanding and write-offs. Being accurate is not the same as changing outcomes.

Likely follow-ups

  • Why might a survival model be better than a binary classifier here?
  • Which features would leak the label, and how would you catch them?
  • How do you prioritise accounts when the team can only contact 200 per week?
