How do you measure and mitigate demographic parity and equalized odds bias in machine learning models?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Ethics · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Demographic parity requires equal positive-prediction rates across groups, while equalized odds requires equal true positive and false positive rates across groups. Measure them on held-out data per protected attribute, then mitigate with pre-processing (reweighing, resampling), in-processing (fairness constraints or adversarial debiasing) or post-processing (group thresholds), noting trade-offs and legal context.

Explained in depth

Where bias comes from

Historical bias in labels (past hiring or lending decisions), sampling bias and under-representation, proxy features (zip code correlating with race), measurement error differing by group, feedback loops, and optimisation targets that ignore subgroup performance. Removing the protected attribute does not remove bias, because other features reveal it.

Definitions (binary classifier Ŷ, true label Y, protected attribute A)

  • Demographic (statistical) parity: P(Ŷ=1 | A=a) = P(Ŷ=1 | A=b). Equal selection rates, independent of group. Measure: difference or ratio of positive rates (the "four-fifths rule" in US employment law uses ratio ≥ 0.8). Ignores true labels, so it can force equal rates even if base rates genuinely differ, and may require accepting less qualified candidates in a group or rejecting more qualified in another.
  • Equalized odds: P(Ŷ=1 | Y=y, A=a) = P(Ŷ=1 | Y=y, A=b) for y ∈ {0, 1}: equal true positive rate and false positive rate across groups. Errors are distributed equally.
  • Equal opportunity: relaxed version requiring only equal TPR (qualified people have an equal chance across groups).
  • Predictive parity (calibration): equal precision (PPV) across groups; calibration within groups: scores mean the same probability for each group.
  • Individual fairness: similar individuals receive similar predictions.
  • Counterfactual fairness: predictions unchanged in a counterfactual world with a different protected attribute.

Impossibility results (Kleinberg et al.; Chouldechova): when base rates differ between groups, a non-perfect classifier cannot satisfy calibration and equalized odds simultaneously (and demographic parity conflicts with calibration and accuracy). You must choose metrics based on harms and context.

Measuring

from fairlearn.metrics import MetricFrame, demographic_parity_difference, equalized_odds_difference, selection_rate
from sklearn.metrics import recall_score, precision_score
mf = MetricFrame(metrics={"selection": selection_rate, "tpr": recall_score, "precision": precision_score},
                 y_true=y_test, y_pred=pred, sensitive_features=A_test)
print(mf.by_group)                                       # per-group metrics
print(demographic_parity_difference(y_test, pred, sensitive_features=A_test))
print(equalized_odds_difference(y_test, pred, sensitive_features=A_test))

Evaluate intersectional groups (race × gender), include confidence intervals (small groups), and slice by performance, calibration and error types. Use disparate impact analysis and audit data distributions.

Mitigation

  1. Pre-processing: fix the data. Reweighing (instance weights so group and label are independent), resampling, relabelling, removing or transforming proxy features, learning fair representations, collecting more data for under-represented groups.
  2. In-processing: change training. Add fairness constraints or penalties to the loss (Fairlearn ExponentiatedGradient, GridSearch reductions with DemographicParity() or EqualizedOdds() constraints), adversarial debiasing (an adversary tries to predict A from representations or scores), regularisation.
  3. Post-processing: adjust outputs. Group-specific thresholds (Fairlearn ThresholdOptimizer) to equalise rates, calibrated equalised odds, reject option classification near the decision boundary. Simple and model-agnostic, but may require using the protected attribute at decision time, which can be legally restricted (disparate treatment).

Always measure the accuracy-fairness trade-off and report it.

Governance and context

  • Choose metrics by harm analysis: lending (equal opportunity and calibration), hiring screens (selection ratios and equal opportunity), criminal risk (false positive rates), medical triage (equal sensitivity).
  • Understand legal frameworks (US ECOA, Title VII, EU AI Act, GDPR automated decision rules) and document model cards, datasets sheets, impact assessments, human oversight, appeals, and monitoring after deployment (fairness drift).
  • Involve domain experts and affected groups; fairness is not purely technical.
  • Toolkits: Fairlearn, AIF360 (IBM), What-If Tool, Aequitas, Google's Fairness Indicators, SHAP for proxy analysis.

Pitfalls

Dropping the protected attribute and assuming fairness, optimising one metric while harming another, ignoring intersectional groups and small samples, evaluating on unrepresentative data, and treating mitigation as a one-time step.

Likely follow-ups
