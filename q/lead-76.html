Explain the four Staff+ archetypes: Tech Lead, Enterprise Architect, Solver, and Right Hand.

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · People · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Will Larson's four Staff-plus archetypes are the Tech Lead (guides one team's technical direction), the Architect (owns direction and quality across a critical area), the Solver (parachutes into the hardest problems for a while) and the Right Hand (extends an executive's reach across the organisation). Roles differ in scope, relationships and how success is measured.

Explained in depth

Origin

Described in Will Larson's book Staff Engineer, based on interviews with staff-plus engineers. The titles vary by company; the archetypes describe the shape of the work.

The four archetypes

ArchetypeFocusTypical workKey skills
Tech LeadOne team (or a few)Sets technical direction, aligns design decisions, unblocks the team, partners with the product and engineering managerTechnical judgement, delivery, mentoring, clarity of communication
ArchitectAn area such as a platform, data, or a critical domainOwns the direction, quality and long-term design for that area across teams, writes and reviews designs, drives standardsDeep domain expertise, systems thinking, influencing without authority
SolverHard, ambiguous problems wherever they ariseTakes on a high-priority problem for months, solves it or sets it up for others, then moves onFast learning, debugging and problem solving, autonomy
Right HandExtends a senior leader (director or VP)Runs cross-organisational initiatives, provides leader-level context, handles operational complexityJudgement, trust, organisational awareness, communication

Common features

  • Impact comes through influence, writing (design docs, strategy), mentoring and sponsorship, not authority.
  • They multiply others, set technical strategy, raise engineering quality and work on the most valuable problems, not just the hardest code.
  • Roles may shift during a career; many engineers move between archetypes.

Using the model

For self-reflection, choose the path that suits your interests; for organisations, define expectations and promotion criteria clearly and avoid creating roles without a clear remit; for managers, understand what each person needs to succeed.
