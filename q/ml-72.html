How do Learning Rate Schedulers (Cosine Annealing with Warmup) stabilize deep learning convergence?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Training · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Warmup starts training with a very small learning rate and ramps linearly to the peak over the first steps, so unstable early gradients and adaptive-optimizer statistics settle. Cosine annealing then decays the rate smoothly along a half cosine toward a minimum, giving large steps early, fine-grained steps late, and stable convergence without tuning abrupt step drops.

Explained in depth

Why schedule the learning rate

A constant learning rate forces a compromise: large values make fast early progress but oscillate or diverge near a minimum, small values converge precisely but slowly and can get stuck. A schedule uses large steps to explore early, then small steps to settle into a good basin, which typically yields better final accuracy and generalisation.

Warmup

For the first T_w steps (often 1 to 5 percent of training, for example 500 to 4000 steps) the learning rate rises linearly from near 0 (or 0.1×) to the peak η_max: η_t = η_max · t / T_w for t < T_w. Reasons:

  • At initialisation weights are random and gradients are large and noisy; large steps can push the model into a bad region or cause loss spikes and divergence.
  • Adam and AdamW estimate gradient moments (m, v) from few samples early on, so the effective update size is unreliable (RAdam analyses this variance problem); warmup gives the statistics time to stabilise.
  • Transformers with layer normalisation and large batches are especially sensitive; warmup is nearly mandatory for BERT, GPT, ViT. Large-batch training also needs a scaled learning rate with warmup (linear scaling rule).
  • It reduces the chance of destroying pretrained weights when fine-tuning.

Cosine annealing

After warmup the rate decays from η_max to η_min following a half cosine over the remaining T steps: η_t = η_min + ½ (η_max − η_min) (1 + cos(π · (t − T_w) / (T − T_w))) Properties: starts near-flat (stay near the peak, keep exploring), decreases fastest in the middle, flattens at the end (very small steps for fine convergence). It is smooth, has few hyperparameters (peak, minimum, length), and works well empirically across vision and language models. LLM recipes typically decay to 10 percent of peak (η_min = 0.1 η_max) and train for a fixed token budget.

import torch, math
opt = torch.optim.AdamW(model.parameters(), lr=3e-4, weight_decay=0.1)
warmup, total = 1000, 100_000
def lr_lambda(step):
    if step < warmup:
        return step / warmup
    progress = (step - warmup) / (total - warmup)
    return 0.1 + 0.9 * 0.5 * (1 + math.cos(math.pi * progress))      # decays to 10% of peak
sched = torch.optim.lr_scheduler.LambdaLR(opt, lr_lambda)
for batch in loader:
    loss = model(batch).loss; loss.backward()
    torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), 1.0)
    opt.step(); sched.step(); opt.zero_grad()

(Also transformers.get_cosine_schedule_with_warmup, CosineAnnealingLR, OneCycleLR.)

Variants

  • Cosine with warm restarts (SGDR): periodically reset the rate to the peak (cycles of increasing length) to escape local minima and enable snapshot ensembles.
  • One-cycle / super-convergence: rise to a high peak then anneal to near zero in one cycle.
  • Linear decay, polynomial, inverse square root (original Transformer), step decay, exponential, ReduceLROnPlateau (reactive), warmup-stable-decay (WSD) (constant plateau then quick decay, allows continuing training).

Practical guidance

Tune the peak learning rate first (LR range test), then warmup length (a few hundred to thousands of steps), and ensure the schedule length matches the actual training budget. Step the scheduler per iteration (not per epoch) when warmup is step-based. Log the learning rate. Interactions: batch size (scale LR), weight decay, gradient clipping, and mixed precision loss scaling.

Pitfalls

No warmup with Adam on transformers (loss spikes), scheduler length mismatched with total steps (cosine ends early or never decays), stepping the scheduler before the optimiser, resuming training without restoring scheduler state, and too small a minimum LR wasting the final tokens.
