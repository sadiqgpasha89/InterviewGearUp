How do you mediate and resolve a bitter technical or architectural conflict between two senior engineers?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 3 (Senior) · People · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Talk to each engineer separately to understand their underlying goals and constraints, then bring them together around a shared goal and agreed decision criteria. Separate facts, assumptions and preferences, test the disagreement with data or a small prototype, document the options, and if there is still no consensus, decide, or name who decides, by a deadline.

Explained in depth

1. Understand first

Meet each person alone. Ask what outcome they are protecting (latency, cost, maintainability, risk, team skills) and what they are worried about. Positions ('use Kafka') often hide interests ('we cannot lose events'). Check whether the conflict is really technical, or about status, past friction or workload.

2. Reframe around a shared goal

Bring them together with the common aim: the customer outcome, system health or the deadline. Ground rules: one person speaks at a time, describe the problem before the solution, and critique ideas, not people.

3. Make the decision tractable

  • Agree on decision criteria and their weights before debating options (performance targets, operational cost, reversibility, time to deliver, team expertise).
  • Separate facts (we can measure these), assumptions (we can test these) and preferences (we must weigh these).
  • Settle facts with data: a time-boxed spike or benchmark, a small prototype, or a look at what comparable teams do.
  • Write the options and trade-offs in a short RFC or ADR so the debate is on the page, and other people can contribute.

4. Decide

Aim for consensus, but set a deadline. If it does not come, the designated decision maker (you, the tech lead or the architect) decides, explains the reasoning and records dissent. Use 'disagree and commit', and note a point at which the decision will be reviewed against evidence.

5. Repair afterwards

Check in one-to-one with each person. Acknowledge the strong points of the option not chosen, and credit them. If the conflict was personal, address the behaviour directly.

Avoid

Taking sides early, deciding by seniority, letting it drag on, and ignoring the effect on the rest of the team.

Likely follow-ups

  • What do you do when both engineers are right and the options are close?
  • How do you handle a conflict that has become personal?
  • How do you make sure the engineer whose option lost stays engaged?
