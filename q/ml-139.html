How do you turn a vague business request for an agent into user stories and testable acceptance criteria?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Agents · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Ask what decision or task the agent supports and what good looks like. Split the request into deterministic rules (always true, tested exactly) and judgement tasks (measured statistically on a labelled set). Write user stories with Given-When-Then criteria, define thresholds for probabilistic behaviour, and list the edge cases where the agent must stop and hand off to a person.

Explained in depth

Clarify

  • 'The agent should intelligently decide if a property claim needs an on-site inspection.' Ask: what happens today, who decides, how often are they wrong, what does a wrong call cost, what data is available at decision time?

Split the requirement

  • Deterministic: 'claims above 25,000 always need an on-site adjuster'; 'images below 720p are rejected'. These are rules with exact tests.
  • Probabilistic: 'spot conflicts between the police report and the customer's description'; 'tell hail damage from wear'. These need a labelled dataset and a target metric.

Write stories and criteria

Story: As a senior claims adjuster, I want roof damage photos classified so that clear wind or hail claims go straight to desk review.

  • Deterministic criterion: Given a photo below 720p, when it is submitted, then the agent asks for a higher-resolution upload and does not call the classifier.
  • Statistical criterion: On an agreed benchmark of 500 labelled claims, precision for 'hail damage' is at least 95% and recall at least 85%, with zero unhandled errors.
  • Handoff criterion: Given conflicting evidence or confidence below 0.7, the claim goes to a person with the reason recorded.

Also define

  • Non-functional needs: latency, cost per claim, availability, audit logging.
  • Out of scope, so the agent is not expected to handle everything.
  • Who owns the benchmark dataset and how it is refreshed.
  • How acceptance is signed off in UAT and monitored after launch.

Likely follow-ups

  • Who should label the benchmark set, and how many examples do you need?
  • How do you write an acceptance criterion for tone or helpfulness?
  • What goes in the out-of-scope list?
