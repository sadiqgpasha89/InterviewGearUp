How does ARIMA model autoregression, differencing, and moving averages for time-series forecasting?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Time Series · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

ARIMA(p, d, q) models a time series with autoregression on its own lags (AR p), differencing d times to remove trend and make it stationary (I), and a moving average of past forecast errors (MA q). Identify orders with ACF and PACF plots and information criteria, check residuals are white noise, and use SARIMA when seasonality exists.

Explained in depth

Components

ARIMA = AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average, parameters (p, d, q).

  • AR(p): the value depends linearly on its previous p values: y_t = c + φ₁ y_{t−1} + ... + φ_p y_{t−p} + ε_t.
  • I(d): difference the series d times (y'_t = y_t − y_{t−1}) to remove trend and achieve stationarity (constant mean, variance and autocovariance over time). d = 1 handles a linear trend, d = 2 a quadratic one.
  • MA(q): the value depends on the previous q forecast errors (shocks): y_t = c + ε_t + θ₁ ε_{t−1} + ... + θ_q ε_{t−q}.

Combined (on the differenced series): y'_t = c + Σ φᵢ y'_{t−i} + Σ θⱼ ε_{t−j} + ε_t. SARIMA(p,d,q)(P,D,Q)_m adds seasonal AR, differencing and MA terms at period m (12 for monthly data with yearly seasonality). ARIMAX / SARIMAX add exogenous regressors.

Box-Jenkins workflow

  1. Plot and decompose: inspect trend, seasonality, outliers, variance changes. Apply a log or Box-Cox transform if variance grows with level.
  2. Make stationary: difference (regular and seasonal) until stationary. Check with the Augmented Dickey-Fuller test (null: unit root, so a small p-value suggests stationary) and KPSS (null: stationary). Avoid over-differencing (induces negative autocorrelation and inflates variance).
  3. Identify orders from the stationary series:
  • PACF cuts off after lag p for an AR(p); ACF decays gradually.
  • ACF cuts off after lag q for an MA(q); PACF decays gradually.
  • Mixed ARMA: both decay; use information criteria.
  1. Estimate parameters by maximum likelihood.
  2. Select among candidates by AIC or BIC (lower is better), or automatically (auto_arima in pmdarima).
  3. Diagnose residuals: they should resemble white noise: no autocorrelation (Ljung-Box test, ACF of residuals), roughly normal and constant variance.
  4. Forecast with prediction intervals and evaluate with time-based validation (rolling-origin backtesting), using MAE, RMSE, MAPE or MASE versus a naive or seasonal-naive baseline.
from statsmodels.tsa.statespace.sarimax import SARIMAX
from statsmodels.tsa.stattools import adfuller
print(adfuller(y.diff().dropna())[1])                        # p-value for stationarity after differencing
model = SARIMAX(y, order=(1, 1, 1), seasonal_order=(0, 1, 1, 12), enforce_stationarity=False).fit(disp=False)
print(model.summary()); fc = model.get_forecast(steps=12); ci = fc.conf_int()

Intuition for AR and MA

AR captures momentum and persistence (today resembles yesterday); MA captures short-lived shocks that echo for a few periods (a promotion affects today and tomorrow). Differencing models trends as changes. Together they give a parsimonious description of autocorrelation.

Strengths and limitations

  • Strong, interpretable baseline for univariate, short to medium horizon forecasting with limited data; gives prediction intervals; well-understood theory.
  • Assumes linear relationships, constant variance (use GARCH for volatility), regular sampling, and a single seasonal period; struggles with multiple seasonalities, holidays, structural breaks, many related series and nonlinear effects; requires manual or search-based order selection; refit as data changes.
  • Alternatives: ETS/Holt-Winters (exponential smoothing), Prophet (trend, multiple seasonalities, holidays), TBATS, state-space models, gradient boosting with lag features, and deep models (N-BEATS, TFT, DeepAR, PatchTST) for many series; hierarchical forecasting reconciles levels.

Pitfalls

Random train-test splits, forecasting before checking stationarity or residuals, over-differencing, ignoring seasonality and calendar effects, and judging accuracy without a baseline.
