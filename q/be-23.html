What are the normal forms (1NF, 2NF, 3NF) and when is denormalization preferred?

Backend engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Databases · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Normal forms remove redundancy and update anomalies. 1NF requires atomic values with no repeating groups, 2NF removes partial dependencies on part of a composite key, and 3NF removes transitive dependencies between non-key columns. Denormalise deliberately for read performance, reporting and distributed data when joins or consistency costs dominate.

Explained in depth

The anomalies normalisation prevents

In one wide table orders(order_id, customer_name, customer_email, product, product_price, ...):

  • Update anomaly: changing a customer's email requires editing many rows; missing one leaves inconsistent data.
  • Insert anomaly: you cannot record a new product until someone orders it.
  • Delete anomaly: deleting the last order for a customer also deletes customer information.

Normal forms

  • 1NF: each column holds one atomic value, each row is unique, and there are no repeating groups or comma-separated lists (tags = 'a,b,c' violates it; use a child table).
  • 2NF: in 1NF and every non-key attribute depends on the whole composite key. In order_items(order_id, product_id, product_name, qty), product_name depends only on product_id, so move it to products.
  • 3NF: in 2NF and non-key attributes depend only on the key, not on other non-key attributes (no transitive dependency). In employees(id, dept_id, dept_name), dept_name depends on dept_id; move it to departments.
  • BCNF: stricter 3NF where every determinant is a candidate key. It resolves rare cases with overlapping candidate keys.
CREATE TABLE customers (id BIGINT PRIMARY KEY, name TEXT, email TEXT UNIQUE);
CREATE TABLE products  (id BIGINT PRIMARY KEY, name TEXT, price NUMERIC(10,2));
CREATE TABLE orders    (id BIGINT PRIMARY KEY, customer_id BIGINT REFERENCES customers(id), created_at TIMESTAMPTZ);
CREATE TABLE order_items (order_id BIGINT REFERENCES orders(id), product_id BIGINT REFERENCES products(id),
                          qty INT, unit_price NUMERIC(10,2), PRIMARY KEY (order_id, product_id));

Notice unit_price is stored on the line item on purpose: it is the historical price at purchase time, which is a real, different fact from the current product price (not redundancy).

When to denormalise

  • Read-heavy paths where joins across large tables are too slow (feeds, product listings): store precomputed counts, totals or copies of names.
  • Reporting and analytics: star schemas and wide fact tables.
  • Distributed or NoSQL stores without joins (embed related data).
  • Snapshots and history: invoices must keep the address and price at the time.
  • Materialised views, summary tables and caches are denormalisation managed by the database.

Costs and safeguards

Denormalisation moves complexity into writes: keep copies consistent with triggers, transactional updates, event-driven projections, or periodic rebuilds, and treat the normalised tables as the source of truth. Document which columns are derived.

Guidance

Start at 3NF, measure real query patterns, and denormalise selectively where evidence shows a need. Normalise OLTP; denormalise analytical models.
