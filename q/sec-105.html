What is the difference between a vulnerability, a threat and a risk?

Security engineer · Gear 1 (Junior) · Fundamentals · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

A vulnerability is a weakness in a system, process or person, a threat is something that could exploit it, such as an attacker or event, and risk is the likelihood and business impact of that exploitation occurring. Security work finds vulnerabilities, understands threats, and reduces risk by mitigating, transferring, accepting or avoiding it.

Explained in depth

Definitions

  • Vulnerability: a weakness that can be exploited. Examples: unpatched software, SQL injection flaw, default password, weak configuration, untrained staff.
  • Threat: a potential cause of harm: a threat actor (criminal, insider, nation state, hacktivist) or event (malware, natural disaster, power failure). A threat has capability and intent.
  • Exploit: the technique or code that takes advantage of a vulnerability.
  • Asset: what you want to protect (data, systems, reputation).
  • Risk: the combination of the likelihood that a threat exploits a vulnerability and the impact on the asset.

A simple view: Risk = Threat x Vulnerability x Impact (or likelihood times impact).

Example

An internet-facing server (asset) runs software with a known remote code execution flaw (vulnerability). Ransomware groups scan for it (threat). The risk is high if the server holds critical data and the flaw is easy to exploit. If the server is isolated and holds nothing important, the same vulnerability is lower risk.

Treating risk

  1. Mitigate: patch, add controls, segment the network.
  2. Transfer: insurance, outsourcing to a provider (some responsibility remains).
  3. Accept: document and approve when cost of control exceeds the impact.
  4. Avoid: stop the risky activity or retire the system.

Residual risk is what remains after controls.

Prioritising

Use severity scores (CVSS) together with context: exposure, whether exploits are known or actively used (CISA KEV, EPSS), asset criticality, and compensating controls. Not every vulnerability is equally risky.

Likely follow-ups

  • How would you prioritise a list of vulnerabilities by risk?
  • What is an exploit and how is it different from a vulnerability?
  • What are the four ways to treat a risk?
