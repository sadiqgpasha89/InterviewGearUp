How do you build and lead a data engineering team: structure, hiring, on-call and ways of working?

Data engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Leadership · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Align the team to business domains and a platform core, hire for fundamentals and ownership, set clear standards for code, testing and data contracts, run sustainable on-call with SLAs and runbooks, protect time for reliability and tech debt, and measure delivery and data product outcomes. Grow people with clear ladders and rotate between platform and domain work.

Explained in depth

Team structure

  • Centralised team: consistency and shared tooling, but can become a request bottleneck.
  • Embedded engineers in domain teams: close to the business, but risk inconsistent practices and isolation.
  • Hub and spoke (common at scale): a platform team builds and runs the shared infrastructure (ingestion, orchestration, warehouse or lakehouse, observability, governance tooling), while domain data engineers own data products. A community of practice and shared standards connect them.

Hiring

Look for strong SQL and data modelling, software engineering habits (testing, version control, code review), understanding of distributed systems basics, debugging skills, and communication with analysts and business partners. Use practical exercises (design a pipeline, find the bug in a query) and structured interviews. Build diverse teams and invest in onboarding with a documented first-month plan.

Ways of working

  • Standards: coding, naming, testing (unit and data tests), code review, CI/CD for pipelines, infrastructure as code, and documentation of every dataset (owner, SLA, schema).
  • Data contracts with source teams, so upstream changes do not silently break pipelines.
  • Intake and prioritisation: a visible backlog, a triage rotation for ad hoc requests, and roadmap commitments (for example 70 percent planned, 20 percent reliability, 10 percent interrupts). Tie work to business outcomes.
  • Incidents: severity levels, postmortems, and tracking of data incidents and time to recovery.

On-call

Primary and secondary rotations with fair compensation, runbooks, and alerting focused on freshness and quality SLAs, not noise. Review pages weekly and automate fixes for recurring issues. Make pipelines idempotent and self-healing to reduce wake-ups.

Growth and culture

Career ladders with technical depth and leadership tracks, mentoring, rotation between platform and domain work, time for learning, and recognition of reliability work. Run regular retrospectives and share wins and failures.

Measures

Delivery speed (lead time for new datasets), pipeline reliability (SLA attainment), data quality incidents, cost per unit, consumer satisfaction, and team health (engagement, attrition).
