When should you make the executive decision to cancel or rewrite a failing multi-quarter project?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Strategy · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Decide to cancel or rewrite by comparing the cost to finish against the expected value with fresh eyes, ignoring sunk costs. Gather evidence on progress, remaining risk, changing requirements and opportunity cost, test whether a smaller scope or a reset would work, and then decide openly. Cancel when the expected future return does not justify the remaining investment, and capture lessons.

Explained in depth

Warning signs

Repeated schedule slips, growing scope, no demonstrable working increments, unresolved fundamental technical risks, declining morale or departing people, a changed market or strategy, and a "just a bit more" attitude with no new evidence.

Structured evaluation

  1. Reassess the goal: is the original business case still valid? What value would a finished product deliver now?
  2. Estimate remaining cost and risk honestly: use independent estimates, a bottom-up plan of what is left, and a range of outcomes (including the chance of not finishing).
  3. Ignore sunk costs: money and time already spent cannot be recovered. Ask only "if we were starting today, knowing what we know, would we begin this project, and what would we do instead?"
  4. Include opportunity cost: what else could the team deliver? What are the costs of the old system staying alive?
  5. Explore alternatives:
  • Reduce scope to the smallest valuable slice and ship it.
  • Pause and reset with a smaller team and a clear checkpoint.
  • Incrementally replace (strangler) instead of a rewrite, buy a solution, or kill it.
  1. Set explicit criteria and a time box ("if we cannot demonstrate X by date Y, we stop") so the decision is not emotional.

Rewrite warnings

Full rewrites usually take longer than predicted, lose hidden knowledge embedded in old code, and deliver no value until done. Rewrites are justified when the existing system blocks essential goals and incremental paths have been tried or ruled out.

Making and communicating the decision

  • Involve key stakeholders, use data, and give people a chance to present counterarguments.
  • Decide and communicate clearly, with respect for the work done: explain the reasoning, what happens next to people and code, and what will be reused.
  • Run a retrospective on how the project got here, so the organisation learns.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you recognise the sunk cost fallacy in a team or in yourself?
  • What is a lower-risk alternative to a full rewrite?
  • How do you communicate a cancellation to the team and stakeholders?
