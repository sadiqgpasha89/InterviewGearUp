What are Data Contracts and how do they establish API-like guarantees between producers and consumers?

Data engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Data Quality · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

A data contract is a versioned, machine-checkable agreement between a data producer and its consumers covering schema, semantics, quality, freshness, ownership and change rules. Like an API, it makes the data product an explicit interface: breaking changes are detected in CI and negotiated, instead of surfacing as broken dashboards downstream.

Explained in depth

The problem

In many companies analytics pipelines are built on top of operational databases and event streams owned by application teams who do not know who consumes their data. A developer renames a column, changes an enum, or alters timestamp semantics, and finance dashboards, ML features and reverse ETL jobs break days later. Trust erodes because the coupling is implicit.

What a data contract contains

  • Identity and ownership: dataset name, owning team, contact and on-call, support SLA.
  • Schema: fields, types, nullability, primary keys, allowed values, formats (for example ISO 8601 UTC timestamps), PII classification tags.
  • Semantics: definitions of fields and metrics ("order_total excludes tax and shipping"), units, currency, time zones, grain (one row per order line).
  • Quality expectations: uniqueness, not-null, referential integrity, ranges, row-count bounds, null-rate thresholds.
  • Service levels: freshness (arrives within 15 minutes of event), availability, retention, delivery mechanism (topic, table, file).
  • Evolution policy: versioning (semver), what counts as breaking, deprecation window, notification channel.
  • Terms of use: allowed purposes, access, privacy and retention constraints.
# datacontract.yaml (Open Data Contract Standard style)
apiVersion: v3.0.0
kind: DataContract
id: orders.v2
owner: checkout-team
status: active
schema:
  - name: orders
    properties:
      - {name: order_id, type: string, required: true, unique: true}
      - {name: amount, type: decimal, required: true, quality: [{type: range, min: 0}]}
      - {name: email, type: string, classification: pii}
slaProperties:
  - {property: freshness, value: 15, unit: minutes}

Enforcement points

  1. At the producer (shift left): CI checks on the producing service's migrations or event schemas run contract tests; a breaking change fails the build or requires a new major version. Tools: Data Contract CLI, Schema Registry, Protobuf buf breaking checks, Pact-like testing.
  2. At ingestion: validate incoming data against the contract (Great Expectations, Soda, dbt tests, Flink or Kafka validation); route violations to a quarantine or dead letter table.
  3. At the platform: catalogue registration, lineage-based impact analysis, and monitoring against SLAs with alerts to the owner.

Benefits

Fewer breakages and firefights, clear accountability, faster onboarding of consumers, safe self-service, and a foundation for data mesh and data products. They also document PII handling for governance and compliance.

Adoption approach

Start with the most critical datasets (revenue, customer, events driving ML), agree a minimal contract (schema, owner, freshness), automate checks, then expand. Pair with a change process: producers announce deprecations, run dual versions during a migration window, and consumers register their usage so impact is known.

Contracts versus schema compatibility

Registry compatibility only validates syntax (can this reader decode that writer). Contracts also cover semantics, quality, SLAs, ownership and process, so both are needed.

Pitfalls

Contracts written but not enforced, ownership unclear, contracts that over-specify and block legitimate change, and treating them as documentation only.
