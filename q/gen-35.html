Explain the Dutch National Flag algorithm (three-way partitioning) for sorting 0s, 1s, and 2s in-place.

Software engineer (core) · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Algorithms · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

The Dutch National Flag algorithm sorts an array of 0s, 1s and 2s in one pass and in place using three pointers: low for the boundary of 0s, high for the boundary of 2s and mid scanning. Swap 0s to the front and 2s to the back, leaving 1s in the middle. It runs in O(N) time and O(1) space.

Explained in depth

Invariant

Maintain four regions: [0, low) are 0s, [low, mid) are 1s, [mid, high] are unknown, (high, n) are 2s.

def sort_colors(a):
    low, mid, high = 0, 0, len(a) - 1
    while mid <= high:
        if a[mid] == 0:
            a[low], a[mid] = a[mid], a[low]
            low += 1
            mid += 1
        elif a[mid] == 1:
            mid += 1
        else:                                  # a[mid] == 2
            a[mid], a[high] = a[high], a[mid]
            high -= 1                          # do not advance mid

Why mid does not move after a swap with high

The element swapped in from high is unexamined, so it must be checked again. After a swap with low, the element that arrives at mid came from the 1s region, so it is already known to be 1 and mid can advance.

Complexity

A single pass with at most N iterations: O(N) time, O(1) space, versus O(N) time with extra space for counting sort or O(N log N) for a general sort.

Where else it is used

Three-way quicksort partitions into less than, equal to and greater than the pivot, which makes arrays with many duplicate keys sort in O(N) instead of degrading.
