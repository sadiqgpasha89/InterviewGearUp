Tell me about a time you drove a cross-team technical initiative without formal authority.

Software engineer (core) · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Behavioral · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Answer with a structured story: the problem and why it mattered, how you built a coalition by understanding each team's incentives, how you made the case with data and a proposal, how you reduced risk with a pilot, and the measurable result. Show influence through listening, clarity and shared credit rather than escalation or force.

Explained in depth

What interviewers look for

Evidence that you can lead through influence: spotting a problem larger than your team, persuading peers and other teams, navigating competing priorities, and delivering a result with lasting effect.

A strong structure (STAR plus lessons)

  1. Situation: briefly, the problem and its cost. Example: "Four teams each maintained a separate authentication library, causing inconsistent security fixes and three incidents in a year."
  2. Task: why it was yours to drive, and that you had no authority over the other teams' roadmaps.
  3. Actions:
  • Listened first: interviewed leads of each team to learn their constraints and what they would gain.
  • Made the case with data: incident counts, engineering hours, risk, a short written proposal (RFC) with options and trade-offs.
  • Built allies: found a sponsor, a champion in each team, and sought feedback early so the plan became theirs.
  • Reduced risk: a small pilot with one willing team, clear success criteria, and an opt-in migration path with tooling to make adoption cheap.
  • Kept momentum: a shared channel, regular demos, visible progress, and credit given to contributors.
  • Handled resistance by addressing concerns directly (for example offering extension points) and escalating only as a last resort, with the facts.
  1. Result: numbers such as "three of four teams migrated in two quarters, auth-related incidents fell to zero, and we saved about 30 engineer-days per quarter".
  2. Reflection: what you learned, for example "I would involve the platform team earlier".

Pitfalls

Using "I" for everything with no sign of teamwork, vague results, blaming resistant teams, or describing authority you actually had. Choose an example that is genuinely cross-team and technical, with real stakes.

Likely follow-ups

  • How did you handle a team that resisted?
  • What would you do differently if you ran the initiative again?
  • How did you measure that the initiative worked?
