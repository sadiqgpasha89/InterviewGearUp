How do you build a detection engineering and threat hunting capability in a security operations team?

Security engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Detection · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Treat detections as software: derive them from threat models and the MITRE ATT&CK framework, build reliable log pipelines, write rules as code with tests and version control, tune for precision with feedback from analysts, measure coverage and quality, and complement automated alerts with hypothesis-driven threat hunting that feeds new detections back. Automate triage and response where safe.

Explained in depth

Foundations

  1. Telemetry: collect high-value logs with consistent schemas: endpoint (EDR), identity (SSO, directory), cloud audit logs, network (DNS, proxy, flow), email, SaaS, and application logs. Normalise to a common model (OCSF, ECS) and ensure time sync, retention and health monitoring of log sources (a silent source is a blind spot).
  2. Threat-informed prioritisation: identify likely adversaries and attack paths for your business, map to MITRE ATT&CK techniques, and rank by risk and prevalence. Use intelligence, past incidents and red team results.

Detection engineering lifecycle

  • Detection as code: rules (Sigma, YARA, KQL, SPL, SQL) in Git with peer review, unit tests against sample logs, CI validation, and staged deployment.
  • Each detection has a description, ATT&CK mapping, data sources, severity, triage steps (runbook), owner and known false positives.
  • Tuning: track precision (true positive rate), volume and analyst feedback; suppress known benign patterns narrowly, enrich alerts with context (asset criticality, user role, threat intel), and retire stale rules.
  • Behavioural and anomaly detection where signatures fall short, but with human-understandable logic.
  • Validation: purple team and adversary emulation (Atomic Red Team, Caldera) to confirm detections fire; track coverage on an ATT&CK heat map and gaps.

Threat hunting

Hypothesis-driven, human-led searches for undetected activity: "an attacker with stolen cloud keys might enumerate IAM from a new location". Steps: form hypothesis from intelligence or ATT&CK, query data, investigate anomalies, document findings, and convert successful hunts into automated detections or fix log gaps. Time-box hunts and track outcomes.

Operations

  • Automation (SOAR) for enrichment, containment and ticketing; alert triage tiers and runbooks.
  • Metrics: mean time to detect and respond, alert-to-incident ratio, false positive rate, ATT&CK coverage, detection test pass rate, hunts completed and detections created.
  • Team skills: analysts, detection engineers and data engineers; training and knowledge sharing; blameless reviews of missed detections after incidents.
