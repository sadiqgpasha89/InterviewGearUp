How would you build a demand forecasting system for thousands of products and stores, including hierarchy, intermittent demand, promotions and inventory decisions?

Data scientist · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Forecasting systems · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Forecast at the level decisions are made, mixing global machine learning models trained across series with features for price, promotions, holidays and weather, handle intermittent demand with specialised methods, reconcile forecasts across the product and location hierarchy, produce probabilistic forecasts for safety stock, backtest with rolling origins, and monitor bias and accuracy by segment.

Explained in depth

Frame the decision

Forecasts exist to drive decisions: replenishment orders, staffing, pricing, production. Define granularity (SKU x store x day or week), horizon (lead time plus review period), refresh frequency, and costs of overstock versus stockouts. The relevant accuracy is at the decision level and horizon, not an arbitrary aggregate.

Data

Sales (distinguish demand from censored sales: stockouts hide true demand, so use availability flags and imputation), prices, promotions and marketing, calendar and holidays, weather, local events, product attributes, store attributes, competitor prices, and inventory and supply constraints. Clean returns, outliers, and structural changes (store closures).

Modeling strategy

ChallengeApproach
Thousands of seriesglobal models (one model across series) such as LightGBM with lag, rolling and calendar features, or deep models (DeepAR, TFT, N-HiTS); per-series ETS/ARIMA baselines for stable items
Promotions and priceexplicit features (discount depth, promo type, display), price elasticity, promo lift models; cannibalisation and halo effects
Intermittent / lumpy demandCroston, SBA, TSB, zero-inflated or negative binomial models, aggregation to weekly level, probabilistic count models
Cold start / new productsuse attributes and similar-product analogues, hierarchical priors, launch curves; update quickly as data arrive
Multiple seasonalitiesday-of-week, week-of-year, holiday features, Fourier terms
Trend and structural changerecent-window weighting, changepoint handling, level adjustments
Hierarchy (SKU, category, store, region, total)reconciliation so forecasts add up

Hierarchical reconciliation: bottom-up, top-down, or optimal MinT (minimum trace) which combines forecasts from all levels using the error covariance to give coherent, usually more accurate forecasts.

Probabilistic forecasts and inventory

Replenishment depends on the demand distribution over the lead time: safety stock at a target service level uses a quantile (e.g., the 95th percentile). Produce quantile forecasts (quantile regression, conformal prediction, distributional models) or sample paths. Evaluate with pinball loss / weighted quantile loss, coverage of intervals, and bias. The newsvendor logic sets the optimal quantile = cost of understock / (cost of understock + cost of overstock).

Evaluation

Rolling-origin backtests on the decision horizon; metrics: WAPE / wMAPE (volume-weighted, robust to zeros), MASE, bias (tendency to over- or under-forecast), RMSSE; report by segment (fast versus slow movers, promo versus non-promo) and by horizon. Simple benchmarks (seasonal naive) must be beaten. Business metrics: fill rate, stockouts, waste, inventory turns.

Production

Daily or weekly pipelines with data quality checks, model versioning, overrides by planners with feedback (forecast value added), exception reports for abnormal series, scalable training (per cluster or global), explainability for planners, and continuous monitoring of bias and accuracy.

Pitfalls

Forecasting sales rather than demand during stockouts, optimising MAPE on low volumes, ignoring promotions' effect on baselines, and delivering incoherent hierarchical numbers.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you forecast a new product with no sales history?
