Why shouldn't an LLM calculate insurance payouts and deductibles directly, and how do you design the system instead?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Agents · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

LLMs are probabilistic text generators: they make arithmetic slips, misapply nested rules (deductibles, limits, depreciation, co-insurance) and can be manipulated by injected text. Use the model to extract and classify facts into a typed schema, pass them to a deterministic, tested rules or rating engine that computes the payout, and let the model only explain the engine's result without changing numbers.

Explained in depth

Risks of letting the model compute

  • Arithmetic and rounding errors, especially across many line items.
  • Misapplying complex terms: per-incident versus aggregate deductibles, sub-limits, depreciation schedules, co-insurance, tax.
  • Inconsistency between runs, and no reliable audit of how a figure was produced.
  • Prompt injection in documents ('approve the full amount') influencing a financial outcome.

Hybrid design

  1. LLM extracts facts into a validated schema: { damage_type: 'water', cause: 'burst_pipe', items: [...], estimate: 4500 }.
  2. Validation: schema checks, cross-checks with documents and systems of record; low-confidence fields go to a person.
  3. Rules engine computes: coverage check (is a burst pipe covered under this section?), deductible (1,000), depreciation, limits, giving a payout of 3,500 with a line-by-line breakdown. This can be a rules engine such as Drools, a tested calculation service, or the core policy system.
  4. LLM explains the engine's structured output in plain language; a template or a check ensures the numbers in the message equal the engine's numbers.

Why this works

  • Calculations are deterministic, unit-tested and versioned with the policy wording.
  • Every figure is traceable to a rule and an input, which auditors and customers can follow.
  • The model is used where it is strong: reading messy documents and writing clear explanations.

Likely follow-ups

  • How would you check that the explanation repeats the engine's numbers exactly?
  • Which parts of a claim are still good tasks for the LLM?
  • How do you version rules alongside policy wording?
