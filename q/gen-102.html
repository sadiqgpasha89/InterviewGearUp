Design a web crawler that fetches billions of pages politely and keeps its index fresh.

Software engineer (core) · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · System design · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

A crawler keeps a URL frontier (a prioritised, per-host queue), distributed fetchers that obey robots.txt and politeness delays, a parser that extracts links, a seen-URL check with a Bloom filter or hash store, content deduplication with fingerprints, and storage for pages. Prioritise by importance and change rate, and shard everything by host.

Explained in depth

Requirements

Crawl billions of pages, respect site rules and load, tolerate failures, avoid duplicates, and keep important or fast-changing pages fresh. Assume around 1 billion pages per month, roughly 400 pages per second sustained, and average page of 100 KB, so tens of terabytes of raw storage monthly.

Components

  1. Seed URLs start the crawl.
  2. URL frontier: queues of URLs to fetch. It has two layers: priority (importance, change rate, depth) and politeness (one queue per host, so only one request per host at a time with a delay such as 1 second or the Crawl-delay value).
  3. Fetchers: many workers, each owning a set of hosts (consistent hashing by hostname). DNS results are cached, connections are reused, and timeouts are strict.
  4. robots.txt cache: fetch and cache per host, and honour disallow rules.
  5. Parser and link extractor: extract text and links, normalise URLs (lowercase host, remove fragments, resolve relative paths, sort parameters).
  6. Seen-URL filter: a Bloom filter in memory backed by a persistent store, so already known URLs are not queued again.
  7. Content dedup: hash of the content for exact duplicates, SimHash or MinHash for near-duplicates.
  8. Storage: raw pages in object storage, metadata (URL, fetch time, ETag, status, checksum) in a key-value store, and a hand-off to the indexing pipeline through a queue.

Hard problems

  • Politeness and fairness: per-host rate limits and a global cap; back off on 429 and 5xx.
  • Spider traps: limit URL length and depth, cap pages per host and detect repeating patterns.
  • Freshness: schedule recrawls with an adaptive interval, using the page's observed change history, sitemaps, and If-Modified-Since or ETag requests to save bandwidth.
  • Dynamic content: a small share needs a headless browser, which is expensive and rationed.
  • Failures: workers are stateless besides their frontier shard; use checkpointing and retries with exponential backoff.

Scaling and ethics

Partition by host, scale fetchers horizontally, and monitor throughput, error rates and queue depth. Identify the crawler with a clear user agent and contact address, and avoid pages that opt out.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you detect duplicate and near-duplicate pages?
  • How do you avoid spider traps and infinite URL spaces?
  • How do you decide how often to recrawl each page?
