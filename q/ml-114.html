How do you prove the ROI of a data science initiative whose goal is reducing risk rather than growing revenue?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Strategy · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Measure the avoided loss causally, not before-and-after. Agree the metrics with finance up front (write-offs, days sales outstanding, cost to collect, hours saved), keep a randomized holdout that gets the old process, and compare outcomes. Convert faster collection into working-capital value, subtract the cost of building and running the system, and report a range with confidence intervals.

Explained in depth

1. Agree what counts, with finance, before launch

  • Bad-debt expense or write-offs avoided.
  • Days sales outstanding (DSO): cash arrives sooner, which frees working capital.
  • Cost to collect: collector hours per recovered dollar, agency fees avoided.
  • Customer side effects: churn or satisfaction among contacted accounts; an approach that recovers cash but loses customers is not a win.

2. Get a counterfactual

  • Before-and-after comparisons are confounded by seasonality, macro conditions and pricing changes.
  • Hold out a random 5 to 10 percent of eligible accounts that keep the standard process, stratified by risk band and size. Compare recovery rate, days to pay and write-offs between arms.
  • If a holdout is not allowed, use a staggered rollout by region or team (difference-in-differences) or a regression discontinuity around a score threshold.
  • Power the test in advance: write-offs are rare and lumpy, so you may need a leading indicator (share of invoices paid within 30 days) for the early readout and the loss metric for the final one.

3. Translate to money

Value = avoided write-offs
      + (DSO reduction in days x daily revenue x cost of capital)
      + collector hours saved x loaded hourly cost
      - build, run and maintenance cost

Present it as an NPV or annual run-rate with a low, expected and high case, and state the assumptions (cost of capital, persistence of the effect).

4. Keep it honest

  • Report the confidence interval, not just the point estimate, and keep a small long-running holdout to check the effect does not fade.
  • Attribute carefully when other initiatives (new payment methods, a policy change) launched at the same time.

Likely follow-ups

  • How would you size the holdout when write-offs are rare?
  • What would you do if leadership refuses a holdout group?
  • How do you value a reduction of 5 days in DSO?
