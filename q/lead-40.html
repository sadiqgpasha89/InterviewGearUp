What changes when scaling an engineering organization from 10 to 50 to 100+ engineers?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Organization · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Each size jump changes the needed structure. Around 10 people, informal communication works. By 50, you need teams with clear ownership, managers, defined interfaces and shared standards. Beyond 100, you add engineering leadership layers, platform teams, formal planning, hiring and onboarding systems, and deliberate culture and communication mechanisms, because ad hoc coordination no longer scales.

Explained in depth

About 10 engineers

  • One team, shared context, little process. The founder or tech lead makes most decisions; communication is direct.
  • Priorities: hire well, set basic engineering practices (version control, CI, code review), and document essentials.

Growing to about 50

  • Teams of 5 to 8 (the two-pizza idea), each with clear ownership of a product area or services and a manager or tech lead.
  • Ownership boundaries and well-defined interfaces between teams, so changes do not need constant negotiation.
  • Introduce engineering managers, regular 1-on-1s, career ladders and levelling, structured hiring and onboarding.
  • Shared tooling and a first platform or infrastructure group, plus basic on-call and incident processes.
  • Written communication (design docs, RFCs) replaces hallway agreement.

Beyond 100

  • Layers of leadership (directors, VPs) with managers of managers; clear accountability and decision rights.
  • Platform teams providing paved roads; architecture and standards forums; security and compliance functions.
  • Formal planning (quarterly roadmaps, OKRs) and dependency management across groups.
  • Investment in culture and communication: values, internal events, newsletters, leadership development, and succession planning.
  • Specialised roles: recruiters, program managers, developer experience, SRE.

Cross-cutting challenges

  • Conway's law: system design mirrors the communication structure, so design teams to match the architecture you want.
  • Coordination cost rises faster than headcount; reduce it by making teams more autonomous.
  • Keep speed: automate (CI, deployment), keep decisions low in the organisation, and add process only when its absence hurts.
  • Protect quality and culture as hiring accelerates, and watch for a split between old and new staff.
  • Revisit organisation shape at least yearly.
