What are the responsibilities of the Incident Commander (IC) during a Sev-1 production incident?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 3 (Senior) · Operations · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

The Incident Commander coordinates the response for a Sev-1 and does not fix the problem personally. They declare and classify the incident, assign roles (operations lead, communications lead, scribe), keep a clear picture of the situation, decide priorities and mitigations, manage escalation, ensure regular stakeholder updates, and call the end of the incident and hand-off to the postmortem.

Explained in depth

Purpose of the role

In a major incident, many people can pull in different directions. The IC provides single-point coordination and decision making so the technical responders can focus on the fix.

Responsibilities

  1. Declare and scope: confirm the severity, open a dedicated channel or bridge, and announce that you are the IC.
  2. Assign roles quickly:
  • Operations or technical lead(s): investigate and apply mitigations.
  • Communications lead: updates for stakeholders, support and customers (status page).
  • Scribe: timestamped log of events, decisions and actions.
  • Subject matter experts and a deputy IC for long incidents.
  1. Maintain situational awareness: what is the impact, what do we know, what are we trying, who is doing what. Ask for hypotheses and next steps, and keep the group focused on one or two at a time.
  2. Prioritise mitigation over root cause: roll back, fail over, disable a feature flag or add capacity first; investigate the cause afterwards.
  3. Make decisions and unblock: call for approvals, additional responders and vendor escalation. Decide trade-offs, for example accepting a degraded service.
  4. Communicate on a schedule: updates every 15 to 30 minutes with status, impact, actions and next update time, even if nothing changed.
  5. Manage people: rotate tired responders, hand over formally in long incidents, protect responders from interruptions by executives.
  6. Close: confirm recovery and monitor, declare resolved, and trigger the postmortem and follow-up.

Principles

Stay calm and clear, avoid hands-on debugging, keep communication concise (use a standard format), and document everything. Practise with game days so the roles feel natural.
