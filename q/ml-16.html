How do advanced optimizers (Momentum, RMSProp, Adam, AdamW) accelerate convergence?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Optimization · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Momentum accumulates a running average of gradients to speed movement along consistent directions and damp oscillations. RMSProp scales each parameter's step by a running average of its squared gradients. Adam combines both with bias correction, and AdamW decouples weight decay from the gradient update, which regularises correctly and is the standard for transformers.

Explained in depth

SGD with momentum

v = beta * v + grad, then w = w - lr * v. Past gradients build up speed along steady directions and cancel in directions that oscillate (such as steep, narrow valleys). A typical beta is 0.9.

RMSProp

Keeps s = rho * s + (1 - rho) * grad^2 and updates w = w - lr * grad / (sqrt(s) + eps). Parameters with large gradients get smaller steps and rarely updated ones get larger steps, which fixes the problem of a single learning rate for all dimensions.

Adam

Tracks both the first moment m (momentum of gradients, beta1 about 0.9) and second moment v (squared gradients, beta2 about 0.999):

m = b1*m + (1-b1)*g
v = b2*v + (1-b2)*g^2
m_hat = m / (1 - b1^t);  v_hat = v / (1 - b2^t)     # bias correction for early steps
w = w - lr * m_hat / (sqrt(v_hat) + eps)

Bias correction matters because m and v start at zero and are too small in the first steps.

AdamW

In Adam, adding L2 penalty to the loss puts the penalty through the adaptive scaling, so it does not behave like true weight decay. AdamW applies decay directly: w = w - lr * (adaptive_step + wd * w). It generalises better and makes the best learning rate less dependent on decay.

Practical guidance

  • Adam or AdamW with warmup and a decay schedule is the default for transformers and many deep nets.
  • SGD with momentum and a tuned schedule often generalises slightly better on vision tasks, but needs more tuning.
  • Typical starting rates: 1e-3 for Adam on small models, 1e-4 to 3e-4 for fine-tuning large ones.
