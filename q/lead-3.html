Your manager sets a deadline you think is unrealistic. What do you do?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 3 (Senior) · Planning · Not yet reviewed by a person (origin not recorded)

In 30 seconds

Break the work down, estimate with ranges and risks, show the trade-off between scope, time and quality, and offer options such as phased delivery or reduced scope instead of a plain yes or no.

Explained in depth

  1. Understand the date: is it tied to a market event or contract, and what is truly fixed?
  2. Decompose and estimate with best, likely and worst cases. Surface unknowns and dependencies, and run a short spike on the riskiest part.
  3. Present options:
  • Full scope at a later date.
  • Reduced scope (an MVP with an explicit cut list) on the requested date.
  • Adding people, with a warning about ramp-up cost (Brooks's law: adding people to a late project makes it later).
  • Cutting quality, only with explicit acknowledgement of risk, and rarely recommended.
  1. Agree the decision in writing, track progress with a burn-up chart, and raise risks early.
  2. If you are told to commit anyway, do so transparently, protect the team from burnout, and make any shortcuts visible as debt.

Trust comes from being accurate over time, not from always saying yes.

Likely follow-ups

  • What if your manager insists on the original date?
  • How do you estimate unfamiliar work?
  • What is Brooks's law?
