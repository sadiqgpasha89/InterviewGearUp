What makes a high-quality, reproducible bug report that accelerates developer remediation?

QA / SDET · Gear 1 (Junior) · Defects · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

A high-quality bug report lets someone reproduce the problem in minutes. It has a clear specific title, exact steps to reproduce, expected versus actual result, environment details (build, browser, device, data), and evidence such as screenshots, logs or a video, plus severity. One issue per report, with no blame or speculation.

Explained in depth

Essential fields

  1. Title: specific and searchable. Good: "Checkout button stays disabled after applying a 100% coupon (Chrome 126)". Bad: "Checkout broken".
  2. Environment: application version or build, operating system, browser or device, test account, and feature flags.
  3. Steps to reproduce: short, numbered, from a known starting state, including the exact data used.
  4. Expected result and actual result: tie them to the requirement if possible.
  5. Frequency: always, intermittent (for example 3 of 10 tries), or once.
  6. Evidence: screenshot or screen recording, console and network logs, request IDs and stack traces.
  7. Severity and impact: who is affected and whether a workaround exists.

Good habits

  • Reproduce it at least twice and reduce it to the minimal steps.
  • Report one defect per ticket so each can be tracked and fixed independently.
  • Stay factual and neutral. Describe what happened, not who caused it, and keep guesses about the cause in a clearly labelled note.
  • Search for duplicates first and link related issues.
  • Never include real customer data or secrets in the report.

For intermittent bugs

Record what you tried, the rate, timing and any pattern (data, order of actions, load), and attach logs from a failing run.

Likely follow-ups

  • What do you do when a bug cannot be reproduced consistently?
  • How do you decide whether two reports are duplicates?
  • What makes a bad bug title?
