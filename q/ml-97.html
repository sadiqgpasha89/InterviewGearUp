What are the trade-offs of deploying LLMs on dedicated GPUs versus serverless inference endpoints?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Deployment · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Dedicated GPUs give predictable low latency, full control over the engine and batching, high utilisation at steady load, and lower cost per token at scale, but you pay for idle capacity and run operations yourself. Serverless endpoints scale to zero and charge per request or token, ideal for spiky or low traffic, but have cold starts, limits, less control and a higher unit price at high volume.

Explained in depth

The two models

  • Dedicated GPU deployment: you reserve GPU instances (A100, H100, L40S) or managed dedicated endpoints (SageMaker real-time endpoints, Vertex AI dedicated endpoints, Azure ML managed endpoints, provisioned throughput on Bedrock and Azure OpenAI, Kubernetes with vLLM or TensorRT-LLM). Capacity runs continuously (or with autoscaling) and you pay per GPU-hour.
  • Serverless inference: a platform runs the model on shared or on-demand infrastructure and bills per token or per request (OpenAI, Anthropic, Bedrock on-demand, Together, Fireworks, Groq, Vertex AI model garden), or per GPU-second with scale-to-zero (Modal, RunPod serverless, Cloud Run GPU, Replicate, SageMaker Serverless Inference for small models, Lambda for CPU-only).

Cost reasoning

  • Dedicated cost ≈ GPU hourly price × number of GPUs × hours. A single H100 at about $3 to $5 per hour serving a quantised 70B model might deliver, say, 1,500 to 3,000 output tokens per second with continuous batching under load, so cost per million tokens drops sharply when utilisation is high (60 to 80 percent).
  • Serverless cost = tokens × price per token. Break-even: monthly token volume at which the per-token bill exceeds the reserved GPU cost at realistic utilisation. If your traffic uses the GPU 10 percent of the time, serverless is usually cheaper; at sustained high utilisation, dedicated wins. Include engineering and on-call cost, redundancy (at least 2 replicas for HA), and peak provisioning (diurnal traffic leaves GPUs idle at night unless autoscaled or shared with batch workloads).
  • Mitigate idle cost with autoscaling, spot or preemptible instances (for batch), scheduling offline jobs in valleys, multi-tenant model sharing and multi-LoRA serving.

Technical differences

  • Cold start: loading tens to hundreds of GB of weights from object storage to GPU takes tens of seconds to minutes; mitigations: keep minimum warm replicas, snapshot or fast-loading formats (safetensors, tensorizer, GPU memory snapshots), local NVMe caches, smaller or quantised models, provisioned concurrency.
  • Performance engineering on dedicated: choose quantisation (FP8, AWQ), tensor parallelism across GPUs, continuous batching and paged attention, prefix caching, speculative decoding, request routing by length, separate prefill and decode (disaggregation), reserve KV cache headroom; monitor time to first token, tokens per second, queue time, GPU memory.
  • Serverless limits: rate limits (tokens per minute), context limits, concurrency caps, timeouts, request size, and provider model deprecations.
  • Compliance: dedicated in your VPC supports strict data residency, private networking, customer-managed keys and no data sharing; serverless needs vendor agreements (DPA, zero data retention, regional endpoints, SOC 2, HIPAA BAA).

Choosing

  • Start serverless for prototypes, unpredictable demand, and when quality of frontier models matters most.
  • Move to dedicated when volume is high and stable, latency SLOs are strict, you need custom or fine-tuned open models, or data cannot leave your boundary.
  • Hybrid: dedicated for baseline traffic, serverless for overflow bursts, with a gateway (LiteLLM, Portkey, Envoy AI gateway) for routing, fallbacks and cost tracking; route easy requests to small models and hard ones to larger models.

Pitfalls

Comparing list prices without utilisation, forgetting redundancy and engineering cost, ignoring cold starts on SLO-bound paths, and locking into a provider-specific API without an abstraction layer.

Likely follow-ups
