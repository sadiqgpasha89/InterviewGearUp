Design a notification service that fans out push notifications, SMS, and emails to millions of users.

Backend engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Architecture · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

A notification service accepts events through an API, applies user preferences and templates, and fans out to push, SMS and email channels via durable queues and per-channel workers with rate limiting, retries and provider failover. Key concerns are scale, idempotency, ordering, preferences and quiet hours, delivery tracking, and cost control.

Explained in depth

Requirements

Multiple channels (mobile push, SMS, email, in-app, web push), transactional (OTP, receipts: low latency, high priority) and bulk or marketing (campaigns: high volume, lower priority) traffic, user preferences and opt-outs, localisation, scheduling, deduplication, delivery receipts, high availability and compliance (CAN-SPAM, GDPR, TCPA quiet hours).

Architecture

Producers (services, campaigns) -> Notification API (validate, idempotency key, auth)
   -> Event bus (Kafka) -> Orchestrator:
        resolve audience, load preferences, apply rules (dedupe, frequency caps, quiet hours, locale), render template
   -> Channel queues (push | sms | email), partitioned and prioritised
   -> Channel workers -> providers (APNs, FCM, Twilio, SES/SendGrid) 
   -> delivery receipts/webhooks -> status store + analytics

Fan-out for millions

  • A campaign targets a segment; do not expand it in one process. A splitter reads the audience in pages (cursor over a segment table or query) and publishes batches of recipient ids to a queue; workers expand each batch and enqueue per-user messages. This allows parallelism, resumability and backpressure.
  • Use partitions by user id for ordering and locality, autoscale workers on queue lag, and rate limit per provider and per tenant.
  • Separate priority lanes: OTP and security alerts never queue behind a million marketing emails.
  • Use batch APIs (FCM multicast, SES bulk) and keep long-lived HTTP/2 connections to APNs.

Reliability

  • Idempotency: key (event_id, user_id, channel) stored with a unique constraint, so retries and duplicate events do not send twice.
  • Retries with exponential backoff and jitter, dead-letter queues, and circuit breakers per provider; failover to a secondary SMS or email provider.
  • Handle provider throttling (429) by honouring Retry-After, and token hygiene: remove invalid device tokens when APNs or FCM report them, process email bounces and complaints, maintain suppression lists.
  • Outbox pattern so a business transaction and the notification event commit atomically.

Preferences and rules

Preference service per user and category (channel opt-in, frequency, digest, quiet hours by time zone), global unsubscribe, consent records, frequency capping with Redis counters, collapse similar notifications (collapse_key, thread ids), and time-zone aware scheduling using a delayed queue or scheduler.

Data and tracking

Store notification records (status: queued, sent, delivered, opened, failed) with TTL, ingest provider webhooks and open and click events, expose analytics and per-user history (in-app inbox via a database plus WebSocket or SSE updates).

Cost and scale estimates

10 million recipients at 5,000 sends per second takes about 33 minutes per channel; plan provider quotas, warm up email sender IPs and domains (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), and compare costs (SMS is the most expensive, so prefer push or email where possible).

Pitfalls

Single shared queue for all priorities, no dedupe (users get repeated pushes after retries), ignoring provider rate limits, synchronous sends in the request path, leaking PII in logs, and missing unsubscribe handling.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you fan out one event to ten million users without overloading the system?
  • How do you handle provider outages and rate limits (APNs, FCM, Twilio, SES)?
  • How do you prevent duplicate or too frequent notifications?
