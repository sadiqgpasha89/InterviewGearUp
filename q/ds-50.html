How do you assess and mitigate unfairness in a machine learning model used for decisions such as credit or hiring?

Data scientist · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Fairness and responsible AI · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Define the harm and the relevant fairness criterion with stakeholders, such as demographic parity, equalised odds or calibration across groups, measure metrics by subgroup with uncertainty, trace sources of bias in data and labels, and mitigate through data, in-training constraints or post-processing thresholds. Fairness criteria conflict, so document trade-offs, legal constraints and monitoring.

Explained in depth

Sources of unfairness

  • Historical bias: data reflect past discrimination (hiring records favouring one group).
  • Representation and measurement bias: some groups under-sampled, features measured differently (arrest records as a proxy for crime; healthcare spending as a proxy for health need).
  • Label bias: labels come from biased human decisions.
  • Proxy variables: postcode, school or purchasing patterns that correlate with protected attributes.
  • Aggregation and evaluation bias: one model for heterogeneous groups; test sets that do not represent all groups.
  • Feedback loops: decisions change who appears in future data (policing, lending).

Removing the sensitive attribute ("fairness through unawareness") does not work because proxies leak it; you often need the attribute to measure disparities.

Fairness criteria

CriterionRequirementTypical use
Demographic (statistical) parityequal selection rates across groupsoutreach, allocation; legal "four-fifths rule" for adverse impact
Equal opportunityequal true positive ratesqualified candidates get selected equally
Equalised oddsequal TPR and FPRerror parity
Predictive parity / calibration within groupssame precision or same meaning of a score across groupsrisk scores
Individual fairnesssimilar individuals receive similar outcomesneeds a similarity metric
Counterfactual fairnessoutcome unchanged in a counterfactual world with a different protected attributerequires a causal model

When base rates differ between groups, these criteria cannot all hold simultaneously (impossibility results by Kleinberg et al. and Chouldechova). So the choice is a value and legal judgement made with legal, policy and domain stakeholders, tied to the specific harm (wrongful denial versus wrongful approval).

Assessment workflow

  1. Define the decision, affected groups, harms, and applicable law (equal credit and employment laws, GDPR, EU AI Act).
  2. Compute metrics by subgroup: selection rate, TPR, FPR, precision, calibration curves, with confidence intervals and intersectional groups (small groups need care).
  3. Investigate causes: data coverage, label process, features and proxies, threshold effects.
  4. Compare model alternatives and thresholds; examine performance-fairness trade-off curves.

Mitigation

StageTechniques
Pre-processingcollect better data, reweighing, resampling, relabelling, remove or transform proxies, fair representations
In-processingconstrained optimisation (Fairlearn reductions), adversarial debiasing, fairness regularisers
Post-processinggroup-specific thresholds to equalise odds or parity, reject-option classification (may raise legal issues where explicit use of protected attributes is restricted)
Processhuman review for borderline cases, appeal mechanisms, explanations and recourse, documentation (model cards, datasheets), monitoring

Governance

Document intended use and limitations, involve affected communities, version audits, run periodic reviews, monitor subgroup metrics in production, and maintain privacy: when protected attributes are not collected, use legally permitted inference methods (BISG) carefully or conduct audits with voluntary self-reported data.

Pitfalls

Optimising a single metric blindly, ignoring intersectional groups, assuming a fair model today stays fair after drift, and treating fairness as purely technical.

Likely follow-ups

  • Why can't you satisfy demographic parity, equalised odds and calibration at the same time when base rates differ?
  • Does removing the protected attribute from the features make a model fair?
  • How would you audit a deployed model when you do not collect protected attributes?
