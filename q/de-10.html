What is the difference between Additive, Semi-Additive, and Non-Additive fact tables?

Data engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Data Modeling · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Additive facts can be summed across every dimension (sales amount). Semi-additive facts can be summed across some dimensions but not time, such as account balances or inventory levels, where you average or take the last value over time. Non-additive facts, such as ratios and percentages, cannot be summed at all and must be recomputed from additive components.

Explained in depth

Additive

Can be summed over all dimensions: date, product, store, customer. Example: sales_amount, quantity_sold. Total revenue for a year, a region or a product category is a simple SUM.

Semi-additive

Meaningful to sum across some dimensions but not across time. Example: a bank account balance or stock inventory_on_hand.

  • Sum of balances across all accounts on one day: valid (total money in the bank that day).
  • Sum of one account's balance over 30 days: meaningless.
  • Over time use average, minimum, maximum or last value:
-- month-end balance per account
SELECT account_id, balance
FROM fact_daily_balance
WHERE date_key = (SELECT MAX(date_key) FROM fact_daily_balance WHERE month = '2024-06')

These facts usually come from periodic snapshot fact tables.

Non-additive

Cannot be summed over any dimension: ratios, percentages, unit prices, averages, temperatures. Example: margin_pct. Adding 20% and 30% gives nothing useful. Store the components (revenue and cost, or numerator and denominator) in the fact table, then compute the ratio after aggregation:

SELECT SUM(revenue - cost) / NULLIF(SUM(revenue), 0) AS margin_pct FROM fact_sales;

Why it matters

Modelling mistakes lead to wrong dashboards: summing balances across days or averaging pre-computed averages. Document each measure's additivity and use semantic layers or tested metrics to enforce the correct aggregation.

Likely follow-ups

  • Why should you store the numerator and denominator instead of a ratio?
