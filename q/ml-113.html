Build or buy: would you fine-tune an open-weight LLM or use a hosted API for an AI collections outreach assistant?

ML / AI engineer · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · LLMs · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Decide on data protection, required quality, volume and the team's ability to run models. Usually start with a hosted model through an enterprise agreement (no training on your data, regional processing) plus RAG over policies and account data to prove value fast. Move to a self-hosted or fine-tuned open-weight model only when volume, latency, residency rules or a measured quality gap justify the operational cost.

Explained in depth

Evaluate on four axes

AxisHosted APISelf-hosted open-weight
Data protectionContract terms: zero retention, no training, regional processingData never leaves your cloud account
Quality out of the boxUsually the strongest general modelsNeeds evaluation; fine-tuning closes gaps on narrow tasks
CostPay per token, no fixed cost; grows with volumeGPUs, serving stack and on-call; cheap per token at high, steady volume
Speed to shipDaysWeeks to months

What the assistant actually needs

  • Knowledge that changes (credit terms, payment plan rules, the account's invoices and history) belongs in retrieval or tool calls, not in model weights. Fine-tuning does not keep facts current.
  • Tone and format (polite but firm, the house template, structured JSON for the CRM) can come from prompts and examples first. Fine-tune only if a measured gap remains.
  • Amounts, dates and payment-plan maths come from systems of record, never generated by the model.

Total cost of ownership

  • Estimate tokens per draft x drafts per month x price, and compare with GPU hours, engineering time, monitoring, security reviews and model upgrades for self-hosting. Low or spiky volume nearly always favours an API.
  • Include the hidden costs on both sides: vendor lock-in and price changes for the API; keeping up with new model releases for self-hosting.

Recommendation

  1. Prototype with a hosted model under an enterprise agreement, RAG over policy documents and tool access to billing data, with a human approving every message.
  2. Build an evaluation set (policy accuracy, tone ratings, no invented amounts, compliance with collection rules) and log real usage.
  3. Revisit at a volume or regulatory trigger: a small open-weight model, possibly fine-tuned on approved messages, may then be cheaper and good enough. Keep the interface model-agnostic so switching is a configuration change.

Collection messages are regulated in many jurisdictions (harassment, disclosure and contact-time rules), so the guardrails and human review matter more than which model you pick.

Likely follow-ups

  • What evaluation would convince you a fine-tuned small model is good enough?
  • How do you keep the assistant from inventing amounts or payment terms?
  • What contract terms would you require from an LLM vendor?
