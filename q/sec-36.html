Compare Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) mechanisms: SMS versus TOTP apps versus FIDO2/WebAuthn hardware keys.

Security engineer · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · IAM · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

SMS codes are the weakest second factor: they can be intercepted, SIM-swapped or phished. TOTP apps generate time-based codes locally and are much stronger, but codes can still be phished in real time. FIDO2 and WebAuthn security keys or passkeys use public-key cryptography bound to the website's origin, so they resist phishing and are the strongest option.

Why WebAuthn is stronger

During registration the authenticator creates a key pair per site; the server stores only the public key. During login, the browser passes the site's origin to the authenticator, which signs the server's challenge. A fake site at another origin cannot obtain a valid signature, and a database leak reveals no reusable secret.

Guidance

  • Prefer passkeys or security keys for administrators and high-risk accounts, and offer them to all users.
  • Use TOTP as a fallback; avoid SMS except as a last resort (NIST treats it as restricted).
  • Beware of MFA fatigue (push approval spam); use number matching.
  • Design recovery carefully: several registered authenticators, one-time backup codes stored safely, and a verified helpdesk process, since a weak recovery path undermines strong MFA.
