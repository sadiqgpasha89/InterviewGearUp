What is the difference between batch and stream processing, and how do you choose?

Data engineer · Gear 1 (Junior) · Fundamentals · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Batch processing handles large bounded datasets on a schedule, trading latency for throughput and simplicity. Stream processing handles unbounded events continuously with seconds or less latency, at the cost of more complex state, ordering and failure handling. Choose by how fresh the result must be: most analytics are fine with batch, while fraud detection or live monitoring needs streaming.

Explained in depth

Batch

  • Processes a bounded dataset (yesterday's orders) at scheduled intervals: hourly, daily.
  • Examples: nightly ETL into a warehouse, monthly billing, model training.
  • Tools: Spark, dbt, SQL in a warehouse, Hadoop, Airflow for scheduling.
  • Pros: simple to build and reason about, easy to re-run and test, high throughput and efficient use of resources, results are consistent.
  • Cons: latency is minutes to hours or more; a failure delays the whole result.

Stream

  • Processes an unbounded flow of events as they arrive, with results in milliseconds to seconds.
  • Examples: fraud detection, real-time dashboards, alerting, personalisation, IoT telemetry.
  • Tools: Kafka with Flink, Kafka Streams, Spark Structured Streaming, Kinesis.
  • Pros: fresh data, immediate reactions.
  • Cons: complexity: windowing, state, out-of-order and late events (watermarks), exactly-once processing, backpressure, always-on operations and harder debugging and testing.

Choosing

Ask: what is the business cost of delay? If a daily refresh is enough, use batch. If minutes matter, consider frequent micro-batches. If decisions need seconds, use streaming. Remember cost: streaming systems run 24 by 7.

Blends

  • Micro-batch (Spark Structured Streaming) processes small batches every few seconds, giving near real-time with batch semantics.
  • Lambda architecture keeps both a batch and a speed layer; Kappa uses a single streaming system and replays the log for reprocessing.
  • Modern lakehouse tables (Delta, Iceberg, Hudi) support streaming writes and batch reads of the same data.
