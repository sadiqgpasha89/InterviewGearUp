How do you define the minimum viable product (MVP) scope when stakeholder requirements are vague?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Delivery · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

When requirements are vague, define the problem and user outcome first, then choose the smallest slice that tests the riskiest assumption. Talk to users and stakeholders, write down goals and non-goals, prioritise with a framework such as MoSCoW, ship a thin end-to-end version, measure, and iterate. An MVP is a learning vehicle, not a bad-quality product.

Explained in depth

Steps

  1. Clarify the problem and outcome: who is the user, what job are they trying to do, what does success look like (a metric such as activation or time saved)? Ask "what would make this a success in three months?"
  2. List assumptions and risks: value (will anyone use it?), usability, feasibility, business viability. Identify the riskiest assumption, the one that would sink the idea if wrong.
  3. Define goals and explicit non-goals and write them down so stakeholders can agree.
  4. Slice vertically: find the smallest end-to-end flow that delivers the core value (for example sign up, create one item, share it), rather than building layers or many half-finished features.
  5. Prioritise with MoSCoW (must, should, could, won't), impact versus effort, or Kano. Push back with data and user stories.
  6. Choose cheap ways to learn: a clickable prototype, a concierge or manual version behind the scenes, a landing page test or a limited beta.
  7. Set success criteria and a review date, instrument the product, and decide what you will do with the results.

Working with ambiguity

Timebox discovery, agree on a small number of open questions, and design the system so likely changes are cheap (modular code, feature flags). Re-confirm scope in short demos instead of long specifications.

Handling stakeholders

Make trade-offs visible: "To add X we must remove Y or move the date." Share what you are not building and why, and keep a visible backlog for later.

Pitfalls

An MVP that is a pile of features, building before validating demand, ignoring quality basics (reliability and security), or never iterating after launch.
