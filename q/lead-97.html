How do you champion web accessibility (a11y) and localization (i18n) as non-negotiable standards?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Strategy · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Make accessibility and localisation non-negotiable by treating them as quality requirements in the definition of done, not late features. Set standards such as WCAG 2.2 AA, build them into the design system, CI checks and release gates, train people, include real user testing, design for i18n from the start with externalised strings and locale-aware formatting, and track measurable outcomes.

Explained in depth

Make the case

  • Accessibility (a11y) serves roughly 15 to 20 percent of people with disabilities, plus temporary and situational limitations (injury, bright sun, noisy rooms). It is a legal requirement in many markets (ADA and Section 508 in the US, European Accessibility Act applicable from June 2025, EN 301 549, AODA, Equality Act), and lawsuits are frequent. It improves SEO, usability for everyone, and brand trust.
  • Localisation (l10n) and internationalisation (i18n) expand market reach, improve conversion in local languages, and meet regulations (local language, currency, date formats, data residency). Retrofitting costs far more than building in from the start.

Frame both as product quality and risk management, with data: percentage of traffic from non-English locales, accessibility complaint volume, lost deals requiring VPAT or accessibility conformance reports, and cost of remediation.

Policy and standards

  1. Adopt explicit standards: WCAG 2.2 Level AA for accessibility (perceivable, operable, understandable, robust), and a documented i18n and l10n standard (supported locales, languages, right-to-left support, character sets, formats). Include them in the engineering handbook, definition of done and acceptance criteria ("No story is done unless keyboard accessible and strings externalised").
  2. Accountability: name owners (an accessibility lead, localisation manager), a champions network, and an executive sponsor. Include goals in OKRs and performance expectations.

Accessibility in the workflow

  • Design: inclusive design principles, colour contrast checks, focus states, touch target sizes, text resizing, plain language, and annotated specs (landmarks, headings, labels, alt text, keyboard behaviour) in design handoffs. Use an accessible design system with components built and tested for a11y once (buttons, forms, modals, menus follow ARIA Authoring Practices), so product teams inherit compliance.
  • Development: semantic HTML first, ARIA only when needed, focus management, keyboard support, reduced motion preferences, screen reader labels, accessible forms and errors, captions and transcripts for media, and mobile accessibility APIs (VoiceOver, TalkBack). Provide linters (eslint-plugin-jsx-a11y), component libraries (Radix, React Aria, Reach), and code review checklists.
  • Testing: automated checks in CI (axe-core, Lighthouse CI, pa11y, Storybook a11y addon, Playwright with axe) catch only about 30 to 40 percent of issues, so add manual testing: keyboard-only navigation, screen reader testing (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver), zoom and high contrast, and usability testing with people with disabilities. Audit major releases by third-party experts, and publish an accessibility statement and VPAT/ACR.
  • Release gating and monitoring: block regressions for critical flows, track issues in the backlog with severity and SLAs, run periodic audits, and provide a feedback channel for users.

i18n and l10n in the architecture

  • Externalise all user-facing strings into resource files with keys (ICU MessageFormat, gettext, i18next, FormatJS), never concatenate sentences, support pluralisation, gender and grammar rules, and avoid text in images.
  • Use locale-aware formatting for dates, times, time zones, numbers, currency, units, sorting, and addresses via Intl, CLDR, or libraries; store timestamps in UTC; avoid assumptions about names (single "full name" field), phone numbers, or postal codes.
  • Unicode (UTF-8) everywhere, including databases and APIs; handle right-to-left layouts with logical CSS properties, and design flexible layouts for text expansion (German and Finnish can be 30 to 40 percent longer), different fonts and line heights (CJK).
  • Translation workflow: a translation management system (Crowdin, Lokalise, Phrase, Transifex) integrated with Git and CI, context for translators (screenshots, comments), glossaries and style guides, pseudo-localisation tests to find hard-coded strings and layout breaks, machine translation with human review, and linguistic QA.
  • Content and legal: localised legal terms, payments, support, SEO (hreflang), cultural adaptation of images and examples.

Making it non-negotiable

Include a11y and i18n in design system governance, architecture reviews and security-like gates, offer training (workshops, lunch and learns), celebrate improvements, and set measurable targets (percentage of components passing audits, number of critical issues open, share of strings localised, time to launch a new locale). Allocate a recurring portion of capacity to remediation of legacy issues, prioritised by user impact.

Pitfalls

Treating a11y as a final audit, relying only on automated tools or overlay widgets (they do not provide compliance), hard-coded strings, text embedded in images, ignoring RTL until late, and no ownership or budget.
