Two senior engineers on your team strongly disagree on a technical direction. How do you resolve it?

Software engineer (core) · Gear 4 (Lead / Staff) · Behavioral · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Surface the real disagreement by restating each position and the criteria that matter, move the discussion from opinions to evidence with a written comparison or small experiment, agree on decision rules and a decider beforehand, then decide, document it and commit together. Preserve the relationship, and revisit only if new data appears.

Explained in depth

Step 1: understand, privately and then together

  • Meet each person to hear their position, their underlying concerns (risk, cost, time, maintainability) and what would change their mind. Often the disagreement is about different assumptions or priorities, not the technology.
  • Restate each view in the other person's terms until both say "yes, that is my position".

Step 2: agree on how to decide

  • Define the decision criteria and their weight: time to market, operational complexity, performance, team skills, cost, reversibility. Agree who the decider is (tech lead, architect, or consensus with a time limit) before debating the content.
  • Ask whether the decision is a one-way door (hard to reverse) or a two-way door (easy to reverse). Two-way doors deserve fast decisions and bias toward action.

Step 3: replace opinion with evidence

  • Ask both to write a short design doc or ADR with options and trade-offs.
  • Run a time-boxed spike or prototype, or benchmark the key risk (for example latency under realistic load).
  • Invite a neutral third perspective or review from another team.

Step 4: decide and commit

  • The decider chooses, explains the reasoning and acknowledges the merits of the alternative. Record it in an ADR, including what would trigger a revisit.
  • Disagree and commit: everyone supports the outcome. Make sure the person whose view lost still has influence, perhaps by owning a risk mitigation.

Watch for

Personal friction (talk privately and address behaviour), seniority-driven outcomes, endless debate (set a deadline), and re-litigating settled decisions.

Likely follow-ups

  • What if the engineers still disagree after the decision?
  • When would you make the call yourself versus let the team decide?
  • How do you avoid decisions being driven by seniority or loudest voice?
