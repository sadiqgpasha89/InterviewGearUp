Compare LLM quantization approaches: Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) versus Quantization-Aware Training (QAT).

ML / AI engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · LLMs · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Post-training quantisation (PTQ) converts an already trained model's weights, and sometimes activations, to lower precision using a small calibration set and no retraining, which is quick and cheap but can lose accuracy at very low bit widths. Quantisation-aware training (QAT) simulates quantisation during training so the model adapts to the rounding error, giving better accuracy at the cost of extra training.

Explained in depth

Why quantise

Lower precision (INT8 or INT4 instead of FP16) shrinks memory and bandwidth, which often limits LLM inference speed, and can speed up compute on supported hardware.

Post-training quantisation

  • Take trained weights and map them to integers with a scale (and zero point), per tensor, per channel or per group.
  • Calibration data estimates activation ranges if activations are quantised too.
  • Weight-only methods (GPTQ, AWQ, bitsandbytes NF4) keep activations in 16-bit and quantise only weights, giving large memory savings with small loss at 4 bits.
  • GPTQ quantises weights layer by layer, using second-order information to compensate for the rounding error.
  • AWQ protects the small share of weights tied to large activations by scaling them first.
  • Weight-and-activation schemes (such as SmoothQuant for INT8) also quantise activations; outliers in activations make this hard, so scaling is moved from activations into weights.
  • Pros: no training, minutes to hours, a few hundred calibration samples.
  • Cons: accuracy can drop noticeably below 4 bits or on small models.

Quantisation-aware training

Insert fake quantisation operations in the forward pass (quantise then dequantise) while keeping full-precision master weights, and use the straight-through estimator to pass gradients through the rounding. The model learns weights that survive quantisation.

  • Pros: best accuracy at low bit widths.
  • Cons: needs the training pipeline, data and compute, which is expensive for large LLMs (though LoRA-style QAT variants exist).

Choosing

Try PTQ first (8-bit or 4-bit weight-only). Use QAT when PTQ loses too much accuracy and you can afford the training. Always evaluate on your task, not only perplexity.
