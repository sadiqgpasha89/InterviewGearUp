How do you establish sustainable working norms around core hours, response times, and Slack etiquette?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 2 (Mid-level) · Culture · Difficulty: easy · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Set sustainable working norms by writing them down with the team: core hours, expected response times per channel, meeting-free focus time, notification etiquette, a clear urgent path, and respect for time off and time zones. Leaders model the behaviour, and the team reviews the norms regularly so they stay realistic and fair.

Explained in depth

Why write norms down

Unwritten expectations lead to always-on pressure, constant interruptions, uneven availability and resentment. Teams without agreed norms drift into "reply immediately to everything", which destroys deep work and well-being. A team working agreement gives clarity, fairness and permission to disconnect, and it is a living document that the team owns.

Components of a working agreement

  1. Core hours and availability: define a window (for example 10 am to 3 pm local or a few hours of overlap across time zones) when people are generally reachable for meetings and collaboration; outside core hours, people choose flexible schedules. Show working hours in calendars and chat profiles, and respect them. Plan meetings within core hours and rotate times fairly across regions.
  2. Response-time expectations by channel: for example:
ChannelTypical expectation
Urgent page or call (incidents)minutes, via on-call tool only
Direct message or mentionwithin a few working hours, same day
Team channel threadwithin one working day
Emailone to two working days
Pull request reviewfirst review within one working day
Documents and RFC commentswithin the stated review window

Make explicit that no one is expected to monitor chat constantly, and that urgent matters use the defined urgent path (page or phone), not Slack DMs at night.

  1. Notification and Slack etiquette:
  • Prefer public channels and threads over DMs for work questions, so knowledge is shared.
  • Write complete messages with context, ask, and deadline (no "hi" alone); use threads to keep channels readable; use @here and @channel rarely; set clear channel purposes.
  • Use scheduled send for off-hours messages, and statuses (focus, vacation); do not expect replies outside working hours.
  • Emojis for acknowledgement ("seen", "done") to reduce noise; avoid long debates in chat (move to a call or doc); use tickets and documents for decisions.
  • Be mindful of tone in text, and assume positive intent.
  1. Meetings and focus time: meeting-free blocks (for example afternoons or one day per week), agendas and notes required, camera optional, default to 25 or 50 minutes, and recording or async alternatives. Limit recurring meetings, and regularly prune.
  2. Time off and boundaries: vacation is real: no work messages, an out-of-office with a backup contact, handover documents, and managers visibly take time off and avoid pinging people on leave. Respect public holidays in all locations. Discourage after-hours work as a norm, and avoid rewarding "heroic" availability.
  3. Escalation path: define how to reach someone in a real emergency (on-call rota, phone tree) and what counts as an emergency, so the rest can remain quiet.
  4. Collaboration practices: pairing windows, review rotations, and a "ask for help early" expectation, plus flexibility for personal commitments (school runs, appointments) as long as outcomes and commitments are met and communicated.

Creating and maintaining the agreement

  • Facilitate a team workshop: ask what frustrates people about communication and meetings, what they need to do focused work, and what is feasible given the business. Draft proposals, vote, and trial for a month, then review in a retrospective. Include people with different roles, locations and life circumstances (caregivers, different time zones, neurodivergent colleagues).
  • Publish and onboard: put it in the team handbook, pin it in the channel, and discuss with new joiners.
  • Leaders model it: avoid late-night messages unless urgent (use scheduled send), respond within the agreed times, take vacations, and protect the norms when stakeholders push. Address violations privately and constructively; look for systemic causes (understaffing, unclear priorities) rather than blaming individuals.
  • Review quarterly and adapt for projects such as launches or incidents, with temporary agreed exceptions (and recovery afterwards).

Measure

Team survey on workload and boundaries, after-hours activity in tools (aggregate only), meeting hours and focus time, response times, burnout indicators, and retention.

Pitfalls

A policy imposed top-down, norms leaders ignore, excessive rules, no urgent path, treating flexibility as availability at all times, and failing to adjust for time zones.

Likely follow-ups

  • What response-time expectations are reasonable for chat, email and urgent pages?
  • How do you handle people who ignore the norms or leaders who message at all hours?
  • How do you adapt the norms for teams across time zones or with caregiving needs?
