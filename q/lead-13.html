How do you foster psychological safety so engineers feel comfortable taking technical risks?

Tech lead / Engineering manager · Gear 3 (Senior) · Culture · Difficulty: medium · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Psychological safety is the shared belief that you will not be punished or humiliated for asking questions, admitting mistakes or disagreeing. Build it by modelling fallibility, thanking people who raise problems, responding to bad news calmly, running blameless reviews and inviting quieter voices, while keeping high standards.

Explained in depth

What it is and is not

Amy Edmondson's definition: a belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking. It is not being nice all the time or lowering standards. High safety plus high standards produces learning; safety without standards produces comfort, and standards without safety produce fear and hidden problems. Google's Project Aristotle found it to be the strongest factor in effective teams.

What leaders can do

  • Model fallibility: say 'I got this wrong' and 'I do not know', and share what you learned.
  • Ask for dissent: 'What could go wrong with this plan?' and 'Who sees it differently?' Reward people who raise problems early, and thank them.
  • React to bad news calmly. How you respond the first time someone admits a mistake sets the norm for everyone.
  • Blameless postmortems that look at the system, not individuals.
  • Include quieter people: collect written input before a discussion, go around the room, and credit ideas to their authors.
  • Make risk-taking small and cheap: experiments, feature flags, easy rollbacks, and a celebrated 'what we learned' after failures.
  • Handle disrespect and interrupting promptly and consistently.

Signs it is missing

Silent design reviews, problems surfacing late, people agreeing in the meeting and disagreeing afterwards, few questions from newer engineers, and hidden incidents.

Checking

Use short anonymous pulse questions (such as 'if I make a mistake, it is not held against me' or 'it is safe to take a risk on this team'), watch the retros, and ask directly in 1-on-1s. Act visibly on what you hear.

Likely follow-ups

  • How do you create safety on a team where one senior person dominates discussion?
  • How do you keep standards high without damaging safety?
  • What would you do the first time someone admits a serious mistake to you?
