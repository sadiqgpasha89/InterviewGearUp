How do you establish and manage a Bug Bounty program and Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure (CVD) policy?

Security engineer · Gear 3 (Senior) · Vulnerability · Difficulty: hard · AI-drafted, not yet reviewed by a person

In 30 seconds

Run bug bounty and CVD programs by first publishing a clear vulnerability disclosure policy (scope, safe harbour, contact, response SLAs), starting with a private invite-only program or VDP, using a platform or in-house triage, defining reward tables and severity rules, and building internal workflows for validation, fixing, communication and metrics, with legal and engineering alignment.

Explained in depth

Concepts

  • Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure (CVD): researchers report privately, the vendor investigates and fixes, and details are published after a fix or agreed deadline (ISO/IEC 29147 and 30111, CERT/CC guidance; Google Project Zero uses 90 days).
  • Vulnerability Disclosure Program (VDP): a public policy and channel for reports, usually without monetary rewards (recognition, hall of fame).
  • Bug bounty program: pays researchers for valid vulnerabilities, public or private (invite-only), often through platforms (HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Intigriti, YesWeHack) or self-run.

Build it step by step

  1. Foundations and readiness: have internal vulnerability management, patching capability and an owner; run internal and third-party testing first so researchers are not finding trivial issues; secure leadership and legal support; budget for rewards and staffing.
  2. Define scope:
  • In scope: specific domains, apps, APIs, mobile apps, cloud assets, versions; targets with highest value first.
  • Out of scope: third-party services, physical attacks, social engineering of employees, denial of service, spam, automated scanning that causes load, issues without security impact (missing headers with no exploit, self-XSS, clickjacking on static pages).
  • Vulnerability types and severity guidance (CVSS or VRT, with environment-specific adjustments).
  1. Write the policy: purpose; how to report (email with PGP key, web form, platform); what a good report contains (steps, proof of concept, impact); safe harbour statement (no legal action for good-faith research that follows the rules; align with DOJ CFAA policy and local laws); rules (do not access other users' data beyond need, no data exfiltration, no persistence, stop and report on discovering sensitive data); response commitments (acknowledge within 1 to 3 business days, triage in 5 to 10 days, fix timelines by severity, regular updates); disclosure rules (do not publish before fix or agreed date; coordinated disclosure timeline, such as 90 days); recognition and reward policy; eligibility and sanctions restrictions.
  2. Publish security.txt (RFC 9116) at /.well-known/security.txt with Contact, Expires, Encryption, Policy, Preferred-Languages, and CVD policy URL.
  3. Choose the model: start with a VDP, then a private bounty with a small group of vetted researchers (limited volume, learn process), then a public bounty when the team can handle volume. Platforms provide triage services, researcher reputation, payment handling, duplicate handling and legal terms; self-hosted gives control but needs triage staffing.
  4. Rewards: tie to severity and impact: for example low 100 to 500, medium 500 to 2,000, high 2,000 to 10,000, critical 10,000 and above, bonuses for quality reports and chaining; publish a table; pay quickly; consider market rates and asset criticality; document how duplicates and partial discoveries are rewarded (first valid report wins).
  5. Operational workflow:
  • Intake and triage (security team or platform triage): reproduce, deduplicate, classify, assign severity, request clarification, mark invalid or out-of-scope with respectful explanation.
  • Routing: create tickets in the engineering backlog with SLA by severity (critical 24 to 72 hours mitigations, 7 to 14 days fix), owners, regression tests.
  • Remediation and verification: fix, retest by the researcher or internal team, deploy, update status.
  • Communication: timely updates, credit, coordinate public disclosure, advisories and CVE assignment (as a CNA or via MITRE or vendor CNA), customer notifications where users are affected.
  • Root cause and learning: feed into secure coding training, SAST rules, architecture reviews, and add detections for exploitation attempts.
  1. People and process: dedicated program manager, rotation of triagers, legal and PR contacts, escalation for critical reports (incident process), handling of reports that reveal active exploitation or sensitive data.
  2. Metrics: time to first response, time to triage, time to fix, number of valid reports by severity, duplicate and invalid rates, researcher satisfaction, cost per valid vulnerability, recurring vulnerability classes, percentage of findings that internal tools missed, reopened issues.

Risks and mitigations

  • Flood of low-quality reports: clear scope, triage service, automated pre-filtering, rate of submission limits.
  • Production impact from testing: rate limits and rules, dedicated test accounts and environments, monitoring and the ability to block abusive sources.
  • Legal and regulatory exposure: counsel review, safe harbour language, data protection handling of any personal data researchers access.
  • Public disclosure disputes: agree timelines up front, extend by mutual consent for complex fixes, respect researcher recognition.
  • Budget surprises: cap per period, start private, forecast based on pilot results.

Pitfalls

Launching a bounty with a backlog of known unpatched issues, slow responses that burn researcher goodwill, unclear scope causing disputes, rewarding inconsistently, treating researchers as adversaries, and no mechanism to fix issues at the pace they arrive.

Likely follow-ups

  • What belongs in a vulnerability disclosure policy and security.txt file?
  • How do you handle duplicate, out-of-scope and low-quality reports without discouraging researchers?
  • How do you decide when to move from a VDP to a paid bounty and how do you set reward amounts?
